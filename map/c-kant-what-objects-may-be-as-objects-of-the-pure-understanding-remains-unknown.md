The second sentence of the passage (AA 3:213.29–32; A 258 / B 314), asserted outright. Its
subject, »dieses«, is resolved by name in the statement from the last clause of the sentence
before it: what objects may be »außer der Beziehung auf mögliche Erfahrung und folglich auf
Sinne überhaupt, mithin als Gegenstände des reinen Verstandes«, the resolution the map's, open to
dispute; the other neuter candidate in the sentence before, »das letztere«, the saying that the
understanding represents objects as they are, comes in its transcendental sense to the same
referent; the opening »Denn« is dropped from the
statement and kept as the link below. »so gar, daß« is "so much so that"; »überall möglich sei«
"is possible at all", Kant's »überall« as at B 311; »außerordentliche« "extraordinary", kept in
its parentheses; »zum wenigsten« "at least"; »eine solche ... Erkenntniß« "such a cognition", a
cognition of objects as objects of the pure understanding, which the sentence calls transcendental.
The sentence says what remains unknown and what the understanding's »wie sie sind« therefore
cannot mean; it says »Gegenstände des reinen Verstandes« and not »Ding an sich« or »Noumenon«, and
that these objects are what the chapter calls noumena, things thought as given to the understanding
alone and not to the senses (the chapter's paragraph before this one, 3:213.15–17, in no passage;
B 307 and B 310 in the map), is the map's reading, open to dispute, as it is for the last
paragraph's »bloß intelligibeler Gegenstände«.

The sentence is the ground, by its »Denn« (3:213.29), of the sentence before it, which the map
holds as a claim that `depends_on` this one

that the understanding's representing objects as they are is to be taken in an empirical sense.
