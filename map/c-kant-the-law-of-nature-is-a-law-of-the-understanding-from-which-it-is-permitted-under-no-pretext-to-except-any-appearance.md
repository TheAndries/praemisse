The one sentence of the passage (AA 3:368.22–34), asserted in the indicative, »ist ein Verstandesgesetz, von welchem es unter keinem Vorwande erlaubt ist abzugehen oder irgend eine Erscheinung davon auszunehmen«; the statement is the sentence whole, and the quotation below is the sentence, since it is the passage. What the sentence asserts is said of a law whose content it states in three »daß« clauses, that everything that happens has a cause, that the causality of this cause has its cause among the appearances, and that consequently all events are empirically determined in an order of nature; the clauses stand in the Konjunktiv of a content clause, »habe«, and in the indicative, »bestimmt sind«, and the English marks neither; »folglich« inside the third draws it from the first two inside the law's statement, a step the map does not formalize, and »da sie in der Zeit vorhergeht ...« inside the second gives the ground of the second, that a causality which precedes in time and in regard to an effect that has arisen cannot itself always have been but must have happened. The main clause asserts of this law, which its relative clause on the subject »dieses Gesetz« describes as the law through which appearances first constitute a nature and can yield objects of an experience, two things, that it is a law of the understanding and that it is permitted under no pretext to depart from it or to except any appearance from it; the »weil« clause, »weil man sie sonst außerhalb aller möglichen Erfahrung setzen ... würde«, grounds the second and has »würde« as its verb, what excepting an appearance would do, and is kept inside the claim as the sentence's own ground, not made a claim of its own and not formalized, since the map formalizes no argument from it; a reader who wants the step checked may ask for it to be made an argument. The pronouns, the qualifiers and the renderings are on the passage; »unter keinem Vorwande« is kept as "under no pretext", »irgend eine« as "any", »allererst« as "first", »bloßen« as "mere".
