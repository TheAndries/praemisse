The first two sentences of § 2, joined in the statement by an added "and" and with the second sentence's colon turned into a comma; the text's »sonach« ("accordingly") and its »denn« clause, which gives the ground, are kept inside the statement, and the step from that ground to the main clause is not formalized as an argument. The quotation carries zeno.org's »daist«, the transcription's letters, where Grisebach has »da ist«, named on the passage. The sentence after these two has everyone find himself as this subject »jedoch nur sofern er erkennt, nicht sofern er Objekt der Erkenntniß ist«; it and the paragraph's five remaining sentences are in no claim, and the passage's page names them. Set in the row of [x-thing-in-itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) on what in the world of appearance is the subject's own, beside § 1's sentence that the whole world is only object in relation to the subject ([c-schopenhauer-world-is-my-representation](../../c/schopenhauer-world-is-my-representation/)), as the sentence that makes the subject the condition of every object; the two sentences are joined by no connective in the text, and the map draws no link between them; the nearest sentence in the map to the »denn« clause is § 1's closing »und ist nur für das Subjekt da« ([p-wwv1-s1-world-is-my-representation](../../p/wwv1-s1-world-is-my-representation/)), and no connective joins them either. No attack link is set from JGB 17 ([c-nietzsche-subject-as-condition-of-thinking-is-falsification](../../c/nietzsche-subject-as-condition-of-thinking-is-falsification/)): that statement calls it a falsification to say that the subject »ich« is the condition of the predicate »denke«, this one makes the subject the condition of every object, and the words that statement denies are not this statement's; a reader may dispute the absence. No link is set to Kant's claim that space is the subjective condition of sensibility ([c-kant-space-subjective-condition-of-sensibility](../../c/kant-space-subjective-condition-of-sensibility/)): that statement is about the form space, this one about the subject as the condition of every object, and neither asserts the other's proposition.
