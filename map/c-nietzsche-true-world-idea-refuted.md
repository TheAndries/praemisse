GD, »Wie die ›wahre Welt‹ endlich zur Fabel wurde«, stage 5: »Die "wahre Welt" - eine
Idee, die zu Nichts mehr nütz ist, nicht einmal mehr verpflichtend, - eine unnütz, eine
überflüssig gewordene Idee, folglich eine widerlegte Idee: schaffen wir sie ab!« The
claim depends on [c-nietzsche-true-world-not-obligating](../../c/nietzsche-true-world-not-obligating/) by the stage's own
connective: »nicht einmal mehr verpflichtend« takes stage 4's result as the last use the
idea had, and »folglich« draws the refutation. The map has not formalized the step as an
argument, because the passage from "of no use" to "refuted" has no stated premise; a
reader who wants it checked may ask for it to be made an argument. The next stage records
the abolition, [c-nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent](../../c/nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent/),
without a step the map could set a dependency on.

Whose voice. The map reads stage 5 as Nietzsche's own, following the owner's reading of
2026-10-10 (`CHANGELOG.md`): the stage's parenthesis, »Teufelslärm aller freien Geister«,
names the free spirits (that Nietzsche writes of himself and his kind under that name in
other texts is not in the map), and the next stage,
[c-nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent](../../c/nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent/),
ends in »INCIPIT ZARATHUSTRA«; the chapter has no sentence that separates the two. On the
reading cited on the argument node (Clark 1990, ch. 3), on which the stages are positions
Nietzsche himself passed through, stage 5 is his as well. Stage 4 keeps its framing as
the positivist stage, whose parenthesis names a position, »Positivismus«, as stages 1
and 3 name Plato and Königsberg; the reason is on
[a-nietzsche-true-world-does-not-obligate](../../a/nietzsche-true-world-does-not-obligate/).
This stage takes that stage's result, »nicht einmal mehr verpflichtend«, as the last use
the idea had and draws its own conclusion from it. The reading may be disputed like any
other.
