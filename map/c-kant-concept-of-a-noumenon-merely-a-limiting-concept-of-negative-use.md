The fifth and sixth sentences of the passage (AA 3:211.34–212.02; A 255 / B 311), the close of
the paragraph, both asserted outright and joined into one statement by the semicolon, the
sixth's »aber gleichwohl« kept as "nevertheless". »Grenzbegriff« is "limiting concept", *Grenze*
being "limit" throughout the map; »Anmaßung« "pretension", as at B xxx, where speculative reason
is deprived »ihre Anmaßung überschwenglicher Einsichten«; »erdichtet« "invented"; »derselben«,
the range »der Sinnlichkeit«, resolved by name in the statement and left as "its" in the quote.
The sentence's two »also« are rendered "thus" in the quote; the first, which opens the sentence
(3:211.35), is dropped from the statement, so that it reads alone, and the step it marks is set
as a link: the claim `depends_on` the sentence before it, that the possibility of such noumena
is not to be seen and the range outside appearances is for us empty
([c-kant-possibility-of-noumena-not-to-be-seen-range-outside-appearances-empty-for-us](../../c/kant-possibility-of-noumena-not-to-be-seen-range-outside-appearances-empty-for-us/)),
by the connective »also« at 3:211.35, read by the map as drawing from that sentence and not
from the paragraph as a whole, which a reader who wants the step formalized as an argument may
ask for, and may dispute. The second »also«, inside the sentence, draws »nur von negativem
Gebrauche« from »Grenzbegriff« and is kept as "so".

The sentence names the use of the concept negative, as B 308–309 says what we call noumenon
»muß als ein solches nur in negativer Bedeutung verstanden werden«
([c-kant-noumenon-negative-only](../../c/kant-noumenon-negative-only/)), »Gebrauch« there
»Bedeutung«; the two sentences are set in the same row and the map draws no link between them,
no connective joining the two places. No premise is shared and no attack is set: Schopenhauer's § 1, that a reality that would be neither representation nor will but an object in itself is »ein erträumtes Unding« ([c-schopenhauer-no-object-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-no-object-in-itself/)), is the nearest Schopenhauer statement to »nicht willkürlich erdichtet«, and it calls an object in itself an absurdity, not the concept of a noumenon an invention, so no attack is set; his sentences on what Kant's distinction is and whence it comes (ZA II 513, 534–535) say nothing of a limiting concept; JGB 21's sentence that we alone invented causes and read the sign-world »als "an sich"« into things ([c-nietzsche-projecting-sign-world-as-in-itself-is-mythology](../../c/nietzsche-projecting-sign-world-as-in-itself-is-mythology/)) shares the verb of inventing and names causes, not this concept, so no link is set; Nietzsche's Fabel, stage 5, that the "true" world is »eine unnütz, eine
überflüssig gewordene Idee, folglich eine widerlegte Idee«, judges an idea refuted and
superfluous where this sentence says a concept is not arbitrarily invented, and whether the
idea of the fable's fifth stage is the concept of this sentence no passage in the map says; the
denial, if it is one, needs that identification, which the map does not make. Each absence is
open to dispute.

Set on the comparison on the thing in itself, in the row on what can be said of the thing in
itself, since 2026-10-30, for what the sentences say the concept of a noumenon is and is not.
