The argument is two sentences at B xxvi–xxvii: it is "always reserved that we must at
least be able to think, even if we cannot cognize, these same objects also as things in
themselves. For otherwise the absurd proposition would follow that there is appearance
without anything that appears" (AA 3:17.02–07). The second sentence is the conditional
(premise 2); that its consequent is absurd gives premise 1; the conclusion follows by
modus tollens. The context, that we cognize objects only as appearances (AA 3:16.34–36),
is what "these same objects" refers to and is not a premise of the step.

What is unstated is why denying the reservation would leave appearance without anything
that appears; the text asserts the conditional and does not argue it. Prolegomena §32
states a stronger form of the inference: by admitting appearances the understanding admits
the existence of things in themselves, and the representation of such beings is
unavoidable (AA 4:315.02–06). The form's conclusion is the "must be able to think"; the
concessive "even if we cannot cognize" (AA 3:17.04) is carried by the separate claim that
we cognize objects only as appearances.
