The first sentence of the aphorism, whole, with its opening clause, »Auf welchen Standpunkt
der Philosophie man sich heute auch stellen mag«, kept. What it calls the surest and firmest
thing is »die Irrthümlichkeit der Welt, in der wir zu leben glauben«, not of a world as it
is. Its second half reports a
temptation: the grounds for that erroneousness »verlocken möchten« to conjectures about a
deceptive principle in the »"Wesen der Dinge"«, the term in Nietzsche's quotation marks,
and the sentence asserts no such principle and no conjecture; the statement keeps the
subjunctive as "would tempt". The comparison [x-thing-in-itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) sets the sentence,
since 2026-10-21, in its row on the division of the world and what follows from refusing it,
as what the aphorism says of the world before it comes to the opposition of »"wahr"« and
»"falsch"« ([c-nietzsche-degrees-of-apparentness-instead-of-true-and-false](../../c/nietzsche-degrees-of-apparentness-instead-of-true-and-false/)); no
connective joins the two sentences, which stand in different passages, and no `depends_on`
is set. No link is set to »Die "scheinbare" Welt ist die einzige« of GD
([c-nietzsche-apparent-world-is-the-only-one](../../c/nietzsche-apparent-world-is-the-only-one/)): this sentence says the world we believe
we live in is erroneous, and that one that the apparent world is the only one, two
propositions. No attack link is set: no Kant or Schopenhauer statement in the map says that
