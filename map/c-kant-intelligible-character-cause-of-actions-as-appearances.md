thinker: t-kant
statement: "One would have to grant a subject of the world of sense that has in itself a faculty by which it can be the cause of appearances, beside its empirical character, also an intelligible character, through which it is indeed the cause of its actions as appearances, but which itself stands under no conditions of sensibility and is not itself appearance; one could call the first, the empirical character, the character of such a thing in the appearance, and the second the character of the thing in itself."

The eighth and ninth sentences of the section (AA 3:366.35–367.05), the last of its first
paragraph. The main clauses are subjunctive, »würde man ... einräumen müssen«, »Man könnte
... nennen«, kept as »would have to« and »could«; the relative clause »dadurch es zwar die
Ursache jener Handlungen als Erscheinungen ist« is indicative, and says what the intelligible
character is the cause of: »jener Handlungen«, the actions the sentence before considered as
appearances
([c-kant-empirical-character-connects-actions-by-laws-of-nature](../../c/kant-empirical-character-connects-actions-by-laws-of-nature/)).
»ihm« and »es« are neuter and bind to »Subjecte«; »der aber« is masculine and binds to
»Charakter«, so that it is the character, not the subject, of which the sentence says that it
stands under no conditions of sensibility and is not appearance. The naming sentence is kept
inside the statement because it is what makes the intelligible character »den Charakter des
Dinges an sich selbst«. This is the sentence in which the map holds the term, »intelligibelen Charakter«, in the Korpus's form, consistent with the first edition;
Nietzsche's JGB 36 puts »"intelligiblen Charakter"« in quotation marks
([c-nietzsche-world-would-be-will-to-power](../../c/nietzsche-world-would-be-will-to-power/)),
and whether they point at Kant's term no passage in the map says. The subject here is »einem
Subjecte der Sinnenwelt« and what it causes is its »Handlungen«; the word »Wille« does not
occur in the section. The comparison on the will sets this sentence in the row on the will as
the cause of the body's actions for those words, as what Kant says of a subject's causing its
actions as appearances, and says that reading B xxvii–xxviii's will »in der Erscheinung (den
sichtbaren Handlungen)« as the will of such a subject is the map's step.
