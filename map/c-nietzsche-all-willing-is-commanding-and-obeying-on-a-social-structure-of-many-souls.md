JGB 19, the fourteenth and last sentence: »Bei allem Wollen handelt es sich schlechterdings um
Befehlen und Gehorchen, auf der Grundlage, wie gesagt, eines Gesellschaftsbaus vieler "Seelen":
weshalb ein Philosoph sich das Recht nehmen sollte, Wollen an sich schon unter den Gesichtskreis der
Moral zu fassen: Moral nämlich als Lehre von den Herrschafts-Verhältnissen verstanden, unter denen das
Phänomen "Leben" entsteht.« The statement is the sentence less »wie gesagt«: »weshalb« is "which is why", the first colon becomes a comma before it and the second a comma before the repeated "morality, namely"; »wie gesagt«, "as said" in the passage, is dropped from the statement, which must be readable alone, and the drop is named here; it points back to the
twelfth sentence, »unser Leib ist ja nur ein Gesellschaftsbau vieler Seelen«, which is in the passage
and in no claim, so that the "many souls" the statement rests on are named there; »schlechterdings« is
"absolutely", »Wollen an sich schon« "willing as such already" (the passage says why not "in itself"),
»sollte« "should", a prescription kept as one. The »weshalb« runs inside the one sentence from what
all willing is a matter of to what a philosopher should claim the right to do; the map formalizes no
argument and sets no `depends_on`, as with the »folglich« inside a sentence of GD »Die vier grossen
Irrthümer« 7 ([c-nietzsche-men-thought-free-to-be-judged-and-punished](../../c/nietzsche-men-thought-free-to-be-judged-and-punished/)). On the
[comparison on the will](../../x/the-will/) the claim is set in the row on what the will is, for »Bei
allem Wollen handelt es sich ... um Befehlen und Gehorchen«, the sentence saying what all willing is a
matter of; the same claim is set in the row on what is drawn from the will for its »weshalb« clause, the sentence drawing from what all willing is what a philosopher should claim the right to do. No attack link and no shared premise: Kant's sentences on morality in the map,
B xxviii's supposition that morality presupposes freedom
([c-kant-if-freedom-unthinkable-morality-yields-to-mechanism](../../c/kant-if-freedom-unthinkable-morality-yields-to-mechanism/)) and its »So aber« on what morality
needs ([c-kant-same-action-in-another-relation](../../c/kant-same-action-in-another-relation/)), suppose what morality presupposes and say what it needs, and stand in the same row on what is drawn from the
will; this sentence says under what morality, understood as a doctrine of the relations of domination,
willing should be grasped, and neither denies nor asserts theirs. No Kant or Schopenhauer statement in
the map speaks of commanding or obeying, or of willing as a relation of domination. The many »"Seelen"«
and JGB 17's subject inferred by grammatical habit
([c-nietzsche-subject-inferred-by-grammatical-habit](../../c/nietzsche-subject-inferred-by-grammatical-habit/)) or JGB 54's »ich« as a synthesis
([c-nietzsche-ich-a-synthesis-made-by-thinking](../../c/nietzsche-ich-a-synthesis-made-by-thinking/)) are joined by no connective, and no link is set.
