Unsere Principien, welche den Gebrauch der Vernunft blos auf mögliche Erfahrung einschränken, könnten demnach selbst transscendent werden und die Schranken unsrer Vernunft für Schranken der Möglichkeit der Dinge selbst ausgeben, wie davon Humes Dialogen zum Beispiel dienen können, wenn nicht eine sorgfältige Kritik die Grenzen unserer Vernunft auch in Ansehung ihres empirischen Gebrauchs bewachte und ihren Anmaßungen ihr Ziel setzte."
translation: "But on the other hand it would be a still greater absurdity if we wanted to admit no things in themselves at all, or to pass off our experience as the only possible mode of cognition of things, hence our intuition in space and time as the only possible intuition and our discursive understanding as the archetype of every possible understanding, and so wanted principles of the possibility of experience to be taken for universal conditions of things in themselves. Our principles, which restrict the use of reason merely to possible experience, could accordingly themselves become transcendent and pass off the bounds of our reason for bounds of the possibility of things themselves, of which Hume's Dialogues can serve as an example, if a careful critique did not guard the limits of our reason even with regard to its empirical use and put a stop to its pretensions."

The second paragraph of §57, which answers the first. "Schranken" (4:351.08) is rendered
"bounds" and "Grenzen" (4:351.10) "limits", following the distinction Kant draws at AA
4:352.21–25; the rendering is reversed in some English translations, and a reader should
check the German. "Humes Dialogen" (4:351.09) are the *Dialogues concerning Natural
Religion*, which Kant names without further reference.
