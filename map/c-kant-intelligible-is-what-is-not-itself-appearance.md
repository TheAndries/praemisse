The first sentence of the section (AA 3:366.08–09), a definition in the first person, »Ich nenne«, which the statement keeps, and one of the section's indicative sentences. What is called intelligible is »dasjenige an einem Gegenstande
der Sinne«, something *in* or *at* an object of the senses; the sentence says of it only that it is not itself appearance. The next sentence applies the word
to the causality of a being that has, in itself, a faculty that is no object of sensible
intuition
([c-kant-causality-of-a-being-considered-from-two-sides](../../c/kant-causality-of-a-being-considered-from-two-sides/)),
and the later sentences to the »intelligibelen Charakter«
([c-kant-intelligible-character-cause-of-actions-as-appearances](../../c/kant-intelligible-character-cause-of-actions-as-appearances/)).
How »an einem Gegenstande der Sinne« relates to the two meanings of the object at B xxvii
is what the two readings of Kant in the map differ on ([two-aspect](../../r/kant-two-aspect/),
[two-object](../../r/kant-two-object/)), and the map does not pick; the sentence itself
names one object of the senses and something in it.
