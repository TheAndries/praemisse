JGB 19, the eleventh sentence, the first of the close: »"Freiheit des Willens" - das ist das Wort
für jenen vielfachen Lust-Zustand des Wollenden, der befiehlt und sich zugleich mit dem Ausführenden
als Eins setzt, - der als solcher den Triumph über Widerstände mit geniesst, aber bei sich urtheilt,
sein Wille selbst sei es, der eigentlich die Widerstände überwinde.« The statement is the sentence,
with the dash after the term rendered by "is", the reported judgement's subjunctive »sei ... überwinde«
as "it is his will itself that really overcomes" inside the frame "judges to himself that", »mit«
kept as "also", »eigentlich« as "really", »zugleich« as "at the same time". The sentence follows the
two on the »Wunderlichste am Willen«
([c-nietzsche-belief-will-and-action-one-an-erroneous-inference](../../c/nietzsche-belief-will-and-action-one-an-erroneous-inference/)), of which the tenth has the one
who wills believe »dass Wille und Aktion irgendwie Eins seien« and ascribe the success to the will
itself; this sentence has him »sich zugleich mit dem Ausführenden als Eins setzt« and judge that his will
overcomes the resistances. No connective joins the eleventh sentence to the tenth, and no `depends_on` is
set; the twelfth, »Der Wollende nimmt dergestalt ...«, continues from this one by »dergestalt«, a manner adverb and not a connective like »mithin«, and is in
the passage and in no claim. On the [comparison on the will](../../x/the-will/) the claim is set in
the row on whether the will is free, for the term »Freiheit des Willens«, the sentence saying what the
word is the word for. It is the aphorism's second gloss on the term, after the seventh sentence's
affect of superiority ([c-nietzsche-freedom-of-the-will-the-affect-of-superiority-over-him-who-must-obey](../../c/nietzsche-freedom-of-the-will-the-affect-of-superiority-over-him-who-must-obey/)); the two are joined by no connective, and the map does not say
whether the state of pleasure and the affect are one, since no sentence does. No attack link is set.
Schopenhauer's § 18 states the identity of the act of will and the action of the body
([c-schopenhauer-act-of-will-and-action-of-body-identical](../../c/schopenhauer-act-of-will-and-action-of-body-identical/)); this sentence says that the one who
wills posits himself as one with the one who carries it out and judges the triumph his will's, and does not state
that the two are not one, which the ninth and tenth sentences, set as the attack, call an erroneous inference;
the denial is not inside this statement, and a reader who holds that the sentence denies § 18 may dispute the absence. Kant's statements in the map have the will thought as free
([c-kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself](../../c/kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself/)) and freedom thinkable
([c-kant-freedom-thinkable-if-critical-distinction-holds](../../c/kant-freedom-thinkable-if-critical-distinction-holds/)), none that the word names no state of
the one who wills, and this sentence denies none of them.
