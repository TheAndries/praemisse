The whole sentence at B xxvii–xxviii (AA 3:17.18–28), the positive half of the case of the
will. The statement is conditional because the sentence is: Kant makes the result depend
on the critique's not having erred and on the deduction's being right, and does not here
assert either, though the »da« of »da sie ... nehmen lehrt« says that the critique does
teach the two meanings. The result itself has three parts, kept in order: the will in the
appearance is »dem Naturgesetze nothwendig gemäß und so fern nicht frei«; the same will
»als einem Dinge an sich selbst angehörig« is »jenem nicht unterworfen, mithin als frei«;
and the two are thought »ohne daß hiebei ein Widerspruch vorgeht«. The verb is »gedacht«,
thought, not cognized; the next sentence says why it cannot be cognized
([c-kant-freedom-cannot-be-cognized-by-speculative-reason](../../c/kant-freedom-cannot-be-cognized-by-speculative-reason/)).

Kant says the will is thought »als einem Dinge an sich selbst angehörig«, as belonging to
a thing in itself, and no passage in the map says that the will is the thing in itself; the comparison with
Schopenhauer, who says »Ding an sich aber ist allein der Wille« (WWV I § 21), rests on that
difference and is set out on the comparison page. On the
[two-aspect reading](../../r/kant-two-aspect/) the »zweierlei Bedeutung« are two ways of
considering one object and the will's belonging to a thing in itself is the same will
considered apart from the conditions of sensibility; on the
[two-object reading](../../r/kant-two-object/) the thing in itself the will belongs to is
a being distinct from the appearance. The sentence's »eben derselbe Wille« is one will
under both; the map does not pick. The negative half of the case, what would follow had
the distinction not been made, is
[c-kant-without-distinction-free-will-is-a-contradiction](../../c/kant-without-distinction-free-will-is-a-contradiction/),
and the application to one action, »in anderer Beziehung genommen«, is
[c-kant-same-action-in-another-relation](../../c/kant-same-action-in-another-relation/)
(B xxviii–xxix). It is a premise of
[a-kant-freedom-thinkable-under-the-critical-distinction](../../a/kant-freedom-thinkable-under-the-critical-distinction/).
