The fifth sentence of the passage (AA 3:363.11–19), asserted. »in dieser Bedeutung« is the cosmological sense of the sentence before ([c-kant-freedom-in-the-cosmological-sense-the-faculty-of-beginning-a-state-of-itself](../../c/kant-freedom-in-the-cosmological-sense-the-faculty-of-beginning-a-state-of-itself/)), restated in the statement so that it reads alone; the closing »wodurch denn das ganze Feld der Erfahrung ... in einem Inbegriff bloßer Natur verwandelt wird« is in the passage and dropped from the statement, named here. »erstlich« and »zweitens« are kept as "first" and "second", »auch« as "also", »bestimmt« as "determinately", »müsse« inside the »weil« clause as "must". The sentence says of freedom in this sense that it is an idea and that its object can be given in no experience, beside which stands B xxviii's sentence that freedom cannot be cognized by speculative reason or by empirical observation ([c-kant-freedom-cannot-be-cognized-by-speculative-reason](../../c/kant-freedom-cannot-be-cognized-by-speculative-reason/)); the two statements have different predicates and the map sets no link between them. No Schopenhauer or Nietzsche statement in the map says what kind of idea freedom is or whether its object can be given in experience: Schopenhauer's § 55 derives the will's freedom from its being the thing in itself ([c-schopenhauer-will-as-such-free-since-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-will-as-such-free-since-thing-in-itself/)), and Nietzsche's JGB 21 says what the desire for freedom of the will in the superlative sense is ([c-nietzsche-desire-for-freedom-of-the-will-is-to-be-causa-sui](../../c/nietzsche-desire-for-freedom-of-the-will-is-to-be-causa-sui/)); neither asserts or denies this sentence in its words, and no link is set. Set on the comparison on the will in the row on freedom, since 2026-10-27, for the word »Freiheit«.
