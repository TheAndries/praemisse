**The texts it rests on.** B xxvi: it is reserved "daß wir eben dieselben Gegenstände auch
als Dinge an sich selbst, wenn gleich nicht erkennen, doch wenigstens müssen denken
können" (AA 3:17.03–05): the same objects, thought also as things in themselves. B xxviii–
xxix: freedom puts no obstacle in the way of "dem Naturmechanism eben derselben Handlung
(in anderer Beziehung genommen)" (3:18.24–25): one action, two relations. A 26 / B 42:
space is no determination of things "die an Gegenständen selbst haftete, und welche bliebe,
wenn man auch von allen subjectiven Bedingungen der Anschauung abstrahirte" (3:55.04–05):
the thing in itself is what is left when one abstracts from the subjective conditions.

**What it holds.** On this reading the thing in itself is the object considered apart from
the conditions under which it appears (Prauss 1974; Allison 2004, ch. 2). The claim that
things in themselves are unknown ([c-kant-things-in-themselves-unknown](../../c/kant-things-in-themselves-unknown/))
is then a claim about what can be known of objects so considered, not about a second set of
entities, and the argument at B xxvi that appearance presupposes something that appears
([a-kant-appearance-requires-thing-in-itself](../../a/kant-appearance-requires-thing-in-itself/))
is read as a claim about the concept of appearance, not an inference to the existence of a
further thing.

**What depends on it.** Read this way, the claim that the same action may be taken in
another relation ([c-kant-same-action-in-another-relation](../../c/kant-same-action-in-another-relation/),
B xxviii–xxix, 3:18.24–25) is the doctrine applied: one action, free in one relation and
mechanically determined in the other, with no second entity. The Prolegomena §57 argument
([a-kant-denying-things-in-themselves-absurd](../../a/kant-denying-things-in-themselves-absurd/))
is read as defending a way of considering objects, not the existence of further ones.

**What tells against it.** B xxvi continues "daß Erscheinung ohne etwas wäre, was da
erscheint" (3:17.06–07), and Prolegomena §32 says the understanding "gesteht auch das
Dasein von Dingen an sich selbst zu" and speaks of "Wesen, die den Erscheinungen zum
Grunde liegen" (4:315.02–05), and in the same passage of "die Art, wie unsre Sinnen von
diesem unbekannten Etwas afficirt werden" (4:314.36–315.01): a grounding and an affecting
relation between distinct relata, the text the
[two-object reading](../../r/kant-two-object/) rests on. A 42 / B 59 asks how it stands
with "den Gegenständen an sich und abgesondert von aller dieser Receptivität unserer
Sinnlichkeit" (3:65.17–18), which both readings can claim.
