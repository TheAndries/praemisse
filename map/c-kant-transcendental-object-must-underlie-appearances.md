The »da« clause of the fifth sentence of the section (AA 3:366.22–24), with its own »weil«
clause inside it. »diesen« binds to »Erscheinungen« in the sentence before. The clause
asserts two things in order: that appearances »an sich keine Dinge sind«, and that for that
reason a transcendental object »zum Grunde liegen muß«, one »der sie als bloße Vorstellungen
bestimmt«; the statement keeps the order and the »because«. It is a premise of the map's
checked argument
[a-kant-double-side-contradicts-no-concept-of-experience](../../a/kant-double-side-contradicts-no-concept-of-experience/),
where the two assertions are two atoms. The word is »transscendentaler Gegenstand«, not
»Ding an sich selbst«; B xxvi has a near thought, as the map reads it, that there would otherwise be »Erscheinung ohne etwas wäre, was da erscheint«
([c-kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing](../../c/kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing/)),
and whether the transcendental object of this sentence is the thing in itself of that one is
not stated in any passage in the map; the map does not say it, and sets no link that would
need it. The sentence says what underlies appearances must do, »bestimmt«, and not what it is. Since 2026-10-28 the claim is set on the comparison on the thing in itself, in the row on the division of appearance from thing in itself, beside the sentence of the paragraph before this section's heading that appearances, in fact »bloße Vorstellungen«, »müssen ... selbst noch Gründe haben, die nicht Erscheinungen sind« ([c-kant-appearances-mere-representations-must-have-grounds-that-are-not-appearances](../../c/kant-appearances-mere-representations-must-have-grounds-that-are-not-appearances/)), which gives what appearances need a third name, as the map reads the two; no connective joins the two sentences across the heading, the map sets no link between them and reads neither as the other's thing in itself, and the premise shared with Schopenhauer's § 21, that an appearance is a representation and nothing more, is entered on that sentence and not on this one, whose »bloße Vorstellungen« is what the transcendental object determines appearances as, a determination by the object and not a statement of what appearance is, a reading open to dispute.
