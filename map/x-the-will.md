# What the three say, in their own words

Kant, B xxvii–xxviii: if the critique has not erred, »da sie das Object in zweierlei
Bedeutung nehmen lehrt, nämlich als Erscheinung oder als Ding an sich selbst«, and if the
deduction of its concepts of the understanding is right, »so wird eben derselbe Wille in der
Erscheinung (den sichtbaren Handlungen) als dem Naturgesetze nothwendig gemäß und so fern
nicht frei und doch andererseits als einem Dinge an sich selbst angehörig jenem nicht
unterworfen, mithin als frei gedacht, ohne daß hiebei ein Widerspruch vorgeht« (AA
3:17.18–28,
[c-kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself](../../c/kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself/)).
Freedom is thought, not cognized: the soul, considered from that side, I can cognize »durch
keine speculative Vernunft, (noch weniger durch empirische Beobachtung)«, and hence not
freedom either, »so kann ich mir doch die Freiheit denken, d. i. die Vorstellung davon
enthält wenigstens keinen Widerspruch in sich« (B xxviii, 3:17.28–18.11,
[c-kant-freedom-cannot-be-cognized-by-speculative-reason](../../c/kant-freedom-cannot-be-cognized-by-speculative-reason/),
[c-kant-freedom-thinkable-if-critical-distinction-holds](../../c/kant-freedom-thinkable-if-critical-distinction-holds/)).
Kant, A 538–541 / B 566–569, since 2026-10-19: if what must be regarded as appearance »an
sich selbst auch ein Vermögen hat, welches kein Gegenstand der sinnlichen Anschauung ist,
wodurch es aber doch die Ursache von Erscheinungen sein kann«, its causality can be
considered »auf zwei Seiten«, intelligible and sensible (AA 3:366.09–16,
[c-kant-causality-of-a-being-considered-from-two-sides](../../c/kant-causality-of-a-being-considered-from-two-sides/));
such a subject would have to be granted »einen intelligibelen Charakter ..., dadurch es zwar
die Ursache jener Handlungen als Erscheinungen ist, der aber selbst unter keinen Bedingungen
der Sinnlichkeit steht« (3:366.35–367.03,
[c-kant-intelligible-character-cause-of-actions-as-appearances](../../c/kant-intelligible-character-cause-of-actions-as-appearances/)),
a character that »könnte zwar niemals unmittelbar gekannt werden« (3:367.15–16,
[c-kant-intelligible-character-never-known-immediately](../../c/kant-intelligible-character-never-known-immediately/)),
under which »dieses thätige Wesen« would be »von aller Naturnothwendigkeit ... unabhängig und
frei« (3:368.01–02,
[c-kant-active-being-free-of-natural-necessity-by-intelligible-character](../../c/kant-active-being-free-of-natural-necessity-by-intelligible-character/)),
so that freedom and nature would be found »bei eben denselben Handlungen ... zugleich und ohne allen Widerstreit« (3:368.10–13,
[c-kant-freedom-and-nature-together-in-the-same-actions](../../c/kant-freedom-and-nature-together-in-the-same-actions/)).
The section's subject is »einem Subjecte der Sinnenwelt« and its »Handlungen«; the word »Wille« does not occur in it; its first paragraph defines, supposes and asserts, and every main clause of its second, third and fourth paragraphs stands under »würde«.

Schopenhauer, §§ 18–23: the body is given to the subject »auf zwei ganz verschiedene
Weisen«, as representation and »als jenes Jedem unmittelbar Bekannte, welches das Wort
Wille bezeichnet« (§ 18, ZA I 142,
[c-schopenhauer-body-given-in-two-ways](../../c/schopenhauer-body-given-in-two-ways/));
»Der Willensakt und die Aktion des Leibes ... sind Eines und das Selbe, nur auf zwei gänzlich
verschiedene Weisen gegeben« (ibid.,
[c-schopenhauer-act-of-will-and-action-of-body-identical](../../c/schopenhauer-act-of-will-and-action-of-body-identical/));
»Ding an sich aber ist allein der Wille« (§ 21, ZA I 154,
[c-schopenhauer-will-alone-is-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-will-alone-is-thing-in-itself/));
the name is »nur eine denominatio a potiori« (§ 22, ZA I 154,
[c-schopenhauer-will-named-a-potiori](../../c/schopenhauer-will-named-a-potiori/)), and the
word designates »ein durchaus unmittelbar Erkanntes« (§ 22, ZA I 155,
[c-schopenhauer-will-not-inferred-but-immediately-known](../../c/schopenhauer-will-not-inferred-but-immediately-known/));
the will as thing in itself is »schlechthin grundlos« and »Einer« (§ 23, ZA I 157,
[c-schopenhauer-will-groundless-and-one](../../c/schopenhauer-will-groundless-and-one/)).

Nietzsche, JGB 16, 19 and 36 and GD: there are still harmless self-observers who believe
in immediate certainties, »zum Beispiel "ich denke", oder, wie es der Aberglaube
Schopenhauer's war, "ich will"« (JGB 16,
[c-nietzsche-schopenhauer-ich-will-a-superstition](../../c/nietzsche-schopenhauer-ich-will-a-superstition/));
»Wollen scheint mir vor Allem etwas Complicirtes, Etwas, das nur als Wort eine Einheit ist«
(JGB 19, [c-nietzsche-willing-is-complicated](../../c/nietzsche-willing-is-complicated/));
among a chain of »irrthümlichen Schlüssen«, »der Wollende glaubt, mit einem ziemlichen Grad
von Sicherheit, dass Wille und Aktion irgendwie Eins seien« (JGB 19,
[c-nietzsche-belief-will-and-action-one-an-erroneous-inference](../../c/nietzsche-belief-will-and-action-one-an-erroneous-inference/));
»Der Wille bewegt nichts mehr, erklärt folglich auch nichts mehr« (»Die vier grossen
Irrthümer« 3, [c-nietzsche-will-moves-nothing](../../c/nietzsche-will-moves-nothing/)); and
JGB 36 closes, »Die Welt von innen gesehen ... sie wäre eben "Wille zur Macht" und nichts
ausserdem« ([c-nietzsche-world-would-be-will-to-power](../../c/nietzsche-world-would-be-will-to-power/)),
a sentence whose force is the map's second contested reading, the one of Nietzsche (below).

# Where a premise is shared

One premise is shared on this page, since 2026-10-19, in two entries, and it is on time, not
on the will. Kant, A 539 / B 566–569, in the clause in which he says outright, not under a »würde«, what time is the condition of and what not: the acting subject would by its intelligible character stand
under no conditions of time, »denn die Zeit ist nur die Bedingung der Erscheinungen, nicht
aber der Dinge an sich selbst« (AA 3:367.07–08,
[c-kant-intelligible-character-under-no-conditions-of-time](../../c/kant-intelligible-character-under-no-conditions-of-time/)).
Schopenhauer, § 24: »Was auch immer das Ding an sich sei, so hat Kant richtig geschlossen, daß Zeit, Raum und Kausalität ... nicht Bestimmungen desselben seyn, sondern ihm erst zukommen konnten, nachdem und sofern es Vorstellung geworden, d. h. nur seiner Erscheinung angehörten, nicht ihm selbst« (§ 24, Grisebach pp. 175–176, the ZA page not given on the passage, [c-schopenhauer-kant-rightly-concluded-forms-not-determinations-of-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-kant-rightly-concluded-forms-not-determinations-of-thing-in-itself/)), the factive »richtig geschlossen« asserting what it reports, as the map already reads it for the premise on space;

# Where they diverge

**On what the will is.** Kant's will is thought »als einem Dinge an sich selbst
angehörig«, as belonging to a thing in itself; Schopenhauer's will is the thing in itself,
»Ding an sich aber ist allein der Wille«. That is the hinge of the comparison, and it is a
difference in the texts' own words: no Kant passage in the map says that the will is a
thing in itself, and what the thing in itself is remains, on his other sentences in the map,
unknown ([c-kant-things-in-themselves-unknown](../../c/kant-things-in-themselves-unknown/)).
What »angehörig« comes to depends on the reading of Kant: on the
[two-aspect reading](../../r/kant-two-aspect/) the will's belonging to a thing in itself is
the same will considered apart from the conditions of sensibility, on the
[two-object reading](../../r/kant-two-object/) the thing in itself it belongs to is a being
distinct from the appearance; the map does not pick, and the section on the readings
below says what each does to the comparison. Schopenhauer's § 22 says why the thing in
itself bears the name: it is that »welches als solches nimmermehr Objekt ist«, and had, if
it was to be thought objectively, to borrow name and concept from the most perfect of its
appearances, »des Menschen Wille«, so that the naming is »nur eine denominatio a potiori«
by which the concept will receives a greater extension
([c-schopenhauer-will-named-a-potiori](../../c/schopenhauer-will-named-a-potiori/)); the will so
named is free of every form of its appearance, »Schon die allgemeinste Form aller
Vorstellung, die des Objekts für ein Subjekt, trifft ihn nicht« (§ 23, ZA I 156,
[c-schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance](../../c/schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance/)),
groundless and one, and § 25 draws, by the map's
[checked argument](../../a/schopenhauer-will-knows-no-plurality/) on two bridges it names,
that if the thing in itself is the will it lies outside time and space and knows no plurality
([c-schopenhauer-will-apart-from-appearance-outside-time-and-space-knows-no-plurality](../../c/schopenhauer-will-apart-from-appearance-outside-time-and-space-knows-no-plurality/)).
The route to the identification is § 19's, conditional and epistemic: if the corporeal world
is to be more than our representation, »so müssen wir sagen, daß sie außer der Vorstellung,
also an sich und ihrem Innersten Wesen nach, Das sei, was wir in uns selbst unmittelbar als
Willen finden« (ZA I 148,
[a-schopenhauer-corporeal-world-in-itself-is-will](../../a/schopenhauer-corporeal-world-in-itself-is-will/)),
a reason and not a proof, as the argument's body says; the step from that conditional to §
21's assertion the map has not formalized. Nietzsche: willing seems to him, »scheint mir«, a unity only as a word, the
one word hiding »eine Mehrheit von Gefühlen«, a »commandirenden Gedanken« and »jener Affekt
des Commando's« (JGB 19,
[c-nietzsche-willing-contains-feelings-thought-and-affect](../../c/nietzsche-willing-contains-feelings-thought-and-affect/)),
and GD says of the will, »Heute wissen wir, dass er bloss ein Wort ist« (»Die ›Vernunft‹ in
der Philosophie« 5,
[c-nietzsche-will-as-cause-the-great-error](../../c/nietzsche-will-as-cause-the-great-error/)).
Since 2026-10-25 the map holds the aphorism whole, in four spans, and
two more of its sentences are set in this row: »Ein Mensch, der will -, befiehlt einem Etwas in
sich, das gehorcht oder von dem er glaubt, dass es gehorcht« (the eighth sentence,
[c-nietzsche-a-man-who-wills-commands-a-something-in-himself-that-obeys](../../c/nietzsche-a-man-who-wills-commands-a-something-in-himself-that-obeys/)), and the last, »Bei allem Wollen handelt es sich schlechterdings um Befehlen und
Gehorchen, auf der Grundlage, wie gesagt, eines Gesellschaftsbaus vieler "Seelen"« ([c-nietzsche-all-willing-is-commanding-and-obeying-on-a-social-structure-of-many-souls](../../c/nietzsche-all-willing-is-commanding-and-obeying-on-a-social-structure-of-many-souls/)),
whose »wie gesagt« points to the twelfth sentence, »unser Leib ist ja nur ein Gesellschaftsbau
vieler Seelen«, in the passage and in no claim, and whose »weshalb« clause, that a philosopher
should grasp willing as such already under morality understood as the doctrine of the relations of
domination, is carried in the one statement and sets the same claim in the row on what is drawn from the will, below. What is parallel between
these and Schopenhauer's cells in this row: nothing the map sets; Schopenhauer's will is the thing in
itself and free of the forms, Nietzsche's willing a commanding and obeying on an edifice of many
"souls", and no sentence in the map of either names the other on this point, so no link is set; the attack links the map holds from JGB 19 are the second sentence's on § 22 and the ninth and tenth sentences' on § 18, under *On immediacy* and *On the will as
cause* below. What JGB 36 then does with »Wille zur Macht« is the contested reading, below; the aphorism
puts »"intelligiblen Charakter"« in Nietzsche's own quotation marks; since 2026-10-19 the
map holds Kant's A 539 / B 566–569, where a subject of the world of sense would have to be granted an »intelligibelen Charakter«, the law of its causality, which one could call the character of the thing in itself, »der aber selbst unter keinen Bedingungen der Sinnlichkeit steht und selbst nicht Erscheinung ist«
([c-kant-intelligible-character-cause-of-actions-as-appearances](../../c/kant-intelligible-character-cause-of-actions-as-appearances/)),
and no passage in the map says whether Nietzsche's quotation marks point at Kant's term, so
the page sets the two side by side and does not call Nietzsche's Kant's. That section also
gives, in Kant's words, the two sides the hinge turns on: the causality of a being that has in
itself a faculty to cause appearances can be considered »als intelligibel nach ihrer Handlung
