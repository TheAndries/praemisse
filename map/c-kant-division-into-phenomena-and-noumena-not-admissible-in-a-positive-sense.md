The first sentence of the passage (AA 3:212.03–08; A 255 / B 311), asserted outright. Its
»daher«, which draws the sentence from the paragraph before it, is dropped from the statement
and named here, and no link is set on it, since it draws from a paragraph and not from one
sentence the map could name; »allerdings« is kept as "certainly", »gar nicht« as "at all",
»den letzteren« resolved by name as the intellectual concepts; the »denn« clause is kept as the ground, its »man kann ... ausgeben« turned passive, "cannot be passed off", the change named here. **The 1781 edition reads the sentence without »in positiver Bedeutung«**, as the collation note on the passage, its source, records, which the second edition adds; the Akademie text is the second
edition's, the statement follows it and keeps the qualification, and a reader of the first
edition finds the division refused without it, as the passage says. The map does not say
which edition's sentence is Kant's position; it holds the one the Akademie text prints and
names the other.

The sentence says of the division what it cannot be, admitted in a positive sense, and why,
no object being determinable for intellectual concepts; it does not say that there is no
division, and the sentences after it in the same passage say that the concept of a noumenon
taken problematically remains unavoidable and that the understanding calls things in
themselves, not considered as appearances, noumena
([c-kant-understanding-calls-things-in-themselves-noumena-and-thinks-them-only-as-an-unknown-something](../../c/kant-understanding-calls-things-in-themselves-noumena-and-thinks-them-only-as-an-unknown-something/)).
What "positive" refuses here is what B 307 calls the noumenon »in positiver Bedeutung«, the
object of a non-sensible intuition ([c-kant-noumenon-negative-only](../../c/kant-noumenon-negative-only/)),
the same words at 3:210.02 and 3:212.04–05; the identification of the two places' »positiv«
is the map's, drawn by no connective, and open to dispute.

No premise is shared and no attack is set. Nietzsche's GD Vernunft 6, fourth proposition,
»Die Welt scheiden in eine "wahre" und eine "scheinbare" ... ist nur eine Suggestion
der décadence« ([c-nietzsche-dividing-world-true-and-apparent-is-decadence](../../c/nietzsche-dividing-world-true-and-apparent-is-decadence/)),
set in the same row, condemns a division into a true and an apparent world; this sentence
refuses, in a positive sense, a division into a world of sense and a world of understanding
and admits it for concepts. Whether Nietzsche's »"wahre"« world is Kant's »Verstandeswelt«
no passage in the map says, and the two sentences do not deny each other in their words: one
says the division is a symptom, the other that it cannot be admitted in a positive sense,
which is nearer agreement than attack, and the map sets neither a premise nor an attack,
since Nietzsche's sentence does not assert what this one asserts and this one does not assert
what his does. Schopenhauer's claim that Kant's greatest merit is the distinction of
appearance from thing in itself (ZA II 513,
[c-schopenhauer-kants-greatest-merit-distinction-of-appearance-from-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-kants-greatest-merit-distinction-of-appearance-from-thing-in-itself/))
praises a distinction this sentence does not name, »Erscheinung« and »Ding an sich« against
»Phaenomena und Noumena« and »Sinnen- und Verstandeswelt«, and no passage in the map
identifies the two pairs; no premise is entered. Each absence is open to dispute.

Set on the comparison on the thing in itself, in the row on the division of appearance from
thing in itself and what follows from refusing it, since 2026-10-30, for what the sentence
says the division of the world is and is not: the row's first question. Not set in the row on
what can be said of the thing in itself, where the two paragraphs' five other claims stand,
since its subject is the division and not the noumenon.
