The first sentence of § 55, the opening of the fourth Book's treatment of the freedom of the will. The sentence draws the will's freedom from its being the thing in itself, »folgt schon daraus«, the step inside one sentence and not formalized; »nach unserer Ansicht« is "on our view" in the passage and "on the view of Schopenhauer's work" in the statement, so that it reads alone. The sentence does not say free of what; the fifth sentence of the passage does ([c-schopenhauer-will-not-subject-to-principle-of-sufficient-reason-knows-no-necessity-is-free](../../c/schopenhauer-will-not-subject-to-principle-of-sufficient-reason-knows-no-necessity-is-free/)). The thing-in-itself-hood it rests on is § 21's »Ding an sich aber ist allein der Wille« ([c-schopenhauer-will-alone-is-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-will-alone-is-thing-in-itself/)) and § 23's »Der Wille als Ding an sich« ([c-schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance](../../c/schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance/)), the same thinker, so no shared-premise link. No link to Kant is set. The nearest Kant statements in the map are B xxvii–xxviii, where the very same will is thought as not free in the appearance and as free »als einem Dinge an sich selbst angehörig« ([c-kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself](../../c/kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself/)), and the resolution of the third antinomy, where the active being would by its intelligible character be »von aller Naturnothwendigkeit ... unabhängig und frei« ([c-kant-active-being-free-of-natural-necessity-by-intelligible-character](../../c/kant-active-being-free-of-natural-necessity-by-intelligible-character/)): the first stands under »Wenn aber die Kritik nicht geirrt hat« and has the will »gedacht«, the second under »würde«, and the convention sets `shares_premise_with` only where both statements assert one proposition; Kant's will there belongs to a thing in itself and is not said to be one. A reader who holds that the two assert the same may dispute the absence. No Nietzsche statement in the map speaks of the will as thing in itself; JGB 36's conditional has the world as will to power on one reading and not on the other, and names no freedom.
