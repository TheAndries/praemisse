The first sentence of the aphorism's close, whole; »nur« is kept. It follows the
sentence that ends »nämlich mythologisch«, in the passage before this one
([c-nietzsche-projecting-sign-world-as-in-itself-is-mythology](../../c/nietzsche-projecting-sign-world-as-in-itself-is-mythology/)), and applies its
word to the »unfreie Wille«, the term in Nietzsche's quotation marks, which the third
sentence of the aphorism had called a misuse of cause and effect
([c-nietzsche-strike-the-unfree-will-with-the-free-will](../../c/nietzsche-strike-the-unfree-will-with-the-free-will/)); the text joins the two sentences by no connective, and the map sets no
`depends_on` and formalizes no argument, which a reader may ask for. What the sentence puts in the place of the »unfreie Wille«, and, as the map reads it, of the pair the sentence before asks to be struck, is »starken und schwachen Willen«, »im wirklichen Leben«. The comparison
[x-the-will](../../x/the-will/) sets the sentence first in its row on whether the will
is free, since 2026-10-20. No attack link is set on Kant's B xxvii–xxviii, where the very
same will is thought in the appearance »als dem Naturgesetze nothwendig gemäß und so fern
nicht frei« ([c-kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself](../../c/kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself/)):
Kant's »nicht frei« is a predicate thought of the will in the appearance if the critique is
right, and this sentence says of the »unfreie Wille«, named by its quotation marks and by
the misuse of cause and effect, that it is mythology; whether Kant's will thought as not
free is that unfree will no passage in the map says, and the map does not say it. Nor on
Schopenhauer's will as thing in itself »völlig frei von allen Formen«
([c-schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance](../../c/schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance/)), whose statement speaks of freedom from the forms of its appearance and not of a will free or unfree in its actions, and no passage in the map has Schopenhauer on the latter. A reader who holds that this sentence denies either may dispute the
absence.
