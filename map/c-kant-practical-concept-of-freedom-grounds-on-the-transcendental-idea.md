The first sentence of the passage (AA 3:363.25–28). The »daß« clause stands in the subjunctive, »gründe«, »ausmache«, under »Es ist überaus merkwürdig«, which asserts its content as remarkable, and the statement keeps that frame. »diese transscendentale Idee« is the idea of the passage before ([c-kant-freedom-in-the-cosmological-sense-a-pure-transcendental-idea](../../c/kant-freedom-in-the-cosmological-sense-a-pure-transcendental-idea/)), restated in the statement as "of the cosmological sense"; »jene« is read as that idea and »dieser« as the practical freedom of »derselben«, both feminine, by their distance, the passage and the statement resolving them as "this idea" and "practical freedom" (an English "the former" and "the latter" would reverse them by the English order), and »ihre Möglichkeit« as practical freedom's; a reader who binds the pronouns otherwise may dispute the reading. The sentence relates two of Kant's own concepts and no `depends_on` is set, the relation being grounding, not a step drawn by a connective. No Schopenhauer or Nietzsche statement in the map distinguishes a practical from a transcendental concept of freedom: § 55 has the concept of freedom negative ([c-schopenhauer-concept-of-freedom-negative](../../c/schopenhauer-concept-of-freedom-negative/)) and asks after the will as thing in itself and the person ([c-schopenhauer-person-never-free-though-appearance-of-free-will](../../c/schopenhauer-person-never-free-though-appearance-of-free-will/)), JGB 21 after »"Freiheit des Willens"« in the metaphysical superlative sense ([c-nietzsche-desire-for-freedom-of-the-will-is-to-be-causa-sui](../../c/nietzsche-desire-for-freedom-of-the-will-is-to-be-causa-sui/)); whether JGB 21's superlative sense is the transcendental idea and its »"unfreie Wille"« the practical concept's contrary no passage says, and no link is set.
