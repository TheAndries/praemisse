B xxvi–xxvii has only the first half, and negatively: without the reservation that we
can think the same objects as things in themselves, "the absurd proposition would follow
that there is appearance without anything that appears" (AA 3:17.05–07); it says
"something that appears", not "thing in itself". The second half is Prolegomena §32,
which puts it positively: by admitting appearances the understanding also admits the existence of things
in themselves, and the representation of such beings is not only admissible but
unavoidable (AA 4:314.33–315.06).
