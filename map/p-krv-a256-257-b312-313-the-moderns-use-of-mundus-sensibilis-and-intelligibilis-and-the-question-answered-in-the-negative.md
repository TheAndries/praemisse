original: "Ich finde indessen in den Schriften der Neueren einen ganz andern Gebrauch der Ausdrücke eines mundi sensibilis und intelligibilis, der von dem Sinne der Alten ganz abweicht, und wobei es freilich keine Schwierigkeit hat, aber auch nichts als leere Wortkrämerei angetroffen wird. Nach demselben hat es einigen beliebt, den Inbegriff der Erscheinungen, sofern er angeschaut wird, die Sinnenwelt, sofern aber der Zusammenhang derselben nach allgemeinen Verstandesgesetzen gedacht wird, die Verstandeswelt zu nennen. Die theoretische Astronomie, welche die bloße Beobachtung des bestirnten Himmels vorträgt, würde die erstere, die contemplative dagegen (etwa nach dem Copernicanischen Weltsystem, oder gar nach Newtons Gravitationsgesetzen erklärt) die zweite, nämlich eine intelligibele Welt, vorstellig machen. Aber eine solche Wortverdrehung ist eine bloße sophistische Ausflucht, um einer beschwerlichen Frage auszuweichen, dadurch daß man ihren Sinn zu seiner Gemächlichkeit herabstimmt. In Ansehung der Erscheinungen läßt sich allerdings Verstand und Vernunft brauchen; aber es frägt sich, ob diese auch noch einigen Gebrauch haben, wenn der Gegenstand nicht Erscheinung (Noumenon) ist, und in diesem Sinne nimmt man ihn, wenn er an sich als bloß intelligibel, d. i. dem Verstande allein und gar nicht den Sinnen gegeben, gedacht wird. Es ist also die Frage: ob außer jenem empirischen Gebrauche des Verstandes (selbst in der Newtonischen Vorstellung des Weltbaues) noch ein transscendentaler möglich sei, der auf das Noumenon als einen Gegenstand gehe, welche Frage wir verneinend beantwortet haben."
translation: "I find, however, in the writings of the moderns an entirely different use of the expressions of a mundus sensibilis and intelligibilis, which deviates entirely from the sense of the ancients, and in which there is admittedly no difficulty, but also nothing to be met with but empty word-mongering. According to it, it has pleased some to call the sum total of appearances, insofar as it is intuited, the world of sense, but insofar as the connection of them is thought according to universal laws of the understanding, the world of understanding. Theoretical astronomy, which presents the mere observation of the starry heavens, would represent the former, the contemplative by contrast (explained, say, according to the Copernican world-system, or even according to Newton's laws of gravitation) the second, namely an intelligible world. But such a twisting of words is a mere sophistical evasion, so as to escape a troublesome question by lowering its sense to one's convenience. In regard to appearances, understanding and reason can certainly be used; but the question is whether these still have any use when the object is not appearance (noumenon), and one takes the object in this sense when it is thought as in itself merely intelligible, that is, as given to the understanding alone and not at all to the senses. The question is thus whether, besides that empirical use of the understanding (even in the Newtonian representation of the structure of the world), a transcendental one is also possible, which goes to the noumenon as an object, a question which we have answered in the negative."

**Translation.** »der Neueren« "the moderns" and »der Alten« "the ancients"; »Wortkrämerei« "word-mongering", with »leere«
"empty"; »freilich« "admittedly" and »allerdings« "certainly", kept apart, »allerdings« as the map renders it at
B 311 (»obgleich Begriffe allerdings die Eintheilung ... zulassen«); »Inbegriff« "sum total", as in
Kant's footnote at B xxvi; »Sinnenwelt« "world of sense" and »Verstandeswelt« "world of
understanding", as at B 311; »vorstellig machen« "represent"; »die erstere ... die zweite« "the
former ... the second", the two worlds in the order the second sentence names them, so that the
English order is the German; »Wortverdrehung« "twisting of words"; »Ausflucht« "evasion";
»beschwerlichen« "troublesome"; »Gemächlichkeit« "convenience"; »herabstimmt« "lowering"; »es frägt sich« "the question is"; »auch noch einigen Gebrauch« "still ... any use", the »auch« of »auch noch« absorbed into "still", a drop named here; »Es ist also die Frage: ob« "The question is thus whether", the text's colon dropped, named here; »an sich als bloß intelligibel« "as in
itself merely intelligible"; »Weltbau« "the structure of the world"; »möglich sei« "is possible"
and »gehe« "goes", the subjunctive of the indirect question rendered by the indicative, as on every
Kant passage in the map; »verneinend« "in the negative". The second sentence's »demselben« is the first sentence's »Gebrauch«, the moderns' use of the two expressions; the fifth sentence's »ihn« is the object,
»der Gegenstand«, of the clause before it, and »diese« understanding and reason; the sixth
sentence's »jenem empirischen Gebrauche« is the use of understanding and reason in regard to
appearances that the fifth sentence names, the resolutions the map's.

**What is held and what is not.** The fifth sentence is a claim set in the row on the division of
the [comparison on the thing in itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) since 2026-11-02
([c-kant-object-taken-as-noumenon-when-thought-as-in-itself-merely-intelligible](../../c/kant-object-taken-as-noumenon-when-thought-as-in-itself-merely-intelligible/)),
for what its last clause says the object is taken as when it is not appearance; the sixth a claim
set in that page's row on what can be said of the thing in itself
([c-kant-question-of-a-transcendental-use-of-the-understanding-reaching-the-noumenon-answered-in-the-negative](../../c/kant-question-of-a-transcendental-use-of-the-understanding-reaching-the-noumenon-answered-in-the-negative/)),
for the answer it reports as given. The first four sentences, on the moderns' use of the two
expressions, on what some have pleased to call the world of sense and the world of understanding,
on the two astronomies, and on such a twisting of words as a sophistical evasion, are in the passage and in no claim: the division they report and judge, »leere Wortkrämerei« and »eine bloße sophistische Ausflucht«, asserted outright, is the moderns' division of »den Inbegriff der Erscheinungen« into a world of sense, »sofern er angeschaut wird«, and a world of understanding, »sofern aber der Zusammenhang derselben nach allgemeinen Verstandesgesetzen gedacht wird«, a division within appearances, as the map reads the second sentence, and not the row's division between appearance and thing in itself, which B 311 names in the same two words; so what the first and fourth sentences say that division is not is said of another division, and the four fall outside the criterion of the row on the division, as of the row on what can be said, which they do not touch, the reading open to dispute.
