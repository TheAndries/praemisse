The argument is the last sentence of [KrV B 308](../../p/krv-b308-at-the-noumenon-all-use-and-meaning-of-the-categories-cease-and-the-possibility-of-a-thing-proved-only-by-a-corresponding-intuition/)
and the first two clauses of [KrV B 308–309](../../p/krv-b308-309/) (AA 3:210.21–27), the one step in the chapter's text in the map that is a »da ..., so« drawn from a conditional the sentence before states. Premise 1 is the third sentence of B 308, a conditional
whose antecedent Kant asserts in no passage in the map: »Wenn wir also die Kategorien auf
Gegenstände, die nicht als Erscheinungen betrachtet werden, anwenden wollten, so müßten wir eine
andere Anschauung als die sinnliche zum Grunde legen, und alsdann wäre der Gegenstand ein Noumenon in
positiver Bedeutung«; the form gives it as `a -> (i and o)`, the claim whole, its second consequent,
that the object would be a noumenon in the positive sense, carried in the atom `o` and used by no
step of the derivation, so that the premise is the claim and not a part of it. Premise 2 is the
ground clause of the next sentence, »Da nun eine solche, nämlich die intellectuelle Anschauung,
schlechterdings außer unserem Erkenntnißvermögen liegt«, asserted as the ground of the »so«, whose
»eine solche« points to premise 1's »eine andere Anschauung als die sinnliche« and whose »nämlich«
is Kant's own identification of that intuition with the intellectual; the atom `e` is the claim's statement, and the identification enters the derivation there and not in premise 1, whose atom `i` says only "an intuition other than the sensible", the words of its sentence.
Premise 3 is the map's: the text passes from the intuition's lying outside our faculty of cognition
to the restriction of the categories' use and does not say that what lies outside our faculty of
cognition cannot be taken as ground, `e -> not i`; the bridge identifies »außer unserem
Erkenntnißvermögen liegt« with the denial of premise 1's »zum Grunde legen«, which a reader may
dispute; premise 1's »müßten«, "would have to", is flattened in `i` to "is taken as ground", the
flattening the map's and named here. Premise 4 is the map's: the sentence's conclusion speaks of »die Grenze der Gegenstände der
Erfahrung«, premise 1 of »Gegenstände, die nicht als Erscheinungen betrachtet werden«, and the text
does not say in words that to reach beyond the one is to apply the categories to the other, `u -> a`;
the bridge is that every object regarded as appearance is an object of experience, so that an object beyond the limit of the objects of experience is not regarded as appearance, and to use the categories on it is to apply them to it: one direction only, the least the step needs, and two identifications, of
the objects regarded as appearances with objects of experience and of the sentence's »Gebrauch« with
premise 1's »anwenden«, neither made in words by any passage in the map, named here as the map's, and
which a reader may dispute. The conclusion is the sentence's main clause,
»so kann auch der Gebrauch der Kategorien keinesweges über die Grenze der Gegenstände der Erfahrung
hinausreichen«, `not u`, the claim's statement word for word, the atom `u` carrying the modal "can" so that its
denial is the statement's "can in no way".

The derivation is one step of modus ponens and two of modus tollens: from premises 2 and 3, no intuition other than the
sensible is taken as ground, `not i`; from premise 1, then, the categories are not applied to
objects not regarded as appearances, `not a`; from premise 4, their use does not reach beyond the
limit of the objects of experience, `not u`. That the »so« draws from premise 1 as well as from the
ground clause it follows is the map's reading of the frame: the clause names only the intuition,
and the sentence before, to which its »eine solche« points, is what makes the intuition's absence a
restriction on the categories. The rival form reads the »da ..., so« as the text's one step, a
sufficient condition on one bridge, `e -> not u`, with premise 1 and both bridges of this form gone,
as the map read A 538's »da ..., so« on 2026-10-19
([a-kant-double-side-contradicts-no-concept-of-experience](../../a/kant-double-side-contradicts-no-concept-of-experience/)),
which named its rival in turn; the map prefers this form because the ground clause's »eine solche«
points to premise 1 and the step from an intuition we lack to a restriction of the categories is
stated nowhere but there, and a reader who holds that the honest form is the text's one step on one
bridge may dispute it.

The truth table says only that `not u` holds in every assignment that satisfies the four premises.
It does not say that applying the categories beyond appearances would require another intuition,
that the intellectual intuition lies outside our faculty of cognition, or that the two bridges are
faithful; those are the premises and matters of fidelity. Nor does the argument reach the
sentence's last clause, that what we call noumenon must be understood only in the negative sense
([c-kant-noumenon-negative-only](../../c/kant-noumenon-negative-only/)): the text draws that by »also«
(3:210.32), after the clause that beings of the understanding correspond to beings of sense and that
our concepts of the understanding, as mere forms of thought for our sensible intuition, do not reach
them in the least (3:210.28–32), which is in the passage and in the statement of no claim; that
step is set as a link from the conclusion's claim, by the connective, and not as an argument, and a
reader who wants it made one, with that clause as a claim, may ask. The chain into the argument is
in the links: premise 1 `depends_on`, by B 308's »also«, the second sentence of B 308, that the
possibility of a thing is proved only by a corresponding intuition
([c-kant-possibility-of-a-thing-never-proved-from-the-non-contradiction-of-its-concept-but-only-by-a-corresponding-intuition](../../c/kant-possibility-of-a-thing-never-proved-from-the-non-contradiction-of-its-concept-but-only-by-a-corresponding-intuition/)),
and the first sentence's »denn« clause
([c-kant-at-the-noumenon-all-use-and-even-all-meaning-of-the-categories-cease](../../c/kant-at-the-noumenon-all-use-and-even-all-meaning-of-the-categories-cease/)),
neither of which is a premise here. Whether the objects regarded as appearances of premise 4 are the
appearances of the two readings of Kant
([two-aspect](../../r/kant-two-aspect/), [two-object](../../r/kant-two-object/)) the form does not
need to settle, and the map does not pick.
