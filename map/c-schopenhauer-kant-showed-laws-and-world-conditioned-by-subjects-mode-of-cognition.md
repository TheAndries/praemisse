The last of the four stored sentences at ZA II 516; the transcription's »Kam« is a slip for
»Kant«, said on the passage. »Jene Gesetze« are the laws of the first sentence ([c-schopenhauer-kant-showed-laws-of-experience-not-applicable-to-derive-existence](../../c/schopenhauer-kant-showed-laws-of-experience-not-applicable-to-derive-existence/)), the
principle of sufficient reason in its shapes. The sentence has two parts, which the statement
keeps in their order: what Kant showed, that the laws »und folglich die Welt selbst« are
conditioned by the subject's mode of cognition; and what followed from it, »woraus folgte«,
that along their guiding thread one comes no step forward in the cognition of the essence of
the world in itself and outside representation. The second part is drawn by the text from the
first inside one sentence, and the map keeps it inside one claim rather than formalizing the
step; a reader may ask for it as an argument. No link `shares_premise_with` is set: the Kant
statements in the map make space the subjective condition of sensibility
([c-kant-space-subjective-condition-of-sensibility](../../c/kant-space-subjective-condition-of-sensibility/)) and restrict cognition to objects of
experience ([c-kant-knowledge-limited-to-experience](../../c/kant-knowledge-limited-to-experience/)), and no Kant statement in the map
says that the world itself is conditioned by the subject's mode of cognition, which is
Schopenhauer's »folglich«, nor words the sentence's second part. In the comparison
[x-thing-in-itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) the sentence stands in the row on reaching the thing in itself by
inference and in the row on what in appearance is the subject's own, in both as Schopenhauer's
statement of what Kant showed. »Erkenntnißweise« is rendered »mode of cognition«, »Wesen«
»essence«.
