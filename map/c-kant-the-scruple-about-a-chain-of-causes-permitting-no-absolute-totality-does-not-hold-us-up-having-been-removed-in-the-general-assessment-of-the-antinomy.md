The first sentence of the passage (AA 3:369.01–06), asserted in the indicative, »so hält uns diese Bedenklichkeit doch gar nicht auf«, under a concessive clause, »Ob es aber gleich hiebei lediglich nach einer Kette von Ursachen aussieht«, which concedes how the matter looks and asserts nothing of how it is; the statement is the sentence whole, its »aber«, which opens the paragraph against the one before, dropped and named here. The »denn« clause grounds the main clause inside the sentence: the scruple has already been removed »in der allgemeinen Beurtheilung der Antinomie der Vernunft«, what the map reads as a text, in no passage in the map, named by Kant's own words only and not located; the ground is kept inside the claim, not made a claim of its own and not formalized, and no `depends_on` is set, since what the sentence draws on is not in the map. »hiebei«, "here", points, as the map reads it, to the law the paragraph before states, that everything that happens has a cause whose causality has its cause among the appearances and that all events are empirically determined in an order of nature ([c-kant-the-law-of-nature-is-a-law-of-the-understanding-from-which-it-is-permitted-under-no-pretext-to-except-any-appearance](../../c/kant-the-law-of-nature-is-a-law-of-the-understanding-from-which-it-is-permitted-under-no-pretext-to-except-any-appearance/)), which is the chain of causes this sentence says the matter looks like; the identification is the map's, and the two sentences are joined by no connective, so no link is set. »sie« in »wenn sie ... aufs Unbedingte ausgeht« is read as reason, the noun nearest it, with the antinomy the other candidate, said on the passage; »diese Bedenklichkeit« is the scruple the concessive clause states, that the chain of causes permits no absolute totality in the regress to their conditions. The scruple's words are those of A 533's sentence on the spontaneity, where under the law that everything that happens has a cause »keine absolute Totalität der Bedingungen im Causalverhältnisse heraus zu bekommen ist«, the ground on which reason creates for itself the idea of a spontaneity ([c-kant-reason-creates-the-idea-of-a-spontaneity-that-could-begin-to-act-of-itself](../../c/kant-reason-creates-the-idea-of-a-spontaneity-that-could-begin-to-act-of-itself/)): that sentence states, inside a »Da« clause, that no absolute totality is to be obtained, and this one that the look of a chain permitting none is a scruple already removed; the two say different things of the same totality, the one that it is not to be had and the other that its not being had does not hold us up, and no connective joins them, so no link is set, and that the two totalities are one is the map's reading, said here. No other Kant statement in the map names the antinomy, a regress, a chain of causes or the unconditioned: »Antinomie«, »Regressus«, »Unbedingte« and »Kette von Ursachen« stand in no other passage, »Kette« only in JGB 19's »Kette von irrthümlichen Schlüssen« ([c-nietzsche-belief-will-and-action-one-an-erroneous-inference](../../c/nietzsche-belief-will-and-action-one-an-erroneous-inference/)), a chain of inferences and not of causes, and »Totalität« in A 533's sentence named above and in the heading of section III, quoted on the A 532–537 passages; the resolution's sentences the map holds, on the two kinds of causality, the two senses of freedom and the two characters, say what freedom is and what the causality of a subject of the sense world would be, not what has been settled of the regress. No Schopenhauer statement in the map names an antinomy or a regress to conditions; § 55's »Dies leidet keine Ausnahme: es folgt aus der unbeschränkten Gültigkeit des Satzes vom Grunde«, in [its passage](../../p/wwv1-s55-will-as-such-free-everything-as-appearance-necessary/) and in no claim, asserts of the principle of sufficient reason an unrestricted validity, where this sentence speaks of the totality a chain of causes permits, and the two are not one proposition; no premise is set. No Nietzsche statement in the map speaks of a regress or a totality of conditions; GD 8's »dass die Art des Seins nicht auf eine causa prima zurückgeführt werden darf« ([c-nietzsche-nobody-responsible-the-great-liberation](../../c/nietzsche-nobody-responsible-the-great-liberation/)) denies a tracing back to a first cause, the end of a regress in other words, and denies nothing this sentence asserts, which is that a scruple about totality has been removed; JGB 21's »causa sui« ([c-nietzsche-causa-sui-best-self-contradiction](../../c/nietzsche-causa-sui-best-self-contradiction/)) names a cause of itself and not a chain of causes; no link is set. The sentence names neither the will, nor freedom, nor the thing in itself, and is set in no row of either comparison; the comparison on the will names it under *On freedom* as the first sentence of the paragraph that states the subsection's question.
