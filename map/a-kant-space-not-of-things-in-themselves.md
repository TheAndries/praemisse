The argument is the one sentence of reason given for conclusion (a) at A 26 / B 42: space
is no determination of things in themselves, "for neither absolute nor relative
determinations can be intuited prior to the existence of the things to which they belong,
hence not a priori" (AA 3:55.06–08). The first premise, that an a priori intuition of
space underlies all concepts of it, is the conclusion of the third point of the
metaphysical exposition (A 25 / B 39–40, AA 3:53.14–16).

What the form captures and what it leaves out: premise 2 is rendered as "if space were a
determination of things in themselves, it would not be intuited a priori", which is the
contrapositive use Kant makes of the general sentence at AA 3:55.06–08; the equation of
"before the existence of the things" with "a priori" is Kant's "mithin" in that sentence
and belongs to premise 2, not to premise 1. The form does not represent the generality of
that sentence (every determination of things), only its application to space.
