§ 21, ZA I 153–154: »Erscheinung heißt Vorstellung, und weiter nichts: alle Vorstellung, welcher Art sie auch sei, alles Objekt, ist Erscheinung.« The sentence sets »Erscheinung« equal to »Vorstellung«; the next sentence, »Ding an sich aber ist allein der Wille«, is [c-schopenhauer-will-alone-is-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-will-alone-is-thing-in-itself/), and the »aber« contrasts the two, it does not infer the one from the other. Since 2026-10-28 the claim shares a premise with Kant's A 536–537 / B 564–565, where appearances count for what they »in der That sind, nämlich nicht für Dinge an sich, sondern bloße Vorstellungen« ([c-kant-appearances-mere-representations-must-have-grounds-that-are-not-appearances](../../c/kant-appearances-mere-representations-must-have-grounds-that-are-not-appearances/)): that an appearance is a representation and nothing more, § 21's »weiter nichts« beside Kant's »bloße«; the entry is on the comparison on the thing in itself, which says what no passage in the map says, how Schopenhauer's »Vorstellung« stands to Kant's, and that the sharing rests on the two statements. The sentence's converse, that all representation is appearance, is § 21's alone; no Kant statement in the map asserts it.
