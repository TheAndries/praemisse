The second sentence of the section (AA 3:366.09–16). The statement is conditional because the
sentence is, »Wenn demnach ... so kann man«: Kant does not here assert that any appearance
has such a faculty, only what one can do if one has. What one can do is »betrachten«, consider,
the causality of »dieses Wesens« from two sides, and the two sides are given each with its
respect: »nach ihrer Handlung als eines Dinges an sich selbst«, and »nach den Wirkungen
derselben als einer Erscheinung in der Sinnenwelt«. The pronoun »derselben« is feminine and
can bind to »Handlung« or to »Causalität«; the translation takes »Handlung«, the nearer, and
nothing in the statement turns on the choice, since on either the effects are those of an
appearance in the world of sense. The faculty is »kein Gegenstand der sinnlichen Anschauung«
and is had by the appearance »an sich selbst«; what it is the sentence does not say, and the
word »Wille« does not occur in the section. The third sentence of the passage, in the
subjunctive, adds that we would form of the faculty of such a subject »einen empirischen,
imgleichen auch einen intellectuellen Begriff seiner Causalität«, which occur together in one
and the same effect; the map holds it in the passage and has not made it a claim. Set beside
B xxvii–xxviii, where »eben derselbe Wille in der Erscheinung (den sichtbaren Handlungen)«
is thought as not free and »als einem Dinge an sich selbst angehörig« as free
([c-kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself](../../c/kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself/)),
this sentence gives the causality of a being the same two sides, one »als eines Dinges an
sich selbst«, one as an appearance; that the being of this sentence and the will of the
preface are one subject is the map's reading, said on the comparison on the will, and no
passage in the map says it.
