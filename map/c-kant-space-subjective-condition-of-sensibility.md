Conclusion (b) from the exposition of space, A 26 / B 42, first paragraph (AA 3:55.09–18).
The first sentence is the statement's first half (3:55.09–11). The second half keeps the
sentence's modal form: Kant says that it »läßt sich verstehen, wie« the form of all
appearances »a priori im Gemüthe gegeben sein könne« (3:55.13–15), an explanation of how
it can be so given, resting on the receptivity of the subject preceding all intuitions of
objects (3:55.11–13); the claim does not turn that into a flat assertion that the form is
given a priori, which the metaphysical exposition asserts separately
([c-kant-space-a-priori](../../c/kant-space-a-priori/), A 24 / B 38). What is subjective
here is a condition of sensibility, not, in Kant's words, of the intellect; Schopenhauer's
Anhang sentence that shares the premise says »Form unsers Intellekts«
([c-schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective/)),
and the comparison's shared premise is worded on the part both assert, that space is a
subjective form. Space as no determination of things in themselves is
conclusion (a), [c-kant-space-not-of-things-in-themselves](../../c/kant-space-not-of-things-in-themselves/).
