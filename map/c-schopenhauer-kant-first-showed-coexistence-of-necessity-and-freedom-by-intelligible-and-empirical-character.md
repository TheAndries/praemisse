The first sentence of the passage, the one sentence of § 55 in the map that names Kant. The statement drops the first half, that Priestley set forth the necessity of individual action »sehr genügend«, and Kant's »dessen Verdienst hier besonders groß ist«, both in the passage and here; »ich« is "Schopenhauer"; »aber« joining the two halves is dropped with the first; the second »aber«, »letzterer aber«, "but" in the passage, is "and" in the statement. The sentence carries the mark of footnote 80, whose text the passage gives: the pages Kant is credited with are A 532–558 / B 560–586 of the first Critique, within which the map holds A 538–541 / B 566–569, and pages of the second Critique, not in the map. What Schopenhauer says he retains »ganz und gar« is the distinction, and his definitions of its two terms are his own: the intelligible character »der Wille als Ding an sich, sofern er in einem bestimmten Individuo, in bestimmtem Grade erscheint«, the empirical »diese Erscheinung selbst«; Kant's sentence calls the empirical character, its »ersteren«, the character of such a thing in the appearance, and the intelligible, its »zweiten«, »den Charakter des Dinges an sich selbst« ([c-kant-intelligible-character-cause-of-actions-as-appearances](../../c/kant-intelligible-character-cause-of-actions-as-appearances/)), under »würde«, of »einem Subjecte der Sinnenwelt« with a faculty of being the cause of appearances, and does not say that the intelligible character is the will. The sentence says Kant showed, »nachgewiesen«, the coexistence; the map holds Kant's sentence that freedom and nature would be found at the same actions ([c-kant-freedom-and-nature-together-in-the-same-actions](../../c/kant-freedom-and-nature-together-in-the-same-actions/)), under »würde«, and sets no link, the convention requiring that both statements assert one proposition, and Schopenhauer's sentence asserting what Kant did and what the two characters are, not the coexistence in Kant's words; the comparison on the will says what is parallel. Charakter«; Nietzsche's JGB 36 has the world »auf ihren "intelligiblen Charakter" hin bestimmt und bezeichnet« ([c-nietzsche-world-would-be-will-to-power](../../c/nietzsche-world-would-be-will-to-power/)), the term in his quotation marks, under »Gesetzt« and of the world, not of a man; it names no thinker, asserts nothing of what the character is, and neither states nor denies this sentence; no link.
