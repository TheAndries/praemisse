The second sentence of the passage. Every quotation mark is Nietzsche's, »"An-sich"« among
them: the sentence speaks of the in-itself as a term set off, and the statement keeps the marks
so that a reader does not take him to assert an in-itself; JGB 16 calls »Ding an sich« a
contradictio in adjecto ([c-nietzsche-immediate-certainty-contradictio-in-adjecto](../../c/nietzsche-immediate-certainty-contradictio-in-adjecto/)). Kant
at B xxvii has things in the second meaning, as things in themselves, not subject to the
principle of causality, if the deduction is right
([c-kant-causality-applies-only-to-things-as-objects-of-experience](../../c/kant-causality-applies-only-to-things-as-objects-of-experience/)), and Schopenhauer's
will as thing in itself is free of the forms whose common expression is the principle of
sufficient reason ([c-schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance](../../c/schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance/), § 23). The three
sentences come close, and the map sets no `shares_premise_with` link: Kant's clause stands under
»wenn die Deduction ... richtig ist«, and Nietzsche's »An-sich« is a term in his own quotation
marks of which the next sentence says that we invent what we then read into things
([c-nietzsche-projecting-sign-world-as-in-itself-is-mythology](../../c/nietzsche-projecting-sign-world-as-in-itself-is-mythology/)); whether the three assert
one proposition is said and left open on [x-thing-in-itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/), where a reader may dispute
the absence of the link.
