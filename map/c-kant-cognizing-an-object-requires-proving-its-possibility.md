The first sentence of Kant's footnote at B xxvi
([the note](../../p/krv-b-xxvi-footnote/)), whose mark stands after »denken können« in the
sentence that reserves that we must at least be able to think the same objects also as
things in themselves
([c-kant-things-in-themselves-thinkable-not-knowable](../../c/kant-things-in-themselves-thinkable-not-knowable/)).
The statement is the sentence whole; its parenthesis, the two ways of proving possibility,
is kept as a "whether ... or" clause, and »könne«, in the subjunctive of the »daß« clause,
is "be able to". The sentence names no object and says nothing of the thing in itself;
that it bears on the thing in itself is read from the place of the mark, and the map says
so on the comparison page where it is set. No link is set: the sentence states a
requirement and infers nothing, and the next sentence is joined to it by »Aber«, a
contrast; no Schopenhauer or Nietzsche statement in the map says what is required to
cognize an object, so no shared premise and no attack link is set, and a reader may
dispute the absence.
