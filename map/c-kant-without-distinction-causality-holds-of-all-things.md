The first sentence of the case of the will at B xxvii (AA 3:17.07–12). It is a
counterfactual, »wollen wir annehmen ... wäre gar nicht gemacht, so müßte ... gelten«, and
the statement keeps it as one: Kant does not say that causality holds of all things, but
what would follow were the distinction not made. The distinction is the one B xxvi
calls proved in the analytical part, that we cognize objects only as appearances
([c-kant-objects-known-only-as-appearances](../../c/kant-objects-known-only-as-appearances/),
3:16.34–37), here named »durch unsere Kritik nothwendig gemachte«. What follows from
the counterfactual in the next sentence is drawn by »also«
([c-kant-without-distinction-free-will-is-a-contradiction](../../c/kant-without-distinction-free-will-is-a-contradiction/)).
