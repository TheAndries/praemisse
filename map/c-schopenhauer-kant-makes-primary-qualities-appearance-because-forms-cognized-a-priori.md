The sixth sentence of the appendix's opening paragraph, which closes the comparison with
Locke: Locke had taken sound, colour, hardness and the like from the thing in itself and left
it extension, shape, solidity, number and mobility; the Kantian distinction, »Diese« in the
sentence, takes those too, »und zwar gerade deshalb, weil Bedingungen desselben, Raum, Zeit
und Kausalität, von uns a priori erkannt werden«. The subject of the sentence is the Kantian
distinction, so what it declares is Kant's doctrine as Schopenhauer states it; the »weil«
clause, in the indicative and outside the report, is Schopenhauer's own ground, so both
statements assert that space is cognized a priori, the convention's first branch for a shared
premise. The second »desselben«, in »Bedingungen desselben«, is not »der Erscheinung«, which is
feminine, but either »des Dinges an sich« or »unserm Auffassungsvermögen«; the sense is the
conditions of the appearance in the faculty, and the statement's »its« is to be read so, not
as conditions of the thing in itself, of which nothing is cognized a priori. Grisebach's text
reads »die Bedingungen desselben«, said on the passage. The link `shares_premise_with` is set to [c-kant-space-a-priori](../../c/kant-space-a-priori/), Kant's A 24 / B 38, space
»eine nothwendige Vorstellung a priori«, and the comparison [x-thing-in-itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) names
the pair in a second entry under the premise that space is given a priori, the first being
this appendix's own sentence »der Raum ... ist eine a priori gegebene, folglich subjektive
Form unsers Intellekts« ([c-schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective/)).
The statement asserts more than the premise: that the ground covers time and causality too,
and that Locke's primary qualities belong to the appearance; Kant's statement says nothing of
Locke. »Auffassungsvermögen« is rendered »faculty of apprehension«.
