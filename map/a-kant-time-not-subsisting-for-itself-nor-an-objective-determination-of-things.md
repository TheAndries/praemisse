The argument is conclusion (a) of the Transcendental Aesthetic's section on time with the
grounds the sentence itself gives, [KrV A 32–33 / B 49](../../p/krv-a32-33-b49-time-not-subsisting-for-itself-nor-an-objective-determination-of-things/) (AA 3:59.19–26), the
parallel of the map's argument for space from A 26 / B 42
([a-kant-space-not-of-things-in-themselves](../../a/kant-space-not-of-things-in-themselves/)), and, as that one, a modus tollens on the
limb about things with a premise from the exposition; A 26 / B 42's sentence has no limb on space
subsisting for itself, and its one ground, »weder absolute, noch relative Bestimmungen«, covers both
its limbs, property and relation; this sentence's first limb, time subsisting for itself, has its own
ground, which the form carries. The conclusion is the
sentence's main clause, »Die Zeit ist nicht etwas, was für sich selbst bestände, oder den Dingen
als objective Bestimmung anhinge, mithin übrig bliebe, wenn man von allen subjectiven Bedingungen
der Anschauung derselben abstrahirt«, `not f and not o and not m`, the claim's statement word for word, the
»mithin« clause, "and so would remain ...", the atom `m`, which the sentence draws from either limb by
its »mithin« and which the form carries as premise 6, the map's reading of that connective as a step
from the two denials together, `(not f and not o) -> not m`; the atoms `o` and `f` are the suppositions
of premises 3 and 4 word for word, without the clause. Premise 1 is the second and third points of § 4
([KrV A 31 / B 46–47](../../p/krv-a31-b46-47-time-a-necessary-representation-given-a-priori/)), the atom `t` the claim's statement, time a necessary
representation underlying all intuitions, given a priori, the general condition of the
possibility of appearances, whose principles instruct us before experience; the statement's "the
apodictic principles of the relations of time" resolves the tenth sentence's »Diese Grundsätze« by
the sixth sentence's words, in no claim, the map's resolution, said on the claim. Premise 2 is the
map's bridge from that statement to the words of conclusion (a)'s second sentence, `t -> p`:
that time, so given, precedes the objects as their condition and is cognized and intuited a
priori through synthetic propositions. It identifies § 4's »Erscheinungen« with the second
sentence's »Gegenstände«, § 4's »Bedingung ihrer Möglichkeit« and »a priori gegeben« with »vor den
Gegenständen als ihre Bedingung vorhergehen« and »a priori ... erkannt und angeschaut«, and § 4's
apodictic »Grundsätze« of the relations of time, which »belehren uns vor derselben«, with the
»synthetische Sätze« through which time is cognized a priori, none of which the text makes in
words in any passage in the map: that these principles are synthetic is said at § 4's fourth point
(3:58.20–21, the sentence that the proposition is synthetic) and in § 5 (3:59.15), both in no passage, named here and not
relied on. The bridge is the map's, one direction only, and a reader may dispute it. Premise 3 is
the second sentence of conclusion (a), `o -> not p` ([c-kant-time-as-a-determination-attaching-to-things-themselves-could-not-precede-the-objects-and-be-cognized-a-priori](../../c/kant-time-as-a-determination-attaching-to-things-themselves-could-not-precede-the-objects-and-be-cognized-a-priori/)): as a determination or order
attaching to the things themselves, time could not precede the objects as their condition and be
cognized and intuited a priori through synthetic propositions; the atom `o` is the supposition
the sentence's »als eine den Dingen selbst anhängende Bestimmung oder Ordnung« makes, the
conclusion's second limb without the »mithin« clause, and its »könnte« is flattened to the plain "does not" of `not p`,
the flattening the map's and named here. Premise 4 is the first sentence's »denn« clause,
`f -> r` ([c-kant-time-subsisting-for-itself-would-be-actual-without-an-actual-object](../../c/kant-time-subsisting-for-itself-would-be-actual-without-an-actual-object/)): were time something that subsisted for itself, it would be something that,
without an actual object, would nevertheless be actual, its »würde« and »wäre« in the atom.
Premise 5 is the
map's, `not r`: time is not something that, without an actual object, would nevertheless be actual, the
instance for time of the general premise that nothing is actual without an actual object, the denial
of the consequence that the sentence treats as absurd and does not state, the premise that a consequence
is unacceptable being the map's, as the bridges of the map's earlier Kant arguments are (B xxix–xxx's, A 538's, B 308–309's). Premise 6 is the sentence's own »mithin«, `(not f and not o) -> not m`: that
time, being neither, is not something that would remain if one abstracts from all subjective
conditions of the intuition of things; the text draws the remaining from either limb, and the map
reads the connective as one step from both denials, named here as the map's reading of a connective
and not a bridge of its own.

The derivation is one step of modus ponens, two of modus tollens and a last of modus ponens: from premises 1 and 2,
time precedes the objects as their condition and is cognized a priori through synthetic
propositions, `p`; from premise 3, then, time is not a determination or order attaching to the
things themselves, `not o`; from premises 4 and 5, time is not something that subsists for itself,
`not f`; from the two and premise 6, not something that would remain, `not m`; together the conclusion. No premise is idle. The rival form reads the first limb as
asserted on the ground's authority alone, `f -> r` and `not r` dropped and `not f` taken as a
premise, a one-ground form like the space argument's, whose sentence has one ground for its
whole; the map prefers to carry the ground the text gives,
and a reader who holds that the honest form is the second limb alone may dispute it.

The truth table says only that `not f and not o` holds in every assignment that satisfies the
five premises. It does not say that time is given a priori, that a determination attaching to
things could not be cognized a priori, that the two bridges are faithful, or what the things are
to which time would attach; those are the premises and matters of fidelity, and whether those
things are the things in themselves of the two readings of Kant ([two-aspect](../../r/kant-two-aspect/),
[two-object](../../r/kant-two-object/)) the form does not need to settle, and the map does not
pick. The conclusion's sharing with Schopenhauer's §§ 23–24 is set on the conclusion claim and
said on the [comparison on the thing in itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/).
