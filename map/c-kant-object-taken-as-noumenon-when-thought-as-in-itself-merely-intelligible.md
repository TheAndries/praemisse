The fifth sentence of the passage (AA 3:213.13–17; A 257 / B 313), one sentence by the map's
count, its semicolon joining two halves. Three things stand in it: an assertion, that in regard
to appearances understanding and reason »läßt sich allerdings ... brauchen«; a question, kept as a question, whether they still have »einigen Gebrauch« when the object is not appearance, which the sentence after it reports as already answered in the negative
([c-kant-question-of-a-transcendental-use-of-the-understanding-reaching-the-noumenon-answered-in-the-negative](../../c/kant-question-of-a-transcendental-use-of-the-understanding-reaching-the-noumenon-answered-in-the-negative/));
and an assertion of the sense in which the object is then taken, »in diesem Sinne nimmt man ihn,
wenn er an sich als bloß intelligibel, d. i. dem Verstande allein und gar nicht den Sinnen
gegeben, gedacht wird«. The statement keeps all three and the text's order. »allerdings« is
"certainly", as at B 311; »es frägt sich« "the question is"; »diese« understanding and reason,
and »ihn« the object of the clause before, »der Gegenstand«, and »in diesem Sinne« the sense the clause before names, that of an object that is not appearance, »(Noumenon)«, so that the title says "as noumenon", the resolutions the map's, said on the passage; »auch noch einigen Gebrauch« "still ... any use", the »auch« absorbed into "still", named on the passage; »(Noumenon)« kept in its parentheses, the text's own gloss on the object that is not
appearance; »an sich als bloß intelligibel« "as in itself merely intelligible"; »gedacht wird« "is
thought", the mode kept, since the sentence says how the object is taken when it is thought so
and does not say that it is given so.

The sentence names the noumenon and the object »an sich« and says what divides the object
taken as noumenon from the object as appearance: being thought as in itself merely intelligible,
given to the understanding alone and not at all to the senses. The paragraph before it, at
B 311, admits the division of concepts into sensible and intellectual and refuses the division
of objects into phenomena and noumena in a positive sense
([c-kant-division-into-phenomena-and-noumena-not-admissible-in-a-positive-sense](../../c/kant-division-into-phenomena-and-noumena-not-admissible-in-a-positive-sense/));
this sentence says in what sense the object is taken as noumenon when the question of a use
beyond appearances is put, and whether that sense is the positive one B 311 refuses the two
sentences do not say, joined by no connective: no link. B 307's noumenon in the negative sense,
a thing »so fern es nicht Object unserer sinnlichen Anschauung ist«
([c-kant-noumenon-negative-only](../../c/kant-noumenon-negative-only/)), says of the thing
what this sentence says of the object taken as noumenon, not given to the senses, and adds the
positive sense; that the two places describe one noumenon is the map's reading, with no link. Nearer still in their terms, B 310's two sentences in the row on what can be said, the concept of a noumenon, »eines Dinges, welches gar nicht als Gegenstand der Sinne, sondern als ein Ding an sich selbst (lediglich durch einen reinen Verstand) gedacht werden soll« ([c-kant-concept-of-a-noumenon-not-contradictory](../../c/kant-concept-of-a-noumenon-not-contradictory/)), and the possibility of such noumena not to be seen ([c-kant-possibility-of-noumena-not-to-be-seen-range-outside-appearances-empty-for-us](../../c/kant-possibility-of-noumena-not-to-be-seen-range-outside-appearances-empty-for-us/)), describe the noumenon as this sentence's last clause does, thought by the pure understanding alone and not as an object of the senses; that the three places describe one object is the map's identification, drawn by no connective, and no link is set. The word »intelligibel« stands in the map's Kant passages also at A 538 / B 566, »Ich nenne
dasjenige an einem Gegenstande der Sinne, was selbst nicht Erscheinung ist, intelligibel«
([c-kant-intelligible-is-what-is-not-itself-appearance](../../c/kant-intelligible-is-what-is-not-itself-appearance/)),
of something in an object of the senses, where this sentence has the object itself thought as in
itself merely intelligible, and in the footnote at B 312, of objects by the kind of intuition
([c-kant-objects-of-one-or-the-other-kind-of-intuition-must-be-called-intelligible-or-sensible](../../c/kant-objects-of-one-or-the-other-kind-of-intuition-must-be-called-intelligible-or-sensible/));
the map does not say how the three uses relate, and sets no link. Of the other thinkers,
Schopenhauer's § 19, »Außer dem Willen und der Vorstellung ist uns gar nichts bekannt, noch
denkbar« ([c-schopenhauer-nothing-known-but-will-and-representation](../../c/schopenhauer-nothing-known-but-will-and-representation/)),
and § 25, that plurality is thinkable only in time and space
([c-schopenhauer-plurality-conditioned-by-time-and-space-not-of-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-plurality-conditioned-by-time-and-space-not-of-thing-in-itself/)),
say what is thinkable, where this sentence says how an object is taken when it is thought in a
certain way and does not say that it is or is not thinkable, so that neither a premise nor an
attack is set; § 22's will, »ein durchaus unmittelbar Erkanntes«
([c-schopenhauer-will-not-inferred-but-immediately-known](../../c/schopenhauer-will-not-inferred-but-immediately-known/)),
is not said in any passage in the map to be given to the understanding alone or to be an object
in this sentence's sense, so that the two stand as the page's divergence on immediacy and not as
an attack; GD »Die ›Vernunft‹ in der Philosophie« 6's fourth proposition, that dividing the world
into a "true" and an "apparent" one is a suggestion of décadence
([c-nietzsche-dividing-world-true-and-apparent-is-decadence](../../c/nietzsche-dividing-world-true-and-apparent-is-decadence/)),
in the same row, condemns a division of the world, where this sentence names the sense in which
one object is taken and divides no world, and whether the one pair is the other no passage in the
map says; the two do not deny each other in their words, and neither a premise nor an attack is
set. Each absence is open to dispute.
