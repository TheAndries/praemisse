The second sentence of [KrV A 34–35 / B 51](../../p/krv-a34-35-b51-time-nothing-when-the-objects-are-taken-as-they-may-be-in-themselves/), the paragraph of § 6 after its three
conclusions (AA 3:60.31–36), kept whole, its semicolon joining two halves: that time is of objective
validity only in regard to appearances, with its »weil« clause, »weil dieses schon Dinge sind, die
wir als Gegenstände unsrer Sinne annehmen«, the neuter »dieses« read as the appearances, and that
it is no longer objective when one abstracts from the sensibility of our intuition and speaks of
»Dingen überhaupt«, "things in general". The statement keeps the »nur«, the »schon«, "already",
the »nicht mehr«, "no longer", the »mithin«, "hence", and the »wenn« of the second half, whose
antecedent, an abstraction one makes, is not asserted; the first half is indicative and asserted.
The sentence's contrast is between appearances and things in general, the things of a concept
that abstracts from every kind of intuition of them, as the paragraph's fifth sentence, in the
passage and in no claim, says in so many words; no passage in the map says whether Kant's
»Dinge überhaupt« are things in themselves, and the identification would be the map's, so the
claim is set in no row of the [comparison on the thing in itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/), which has rows for the thing in itself and for what divides it
from appearance and none for things in general; it is named there under *What the three say*. The
third sentence of the paragraph, »Die Zeit ist also lediglich eine subjective Bedingung unserer
(menschlichen) Anschauung ... und an sich, außer dem Subjecte, nichts« ([c-kant-time-merely-a-subjective-condition-of-our-intuition-and-in-itself-apart-from-the-subject-nothing](../../c/kant-time-merely-a-subjective-condition-of-our-intuition-and-in-itself-apart-from-the-subject-nothing/)), is drawn from
this sentence and the one before it by »also«, set as a link on that claim. A 42 / B 59, that what
objects may be in themselves, apart from all the receptivity of our sensibility, »bleibt uns
gänzlich unbekannt« ([c-kant-things-in-themselves-unknown](../../c/kant-things-in-themselves-unknown/)), abstracts in the same words, »aller dieser Receptivität unserer
Sinnlichkeit«, from what this sentence abstracts from, »der Sinnlichkeit unsrer Anschauung«, and
says what then remains unknown, not what time then is; no connective joins the two and no link
is set. No Schopenhauer or Nietzsche statement in the map asserts or denies that time is
objectively valid only in regard to appearances: § 24's forms in consciousness »ganz unabhängig
von den Objekten« ([c-schopenhauer-forms-in-consciousness-independent-of-objects](../../c/schopenhauer-forms-in-consciousness-independent-of-objects/)) and § 25's forms »nur unsere Erkenntnißform« ([c-schopenhauer-plurality-conditioned-by-time-and-space-not-of-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-plurality-conditioned-by-time-and-space-not-of-thing-in-itself/)) say
where the forms are and what they are, not of what they hold, and JGB 34's conditional on whoever
takes the world »sammt Raum, Zeit ... als falsch erschlossen« ([c-nietzsche-whoever-blames-thinking-for-the-worlds-falseness-should-distrust-thinking](../../c/nietzsche-whoever-blames-thinking-for-the-worlds-falseness-should-distrust-thinking/)) asserts nothing of
time; no link is set, said on each.
