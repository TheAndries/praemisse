The third sentence of [KrV A 34–35 / B 51](../../p/krv-a34-35-b51-time-nothing-when-the-objects-are-taken-as-they-may-be-in-themselves/), the paragraph of § 6 after its three
conclusions (AA 3:60.36–61.02), kept whole but for its »also«, "therefore", dropped from the
statement and carried by the links: the text draws the sentence from the two before it by that
connective (3:60.36), from the first, that if we take the objects as they may be in themselves time
is nothing ([c-kant-taking-the-objects-as-they-may-be-in-themselves-time-is-nothing](../../c/kant-taking-the-objects-as-they-may-be-in-themselves-time-is-nothing/)), which the sentence's »an sich, außer dem Subjecte, nichts« restates outright,
and from the second, that time is objectively valid only in regard to appearances and no longer
objective when one abstracts from the sensibility of our intuition ([c-kant-time-objectively-valid-only-in-regard-to-appearances-not-objective-of-things-in-general](../../c/kant-time-objectively-valid-only-in-regard-to-appearances-not-objective-of-things-in-general/)), which its »lediglich
eine subjective Bedingung unserer (menschlichen) Anschauung« restates; that the »also« reaches back
to both is the map's reading, said here, and a reader who wants the step made an argument may ask.
The two parentheses are kept in their parentheses; »lediglich« is "merely"; »sofern« "insofar as";
»afficirt« "affected"; the sentence is indicative and asserted in Kant's own voice. It parallels
conclusion (b) on space, »Der Raum ist nichts anders, als nur die Form aller Erscheinungen äußerer
Sinne, d. i. die subjective Bedingung der Sinnlichkeit« ([c-kant-space-subjective-condition-of-sensibility](../../c/kant-space-subjective-condition-of-sensibility/)), in its first half,
»subjective Bedingung«, the word both use, and goes beyond it in its second, »an sich, außer dem
Subjecte, nichts«, which A 26 / B 42 says of space in no sentence in the map; the two are drawn from
no connective to each other and no link between Kant's claims is set. The second half says in
other words what conclusion (a) on time says, that time attaches to no thing as an objective
determination remaining when one abstracts from the subjective conditions of intuition
([c-kant-time-not-subsisting-for-itself-nor-an-objective-determination-attaching-to-things](../../c/kant-time-not-subsisting-for-itself-nor-an-objective-determination-attaching-to-things/)), and what A 539's »denn« clause says, that time is the condition of appearances and not
of things in themselves ([c-kant-intelligible-character-under-no-conditions-of-time](../../c/kant-intelligible-character-under-no-conditions-of-time/)); the premise that time is no determination of the thing in
itself is entered on the [comparison on the thing in itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) under conclusion (a), the sentence of the Aesthetic that denies the
determination in Schopenhauer's own word, »Bestimmung«, and this sentence is named beside it and
not entered, a reader may ask for a third entry. A 26 / B 42, are named here, and in the row on what is the subject's own, A 26 / B 42's conclusion
(b), named above. The Schopenhauer sentence nearest the first half, the Anhang's »der Raum ... ist
eine a priori gegebene, folglich subjektive Form unsers Intellekts« ([c-schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective/)), which shares

»mit gleichem Recht« as the object's qualities, a licence to name and not an assertion that time is
