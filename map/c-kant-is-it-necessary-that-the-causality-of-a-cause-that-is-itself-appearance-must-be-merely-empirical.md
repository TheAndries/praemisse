The first sentence of the passage (AA 3:369.24–26), a question, kept as a question in the statement in the form the map gave the questions of JGB 34 ([c-nietzsche-why-might-the-world-not-be-a-fiction](../../c/nietzsche-why-might-the-world-not-be-a-fiction/)), "Kant asks whether"; the statement drops the paragraph-opening »aber«, which the translation keeps as "But", and keeps »denn« as "then" and »auch« as "also", the particles of the question, with the reason on the passage. What the sentence asserts is nothing: it asks whether a necessity holds, the necessity that »die Causalität ihrer Ursache ... lediglich empirisch sein müsse« if the effects are appearances, and gives neither answer; that the question expects the answer no is a reading of its tone which the map does not write into the statement, and the second sentence of the passage, which asks whether the contrary is not rather possible ([c-kant-is-it-not-rather-possible-that-empirical-causality-itself-be-an-effect-of-a-non-empirical-but-intelligible-causality](../../c/kant-is-it-not-rather-possible-that-empirical-causality-itself-be-an-effect-of-a-non-empirical-but-intelligible-causality/)), is joined to it by »Und« and, in the 1781 edition, which sets the two as one sentence without a question mark, by a lower-case »und«, named on the passage; no link is set between the two, the connective being the conjunction of two questions and not a step. The cause asked about is one that »selbst auch Erscheinung ist«, Kant's parenthesis »(nämlich Ursache)« resolving his relative pronoun to the cause and not to the causality, both feminine, and the statement keeps the parenthesis. »lediglich empirisch« is "merely empirical"; what an empirical causality is the sentence does not say, and the paragraph before, in the map since 2026-11-06, says of the causes in appearance that none can begin a series of itself and that every action as appearance presupposes another state in which the cause is found ([c-kant-among-the-causes-in-appearance-nothing-can-begin-a-series-absolutely-and-of-itself](../../c/kant-among-the-causes-in-appearance-nothing-can-begin-a-series-absolutely-and-of-itself/), [c-kant-every-action-as-appearance-is-itself-an-event-and-everything-that-happens-only-a-continuation-of-the-series](../../c/kant-every-action-as-appearance-is-itself-an-event-and-everything-that-happens-only-a-continuation-of-the-series/)); that this question asks whether that is all the causality of such a cause can be is the map's reading of the two paragraphs, joined by no connective, and no link is set. Schopenhauer's § 23, that every appearance of the will is »durchaus dem Satz vom Grunde unterworfen« ([c-schopenhauer-will-groundless-and-one](../../c/schopenhauer-will-groundless-and-one/)), says of the will's appearances that they stand under the principle of sufficient reason, not of a cause's causality that it is merely empirical, so the two are not one proposition and nothing is set. The law of nature of A 542–543, that everything that happens has a cause whose causality has its cause among the appearances ([c-kant-the-law-of-nature-is-a-law-of-the-understanding-from-which-it-is-permitted-under-no-pretext-to-except-any-appearance](../../c/kant-the-law-of-nature-is-a-law-of-the-understanding-from-which-it-is-permitted-under-no-pretext-to-except-any-appearance/)), is asserted outright, and this sentence asks whether a cause's causality must be empirical only; a question asserts nothing a premise could share or a link could carry, and none is set. The sentence names neither the will nor freedom nor the thing in itself, and names no causality other than the empirical one it asks about. It is set on no row of [the comparison on the will](../../x/the-will/): the second and third sentences of the paragraph stand since 2026-11-10 in the Kant cell of the row on the will as cause for the matter the map reads the four sentences of A 538–541 set there as sharing, formulated on 2026-11-10 as the map's reading and not as a rule the cell carried before: a cause that belongs to the world of sense whose causality is named as other than merely empirical, intelligible, and this sentence, which asks whether a cause's causality must be merely empirical and names nothing else, is tested by that criterion and not set, as A 543–544's sentence that an original action is not to be expected from the causal connection of appearances ([c-kant-an-original-action-is-not-to-be-expected-from-the-causal-connection-of-appearances](../../c/kant-an-original-action-is-not-to-be-expected-from-the-causal-connection-of-appearances/)) is not; the row's note and the paragraph under *On the will as cause* say so, and the paragraph under *On freedom* names it with the other sentences of the explanation on no row. It is set in no row of the comparison on the thing in itself, since it names neither the thing in itself nor the division. No attack is set: a question denies nothing, and no Schopenhauer or Nietzsche statement in the map says that the causality of a cause that is appearance must be merely empirical; Schopenhauer's § 55, that every thing as appearance is thoroughly necessary ([c-schopenhauer-every-thing-as-appearance-necessary-in-itself-will-and-free](../../c/schopenhauer-every-thing-as-appearance-necessary-in-itself-will-and-free/)), says of every thing as appearance that it is necessary, not that its causality is merely empirical, and Nietzsche's JGB 21, that cause and effect are to be used as conventional fictions ([c-nietzsche-cause-and-effect-conventional-fictions](../../c/nietzsche-cause-and-effect-conventional-fictions/)), is a prescription on use, which denies nothing a question asks.
