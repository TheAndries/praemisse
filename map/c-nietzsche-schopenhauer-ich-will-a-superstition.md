JGB 16, first sentence: »Es giebt immer noch harmlose Selbst-Beobachter, welche glauben,
dass es "unmittelbare Gewissheiten" gebe, zum Beispiel "ich denke", oder, wie es der
Aberglaube Schopenhauer's war, "ich will"«. The sentence names Schopenhauer and no
passage. The map sets this claim as an attack on
[c-schopenhauer-will-not-inferred-but-immediately-known](../../c/schopenhauer-will-not-inferred-but-immediately-known/),
and the identification is the map's: what the sentence denies is immediacy, and the
words of the target it denies are »ein durchaus unmittelbar Erkanntes« (WWV I § 22, ZA I
155); it says nothing about the target's other half, that the will is not reached by
inference. § 22 speaks of knowing »was Wille sei«, the concept, and Nietzsche's »ich will«
is a first-person certainty of a present act; the nearer Schopenhauer sentence for the
act is § 18's »jenes Jedem unmittelbar Bekannte, welches das Wort Wille bezeichnet«
([p-wwv1-s18-body-given-in-two-ways](../../p/wwv1-s18-body-given-in-two-ways/)), and the
map cites it beside § 22. The next sentence,
[c-nietzsche-immediate-certainty-contradictio-in-adjecto](../../c/nietzsche-immediate-certainty-contradictio-in-adjecto/),
and the analysis of »ich denke« that follows,
[a-nietzsche-ich-denke-has-no-immediate-certainty](../../a/nietzsche-ich-denke-has-no-immediate-certainty/),
carry the denial out; the aphorism does not run the analysis for »ich will«, and JGB 19
does ([c-nietzsche-schopenhauer-took-over-popular-prejudice](../../c/nietzsche-schopenhauer-took-over-popular-prejudice/)).
