The second sentence of the passage (AA 3:210.18–21; B 308), asserted outright, a general principle
on how the possibility of a thing is proved. »Nun ... aber«, which opens the sentence (3:210.18) and adds its general principle to the first sentence's »denn« clause, the frame from which the third sentence's »also« draws, is dropped from the statement and named here; »niemals bloß aus« "never merely from"; »dem Nichtwidersprechen eines Begriffs desselben« "the
non-contradiction of a concept of it"; »diesen« is the concept, resolved by name in the statement,
the resolution the map's; »man« "one's"; »belegt« "supporting"; »bewiesen werden« "be proved". The
sentence names neither the thing in itself nor a noumenon and speaks of the possibility of a thing
in general.

possibility are in this sentence's terms the »Nichtwidersprechen eines Begriffs«, from which the
possibility of the thing is never proved; that the footnote's "something more" and this sentence's
corresponding intuition name one requirement is the map's reading, and the two places differ in
their words: this sentence says the possibility is proved »nur dadurch«, only by supporting the concept
with a corresponding intuition, where the footnote says of the more that »es kann auch in praktischen liegen«, in practical sources of cognition, the noun supplied from the clause before. How a proof only by intuition and a more that may also lie in practical
sources relate, no passage in the map says; the map does not reconcile them, and no connective joins
the preface to the chapter, so no link is set, the absence open to dispute. In the chapter, B 310's
