The first of the four stored sentences at ZA II 516; »Er« is Kant, named in the sentences
before, and the statement says so. The sentence reports what Kant showed, and »zeigte« asserts
what is shown: Schopenhauer puts the result forward as true, not as Kant's opinion. The laws
are identified in the next sentence as those »nach welchen die Erscheinungen an einander
geknüpft sind«, time, space, causality and inference, the principle of sufficient reason; the
statement keeps the first sentence's own words for them. The link `shares_premise_with` is set
to [c-kant-understanding-principles-only-for-experience](../../c/kant-understanding-principles-only-for-experience/), Kant's A 236 / B 295, where all that the understanding draws from itself serves
»zu keinem andern Behuf, als lediglich zum Erfahrungsgebrauch« and its principles are »nur das
reine Schema zur möglichen Erfahrung«; the comparison [x-thing-in-itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) names the pair
under the premise that the laws governing experience are not to be applied beyond it, and
says what the link rests on: Kant's subject is what the understanding draws from itself,
Schopenhauer's the laws that rule in experience, time, space, causality and inference, and
the identification of the two is a step the map takes, supported by the Kant claim's second
passage, Prolegomena § 57, where Kant says of »Zeit, Raum und alle Verstandesbegriffe« that
they have »keinen andern Gebrauch ..., als blos Erfahrung möglich zu machen« (4:350.27–33),
and by the paragraph's last sentence, where Kant showed those laws conditioned by the
subject's mode of cognition ([c-schopenhauer-kant-showed-laws-and-world-conditioned-by-subjects-mode-of-cognition](../../c/schopenhauer-kant-showed-laws-and-world-conditioned-by-subjects-mode-of-cognition/)). A reader who holds the two subjects distinct may
dispute the link. The statement differs from § 19's
([c-schopenhauer-causality-never-leads-beyond-representation](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-never-leads-beyond-representation/)), which is Schopenhauer's
own doctrine of the law of causality; this one is his report of Kant.
