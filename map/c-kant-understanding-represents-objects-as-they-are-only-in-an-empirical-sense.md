title: If we say that the senses represent objects as they appear and the understanding as they are, the latter is to be taken in an empirical, not a transcendental, sense

The first sentence of the passage (AA 3:213.22–29; A 258 / B 313–314), the opening of the
paragraph. The sentence is a conditional whose antecedent is a saying, »Wenn wir denn also
sagen«, and whose consequent prescribes how the saying's second half is to be taken, »ist ... zu
nehmen«; the statement keeps both the conditional and the prescription, and drops the sentence's
opening connectives »denn also«, which draw the sentence from the paragraph before it
(3:212.28–213.21, in no passage), so that it reads alone; the body names the drop here. The
sentence does not assert that the understanding represents objects as they are, and the
statement does not either. »Bedeutung« is "sense", as the map renders it at B 308–309 (»nur in
negativer Bedeutung«); »das letztere« "the latter", in English as in the German the second of
the two sayings; »Sinne überhaupt« "the senses in general"; »mithin« "hence". The sentence names
»Erscheinung« by the verb »erscheinen« and the thing in itself by no word of its own: what the
understanding would represent in the transcendental sense is »was sie außer der Beziehung auf
mögliche Erfahrung ... sein mögen«, and the sentence calls that »Gegenstände des reinen
Verstandes«, not »Dinge an sich«; the map does not supply the term.

The claim `depends_on` the sentence that follows it, that what objects may be as objects of the
pure understanding will always remain unknown to us
([c-kant-what-objects-may-be-as-objects-of-the-pure-understanding-remains-unknown](../../c/kant-what-objects-may-be-as-objects-of-the-pure-understanding-remains-unknown/)),
by that sentence's »Denn« (3:213.29), which gives the ground for taking the understanding's
»wie sie sind« in the empirical sense; the step is the text's, marked by the connective, and a
reader who wants it formalized as an argument may ask for it.

No other link is set. The sentence set in the same row on 2026-10-30, that the division of
objects into phenomena and noumena cannot be admitted »in positiver Bedeutung«
([c-kant-division-into-phenomena-and-noumena-not-admissible-in-a-positive-sense](../../c/kant-division-into-phenomena-and-noumena-not-admissible-in-a-positive-sense/)),
refuses a division in a positive sense where this sentence restricts a saying to an empirical
sense; whether the chapter's »positiver Bedeutung« at B 311 and its »transscendentaler ...
Bedeutung« at B 313 name one sense no connective says, the identification would be the map's,
and the two sentences are set in one row with no link between them. The preface's sentence at
B xxvii on »Unterscheidung der Dinge als Gegenstände der Erfahrung von eben denselben als Dingen an sich selbst« ([c-kant-without-distinction-causality-holds-of-all-things](../../c/kant-without-distinction-causality-holds-of-all-things/))
names a distinction this sentence does not name, and no connective joins the places. The
statement is a conditional on a saying, which in no passage in the map is asserted as Kant's own,
and its consequent is a prescription, »ist ... zu nehmen«, how the saying is to be taken; by the
map's convention a prescription shares a premise with nobody and is denied by nobody: GD »Die ›Vernunft‹ in der Philosophie« 2,
»Die "scheinbare" Welt ist die einzige« ([c-nietzsche-apparent-world-is-the-only-one](../../c/nietzsche-apparent-world-is-the-only-one/)),
and 6's fourth proposition, that dividing the world into a true and an apparent one is
décadence ([c-nietzsche-dividing-world-true-and-apparent-is-decadence](../../c/nietzsche-dividing-world-true-and-apparent-is-decadence/)),
the nearest Nietzsche statements, speak of a true and an apparent world and not of how a saying
about the senses and the understanding is to be taken, and deny nothing the statement asserts;
Schopenhauer's § 21, »Erscheinung heißt Vorstellung, und weiter nichts«
([c-schopenhauer-appearance-is-representation](../../c/schopenhauer-appearance-is-representation/)),
the nearest Schopenhauer statement, says what appearance is and nothing of the understanding's
representing objects as they are. Each absence is open to dispute.

Set on the [comparison on the thing in itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/), in the row on the division of the
world into appearance and thing in itself, since 2026-11-01, for what the sentence says the
distinction between how objects appear and how they are is and is not, the row's first question:
a distinction to be taken in an empirical sense, between objects as they must be represented in
the connection of appearances and objects as they appear, and not a transcendental one, between
appearances and what objects may be apart from all relation to the senses. It is not set in the
row on what can be said of the thing in itself, although it names »Gegenstände des reinen
Verstandes«, since it says how a saying about them is to be taken and not what can or cannot be
said of them, which its ground, the sentence after it, says.
