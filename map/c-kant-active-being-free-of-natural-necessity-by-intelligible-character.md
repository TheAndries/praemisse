title: By its intelligible character the same subject would have to be absolved from all determination through appearances, and, since as noumenon nothing happens in it, would be so far independent of all natural necessity of the world of sense, and free in its actions
thinker: t-kant
statement: "According to its intelligible character, of which we can have nothing but merely the general concept, a subject of the world of sense that has in itself a faculty by which it can be the cause of appearances would nevertheless have to be absolved from all influence of sensibility and determination through appearances; and since in it, insofar as it is noumenon, nothing happens, no change that requires dynamical determination in time, hence no connection with appearances as causes is found, this active being would so far be independent of all natural necessity, such as is found only in the world of sense, and free in its actions."

The first sentence of the section's fourth paragraph (AA 3:367.31–368.02). The two main
clauses are subjunctive, »würde ... freigesprochen werden müssen«, »würde ... frei sein«,
kept as »would«; the parenthesis »ob wir zwar davon nichts als bloß den allgemeinen Begriff
desselben haben können« and the ground »da in ihm, so fern es Noumenon ist, nichts geschieht
... angetroffen wird« are indicative and asserted, the statement keeping both. »frei« here is
»von aller Naturnothwendigkeit ... unabhängig und frei«, independence from natural necessity
in its actions, and the sentence restricts the necessity, »als die lediglich in der Sinnenwelt
angetroffen wird«, and the freedom, »so fern«; the statement keeps both restrictions. »dasselbe Subject« and »dieses thätige Wesen« are the acting subject of the paragraphs before, and the statement restates the supposition that says which subject, since the sentence opens with an anaphor. This is
the sentence of the section on the freedom of the subject, and the comparison on the will sets
it in the row on freedom beside B xxvii–xxviii's will thought »als einem Dinge an sich selbst
angehörig jenem nicht unterworfen, mithin als frei«
([c-kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself](../../c/kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself/)):
there the subject is the will, here »dieses thätige Wesen«, and that the two are one is the
map's reading, said on the page. The subject's other side is
[c-kant-actions-explicable-by-laws-of-nature-by-empirical-character](../../c/kant-actions-explicable-by-laws-of-nature-by-empirical-character/),
and the sentence that joins the two is
[c-kant-freedom-and-nature-together-in-the-same-actions](../../c/kant-freedom-and-nature-together-in-the-same-actions/).
»Noumenon« is the text's word; the map's other Kant sentences on it are B 307 ([c-kant-noumenon-negative-only](../../c/kant-noumenon-negative-only/)), since 2026-10-30 the two paragraphs at A 254–256 / B 310–312 ([c-kant-concept-of-a-noumenon-not-contradictory](../../c/kant-concept-of-a-noumenon-not-contradictory/) and the claims set with it) and, since 2026-11-01, the fourth sentence of the chapter's last paragraph at A 258–260 / B 314–315 ([p-krv-a258-260-b314-315-concept-of-purely-intelligible-objects-empty-of-all-principles-of-its-application](../../p/krv-a258-260-b314-315-concept-of-purely-intelligible-objects-empty-of-all-principles-of-its-application/)), where the word stands in a parenthesis after »Dingen an sich selbst«, in a passage and in no claim, and, since 2026-11-02, the fifth and sixth sentences of the chapter's paragraph at A 256–257 / B 312–313 ([c-kant-object-taken-as-noumenon-when-thought-as-in-itself-merely-intelligible](../../c/kant-object-taken-as-noumenon-when-thought-as-in-itself-merely-intelligible/) and [c-kant-question-of-a-transcendental-use-of-the-understanding-reaching-the-noumenon-answered-in-the-negative](../../c/kant-question-of-a-transcendental-use-of-the-understanding-reaching-the-noumenon-answered-in-the-negative/)); the map does not say how the uses relate. Since 2026-11-10 the map holds A 544 / B 572's explication of an intelligible causality as an original action of a cause that is in so far not appearance but intelligible ([c-kant-an-intelligible-causality-is-an-original-action-of-a-cause-that-is-in-so-far-not-appearance-but-intelligible](../../c/kant-an-intelligible-causality-is-an-original-action-of-a-cause-that-is-in-so-far-not-appearance-but-intelligible/)), set on the comparison on the will in the row on the will as cause; this sentence says under »würde« what the active being would be absolved from »Nach dem intelligibelen Charakter«, of a character and in the word »frei«; no connective joins the two, no link is set, and this sentence passes that row's criterion and is left in the row on freedom for its words, said on the page.
