The last sentence of the section's second paragraph (AA 3:367.15–20). The two main clauses
are subjunctive, »könnte zwar niemals«, »würde doch ... müssen«, kept as »could« and »would«;
the ground, »weil wir nichts wahrnehmen können, als so fern es erscheint«, and the
comparison, »so wie wir überhaupt einen transscendentalen Gegenstand den Erscheinungen in
Gedanken zum Grunde legen müssen, ob wir zwar von ihm, was er an sich selbst sei, nichts
wissen«, are indicative and asserted. »gekannt« is »known«, not »erkannt«, »cognized«; what could never be known immediately is said under »könnte«, and the ground, that perception reaches only what appears, is asserted. »Dieser intelligibele Charakter« is the one granted to the subject of the world of
sense
([c-kant-intelligible-character-cause-of-actions-as-appearances](../../c/kant-intelligible-character-cause-of-actions-as-appearances/));
the statement says whose it is, since the sentence opens with an anaphor. What is denied
immediate knowledge is the intelligible character, the law of the subject's causality as a
thing in itself; the sentence does not name the will, and the comparison on the will sets it
in the row on immediacy for the word »unmittelbar«, beside Schopenhauer's will as »ein
durchaus unmittelbar Erkanntes«
([c-schopenhauer-will-not-inferred-but-immediately-known](../../c/schopenhauer-will-not-inferred-but-immediately-known/)),
saying that whether Schopenhauer's will is such a character is the map's question and no
passage's. No attack link is set: Schopenhauer's statement asserts immediate cognition of the
will, Kant's has an intelligible character as what could never be known immediately, and neither statement
names what the other's does; a reader who holds that § 22 denies what this sentence states may
dispute the absence. The map's other Kant sentence on knowing from that side is B xxviii's
([c-kant-freedom-cannot-be-cognized-by-speculative-reason](../../c/kant-freedom-cannot-be-cognized-by-speculative-reason/)),
which speaks of cognizing by speculative reason and by empirical observation and not of
immediacy.
