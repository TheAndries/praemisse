JGB 16, second sentence: »Dass aber "unmittelbare Gewissheit", ebenso wie "absolute
Erkenntniss" und "Ding an sich", eine contradictio in adjecto in sich schliesst, werde ich
hundertmal wiederholen«. The sentence gives no ground for the contradiction; the aphorism
goes on to take »ich denke« apart ([a-nietzsche-ich-denke-has-no-immediate-certainty](../../a/nietzsche-ich-denke-has-no-immediate-certainty/)),
which is a ground for the first of the three cases and not for the other two. The map
sets this claim as an attack on Kant's
[c-kant-things-in-themselves-thinkable-not-knowable](../../c/kant-things-in-themselves-thinkable-not-knowable/),
which states that we must at least be able to think things in themselves: a concept that
includes a contradictio in adjecto cannot be consistently thought, so the statement
denies what the target states. No sentence of the aphorism names Kant or a passage of
his, and the sentence gives no ground; the link rests on the words »Ding an sich« alone,
and the map says so.

Since 2026-10-30 the same claim is set also as an attack on Kant's [c-kant-concept-of-a-noumenon-not-contradictory](../../c/kant-concept-of-a-noumenon-not-contradictory/), the sentence at A 254 / B 310 that the concept of a noumenon, a thing to be thought as a thing in itself, »ist gar nicht widersprechend«: there the target states in its own words the absence of the contradiction this sentence asserts, so the denial needs no step; as before, no sentence of the aphorism names Kant, and the link rests on the words »Ding an sich«.
