§ 24, first paragraph. The sentence is a conditional, »Sollten nun aber ... so müßten sie«, and what follows it is a question, »Demnach ließe sich wenigstens fragen«; the statement keeps the conditional and the »wenigstens«, and asserts neither that the objects have a meaning nor that there is a thing in itself. The paragraph's last sentence answers the question with the will, »unsere Antwort«, which § 21 states ([c-schopenhauer-will-alone-is-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-will-alone-is-thing-in-itself/)) and which the sentence here sets aside »für jetzt«. Set beside Kant's B xxvi ([c-kant-without-things-in-themselves-appearance-without-appearing](../../c/kant-without-things-in-themselves-appearance-without-appearing/)), the two conditionals differ: Kant's antecedent is our being unable to think the objects also as things in themselves, its consequent an absurdity, appearance without anything that appears, in the consequent; Schopenhauer's has a meaning in the antecedent and a thing in itself in the consequent, and the Anhang's charge against Kant's derivation ([c-schopenhauer-kant-grounds-thing-in-itself-on-causal-inference](../../c/schopenhauer-kant-grounds-thing-in-itself-on-causal-inference/)) is not raised against this sentence of his own, which names no inference. No link is set between the two claims, since neither states the other's conditional; the comparison [x-thing-in-itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) sets them side by side.
