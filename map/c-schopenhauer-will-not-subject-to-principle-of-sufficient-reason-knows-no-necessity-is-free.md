The fifth sentence of § 55, the answer to the question free of what: the will, as thing in itself, is not subject to »dem Satz vom Grunde, der Form alles Objekts«, hence not determined as consequent by a ground, hence knows no necessity, »d.h. frei ist«, the two »also« inside the one sentence and not formalized. The statement drops the opening »Andererseits nun aber« and »diese nämliche«, which set the sentence against the preceding ones on the necessity of everything that appears (in the passage and in no claim), and keeps »uns«, "for us". The same thinker says at § 23 that the will as thing in itself is »frei von allen Formen« of its appearance ([c-schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance](../../c/schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance/)) and »schlechthin grundlos« ([c-schopenhauer-will-groundless-and-one](../../c/schopenhauer-will-groundless-and-one/)); this sentence adds the words »keine Nothwendigkeit kennt« and the identification of freedom with that, from which the next sentence draws the concept of freedom ([c-schopenhauer-concept-of-freedom-negative](../../c/schopenhauer-concept-of-freedom-negative/)). No link to Kant is set. The nearest Kant statements in the map are B xxvii–xxviii, where the very same will is thought as not free in the appearance and as free »als einem Dinge an sich selbst angehörig« ([c-kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself](../../c/kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself/)), and the resolution of the third antinomy, where the active being would by its intelligible character be »von aller Naturnothwendigkeit ... unabhängig und frei« ([c-kant-active-being-free-of-natural-necessity-by-intelligible-character](../../c/kant-active-being-free-of-natural-necessity-by-intelligible-character/)): the first stands under »Wenn aber die Kritik nicht geirrt hat« and has the will »gedacht«, the second under »würde«, and the convention sets `shares_premise_with` only where both statements assert one proposition; Kant's will there belongs to a thing in itself and is not said to be one. A reader who holds that the two assert the same may dispute the absence. Nietzsche's JGB 21 says »Im "An-sich" giebt es nichts von "Causal-Verbänden", von "Nothwendigkeit", von "psychologischer Unfreiheit"« ([c-nietzsche-in-the-in-itself-nothing-of-causal-connections](../../c/nietzsche-in-the-in-itself-nothing-of-causal-connections/)), its »"Nothwendigkeit"« the nearest word in the map to this sentence's »keine Nothwendigkeit kennt«, every term in his quotation marks, a sentence about what is read into things and not about a will as thing in itself; no shared premise is set, the convention requiring that both statements assert one proposition, and a reader who holds that they do may dispute the absence.
