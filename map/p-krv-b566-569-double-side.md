translation: "To think the faculty of an object of the senses with such a double side contradicts none of the concepts that we have to form of appearances and of a possible experience. For since a transcendental object, which determines them as mere representations, must underlie these, because they are in themselves no things, nothing hinders us from attributing to this transcendental object, besides the property through which it appears, also a causality that is not appearance, although its effect is nevertheless found in the appearance."

The fourth and fifth sentences of the section (AA 3:366.19–27), following at once on
[the three that open it](../../p/krv-b566-569-intelligible/); the A page is the 1781 edition's, checked against the page-by-page transcription of its scan on de.wikisource.org ([the opening passage](../../p/krv-b566-569-intelligible/) says how); the B page break inside B 566–569 is not marked in the korpora transcription and is not verified, so the B range given is the section's; the AA line is the authoritative locator. The first sentence is indicative and asserted: »widerspricht
keinem«. The second is the ground for it, »Denn«, and has the form »da ..., so hindert nichts,
daß ... nicht ... sollten«: the »da« clause asserts that a transcendental object must underlie
appearances and gives as its own reason, »weil sie an sich keine Dinge sind«, that appearances
are in themselves no things; the main clause asserts that nothing hinders the attribution. The
»nicht« after »daß« is the pleonastic negation of the construction »hindern, daß ... nicht« and
is not rendered. »Eine solche doppelte Seite, das Vermögen eines Gegenstandes der Sinne sich zu
denken« sets two accusatives side by side, the double side and the faculty, and can be read as thinking the faculty with such a side or such a side as the faculty; the »with« is the translation's, and it reorders the clause. »transscendentaler Gegenstand« is »transcendental object«, kept
distinct from »Ding an sich selbst«, which this sentence does not use; whether the two are one
is not stated in any passage in the map, and the map does not say. »Eigenschaft« is »property«.
