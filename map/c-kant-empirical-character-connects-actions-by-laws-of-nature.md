The seventh sentence of the section (AA 3:366.29–35), in the subjunctive throughout,
»würden wir ... haben«, »ständen«, »abgeleitet werden könnten«, »ausmachten«, kept as
»would« and »could«: Kant is drawing out what would hold of a subject with a faculty of the
kind the second sentence supposed, not describing one. »erstlich« answers to the »Zweitens«
of the next sentence
([c-kant-intelligible-character-cause-of-actions-as-appearances](../../c/kant-intelligible-character-cause-of-actions-as-appearances/)).
Its actions are considered »als Erscheinungen«, and three things are said of them under that
character: connection with other appearances by constant laws of nature, derivability from
them as their conditions, membership in a single series of the natural order. What the same
subject's actions would be under its empirical character is said again two paragraphs later
([c-kant-actions-explicable-by-laws-of-nature-by-empirical-character](../../c/kant-actions-explicable-by-laws-of-nature-by-empirical-character/)).
