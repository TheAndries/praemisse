One sentence at B xxix–xxx (AA 3:18.33–19.05). The main clause is the conclusion: I cannot
assume God, freedom and immortality for practical use "wenn ich nicht der speculativen
Vernunft zugleich ihre Anmaßung überschwenglicher Einsichten benehme" (3:18.35–37). The
"weil" clause gives two steps: to reach such insights speculative reason "muß" use
principles that reach only to objects of possible experience, applying them to what cannot
be an object of experience (3:18.37–19.03), premise 1; so applied they "wirklich dieses
jederzeit in Erscheinung verwandeln und so alle praktische Erweiterung der reinen Vernunft
für unmöglich erklären" (3:19.03–05), premise 2. The conclusion is the contrapositive of
the chain: if practical extension is not to be declared impossible, the pretension must go.

The form reads "wenn ich nicht ... benehme" as stating a necessary condition: the
assumption is possible only if the pretension is removed, a → ¬k. To reach it from the two
textual premises the form needs a bridge, premise 3, which is the map's and not Kant's: that
assuming God, freedom and immortality for practical use is a practical extension of pure
reason, so that it cannot stand while all such extension is declared impossible. It is
stated as an assumption so that a reader can see exactly where the text stops and the
reconstruction begins. The gloss of k, "pursues its pretension", also carries a small step:
Kant speaks of what speculative reason must do "um zu diesen zu gelangen" (3:18.37), and
the map equates having the pretension with attempting to reach the insights. What is not
in the form is the practical premise that the assumption is necessary ("des nothwendigen
praktischen Gebrauchs", 3:18.34–35); with it, the next sentence follows, "Ich mußte also
das Wissen aufheben" (3:19.05–06), which the map holds as a claim depending on this
conclusion rather than as part of this step. The form also does not say why principles so
applied "transform" their object into appearance; Kant asserts it here and argues it in the
Analytic, to which the preface refers (3:16.30–17.02).
