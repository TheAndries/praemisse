The fourth sentence of the passage (AA 3:365.21–25), the sentence that straddles the 1781 edition's page break at »Wenn dagegen«, so A 536–537 / B 564–565. Its antecedent, that appearances count for nothing more than they in fact are, carries inside it Kant's own assertion of what they are, »als sie in der That sind, nämlich nicht für Dinge an sich, sondern bloße Vorstellungen, die nach empirischen Gesetzen zusammenhängen«, and the statement sets that first and asserted; the consequent, that they »müssen ... selbst noch Gründe haben, die nicht Erscheinungen sind«, stands under the »wenn«, and the statement keeps it there, so that what the sentence asserts outright is what appearances are, and what it draws for the case that they are taken for no more than that is the grounds. »dagegen«, which sets this case against the one before it, »sind Erscheinungen Dinge an sich selbst« ([c-kant-if-appearances-are-things-in-themselves-freedom-not-to-be-saved](../../c/kant-if-appearances-are-things-in-themselves-freedom-not-to-be-saved/)), is dropped from the statement and named here; »für ... gelten« is "count for", »in der That« "in fact", »bloße« "mere", »zusammenhängen« "cohere", »selbst« "themselves", »noch« "still". The sentence says »Gründe«, grounds, and the sentence after it opens »Eine solche intelligibele Ursache«, in the passage and in no claim; it does not say »Ding an sich« of the grounds. Whether they are the thing in itself of B xxvi, the »etwas ..., was da erscheint« without which there would be appearance of nothing ([c-kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing](../../c/kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing/)), or the »transscendentaler Gegenstand« that the next section says must underlie appearances »weil sie an sich keine Dinge sind« and determines them »als bloße Vorstellungen« ([c-kant-transcendental-object-must-underlie-appearances](../../c/kant-transcendental-object-must-underlie-appearances/)), is not said in any passage in the map: three Kant sentences give what appearances need three names, no connective joins the three places, and the map sets no link among them and does not enter this sentence under the shared premise that there is a thing in itself to a given appearance, as the comparison on the thing in itself says in that premise's paragraph. One premise is shared, with Schopenhauer's § 21, »Erscheinung heißt Vorstellung, und weiter nichts« ([c-schopenhauer-appearance-is-representation](../../c/schopenhauer-appearance-is-representation/)): that an appearance is a representation and nothing more, which this sentence asserts by »in der That sind« and »bloße« and § 21 by »heißt« and »weiter nichts«. The one word both use is »Vorstellung«; no passage in the map says how Schopenhauer's use of the word stands to Kant's, so the sharing rests on the two statements, as the convention asks, and a reader may dispute it. Schopenhauer's § 24 conditional, that should the objects appearing in the forms not be empty phantoms but have a meaning, »so müßten sie auf etwas deuten ..., das ... keine Vorstellung, sondern ein Ding an sich wäre« ([c-schopenhauer-objects-not-empty-phantoms-must-point-to-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-objects-not-empty-phantoms-must-point-to-thing-in-itself/)), is the nearest Schopenhauer sentence in the map to the consequent, and no premise is set on it: its antecedent he does not assert, where Kant's »in der That« asserts his, and its consequent names a thing in itself where Kant's names grounds; § 21's third sentence, that the will is that »wovon alle Vorstellung, alles Objekt, die Erscheinung ... ist« ([c-schopenhauer-will-alone-is-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-will-alone-is-thing-in-itself/)), asserts of all representation that it is the appearance of something that is not representation and names it the will, where Kant's consequent stands under »wenn« and names grounds, so no premise is set on it either. No Nietzsche statement in the map denies, in its words, that appearances are mere representations or that they must have grounds that are not appearances: GD's first proposition, that another kind of reality »ist absolut unnachweisbar« ([c-nietzsche-grounds-for-apparent-establish-reality](../../c/nietzsche-grounds-for-apparent-establish-reality/)), denies that it can be shown, not that appearances have grounds; JGB 36's attempt to understand the mechanistic world not as »eine "Vorstellung" (im Berkeley'schen und Schopenhauerischen Sinne)« ([c-nietzsche-mechanistic-world-not-representation-in-schopenhauers-sense](../../c/nietzsche-mechanistic-world-not-representation-in-schopenhauers-sense/)) stands under »Gesetzt« and names Schopenhauer's sense, which no passage in the map identifies with Kant's; and JGB 15's sense organs that are not appearances »im Sinne der idealistischen Philosophie« ([c-nietzsche-sense-organs-not-appearances-in-idealist-sense](../../c/nietzsche-sense-organs-not-appearances-in-idealist-sense/)) say what the organs are not, not what appearances are, and name no one. No attack link is set, and each absence is open to dispute.
