original: "Auf welchen Standpunkt der Philosophie man sich heute auch stellen mag: von jeder Stelle aus gesehn ist die Irrthümlichkeit der Welt, in der wir zu leben glauben, das Sicherste und Festeste, dessen unser Auge noch habhaft werden kann: - wir finden Gründe über Gründe dafür, die uns zu Muthmaassungen über ein betrügerisches Princip im \"Wesen der Dinge\" verlocken möchten. Wer aber unser Denken selbst, also \"den Geist\" für die Falschheit der Welt verantwortlich macht - ein ehrenhafter Ausweg, den jeder bewusste oder unbewusste advocatus dei geht -: wer diese Welt, sammt Raum, Zeit, Gestalt, Bewegung, als falsch erschlossen nimmt: ein Solcher hätte mindestens guten Anlass, gegen alles Denken selbst endlich Misstrauen zu lernen: hätte es uns nicht bisher den allergrössten Schabernack gespielt? und welche Bürgschaft dafür gäbe es, dass es nicht fortführe, zu thun, was es immer gethan hat? In allem Ernste: die Unschuld der Denker hat etwas Rührendes und Ehrfurcht Einflössendes, welche ihnen erlaubt, sich auch heute noch vor das Bewusstsein hinzustellen, mit der Bitte, dass es ihnen ehrliche Antworten gebe: zum Beispiel ob es \"real\" sei, und warum es eigentlich die äussere Welt sich so entschlossen vom Halse halte, und was dergleichen Fragen mehr sind."
translation: "Whatever standpoint of philosophy one may take today: seen from every point, the erroneousness of the world in which we believe we live is the surest and firmest thing our eye can still get hold of: - we find grounds upon grounds for it, which would tempt us to conjectures about a deceptive principle in the \"essence of things\". But whoever holds our thinking itself, that is \"the spirit\", responsible for the falseness of the world - an honourable way out, which every conscious or unconscious advocatus dei takes -: whoever takes this world, together with space, time, shape, motion, as falsely inferred: such a one would have at least good occasion to learn at last distrust towards all thinking itself: would it not have played us the very greatest prank so far? and what guarantee would there be that it would not go on doing what it has always done? In all seriousness: the innocence of thinkers has something touching and awe-inspiring about it, which permits them even today still to place themselves before consciousness with the request that it give them honest answers: for example whether it is \"real\", and why it actually keeps the external world so resolutely at arm's length, and whatever more such questions there are."

The first three sentences of aphorism 34, to the sentence before the one on the belief in
immediate certainties ([p-jgb-34-immediate-certainties](../../p/jgb-34-immediate-certainties/)). With that passage,
the one on truth and semblance ([p-jgb-34-truth-and-semblance](../../p/jgb-34-truth-and-semblance/)) and the close
([p-jgb-34-the-world-a-fiction](../../p/jgb-34-the-world-a-fiction/)), the aphorism is in the map but for the sentences between the passage on
immediate certainties and the one on truth and semblance, which no passage holds. The
transcription's »Muthmaassungen« and »gesehn« are kept. »Irrthümlichkeit« is rendered
"erroneousness"; »das Sicherste und Festeste« "the surest and firmest thing"; »habhaft
werden« "get hold of"; »Muthmaassungen« "conjectures"; »verlocken möchten« keeps its
subjunctive as "would tempt". »"Wesen der Dinge"«, »"den Geist"« and »"real"« carry
Nietzsche's quotation marks, and »advocatus dei« is left in Latin as the text has it. the words »als
falsch erschlossen« are "as falsely inferred"; »Schabernack« "prank"; »Bürgschaft«
"guarantee". In the third sentence »welche ihnen erlaubt« is feminine and takes »die
Unschuld« as its antecedent; the English "which permits them" is left where the German puts it and does not by itself fix the antecedent, which the German gender does; »sich
... vom Halse halte«, literally keeps off its neck, is rendered "keeps ... at arm's length".
The first sentence is a claim whole ([c-nietzsche-erroneousness-of-the-world-the-surest-thing](../../c/nietzsche-erroneousness-of-the-world-the-surest-thing/)); the second's main clause, to »Misstrauen zu
lernen«, is a claim ([c-nietzsche-whoever-blames-thinking-for-the-worlds-falseness-should-distrust-thinking](../../c/nietzsche-whoever-blames-thinking-for-the-worlds-falseness-should-distrust-thinking/)), and its two questions after that colon are in no statement;
the third is a claim whole ([c-nietzsche-innocence-of-thinkers-asks-consciousness-for-honest-answers](../../c/nietzsche-innocence-of-thinkers-asks-consciousness-for-honest-answers/)).
