The second sentence of the passage (AA 3:363.28–30), asserted, a definition in the indicative; the 1781 transcription marks »Freiheit im practischen Verstande« as emphasized, as it marks a dozen other words in these spans, and the map reproduces no emphasis. »Willkür« is rendered "power of choice" and shown in the statement, since the map holds Kant's »Wille« at B xxvii–xxviii ([c-kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself](../../c/kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself/)) and no passage in the map says how the two words relate; the map does not identify them, and every cell set from this passage says so. The sentence before it says that the practical concept of freedom grounds itself on the transcendental idea ([c-kant-practical-concept-of-freedom-grounds-on-the-transcendental-idea](../../c/kant-practical-concept-of-freedom-grounds-on-the-transcendental-idea/)), and the two after it say what a power of choice is when sensible and when animal, the first in the passage and in no claim, and that the human one is free ([c-kant-human-power-of-choice-sensitivum-but-liberum](../../c/kant-human-power-of-choice-sensitivum-but-liberum/)). Free of what, in this sense: of necessitation by impulses of sensibility, where the cosmological sense is free of a prior determining cause ([c-kant-freedom-in-the-cosmological-sense-the-faculty-of-beginning-a-state-of-itself](../../c/kant-freedom-in-the-cosmological-sense-the-faculty-of-beginning-a-state-of-itself/)), B xxvii's will »jenem nicht unterworfen« is free of the law of nature, and Schopenhauer's will is free of the forms of its appearance ([c-schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance](../../c/schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance/)) and of necessity ([c-schopenhauer-will-not-subject-to-principle-of-sufficient-reason-knows-no-necessity-is-free](../../c/schopenhauer-will-not-subject-to-principle-of-sufficient-reason-knows-no-necessity-is-free/)); the four are four answers to the row's question, and the map sets no link among them, no two statements asserting one proposition. No Nietzsche statement in the map defines freedom; JGB 19 says what is called »"Freiheit des Willens"« and what the word is the word for ([c-nietzsche-freedom-of-the-will-the-affect-of-superiority-over-him-who-must-obey](../../c/nietzsche-freedom-of-the-will-the-affect-of-superiority-over-him-who-must-obey/), [c-nietzsche-freedom-of-the-will-the-word-for-the-pleasure-of-the-one-who-commands](../../c/nietzsche-freedom-of-the-will-the-word-for-the-pleasure-of-the-one-who-commands/)), an affect and a state of pleasure, which neither asserts nor denies independence from sensible necessitation, and no link is set.
