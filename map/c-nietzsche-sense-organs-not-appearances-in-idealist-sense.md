The first sentence of JGB 15. The statement keeps the sentence's frame, »um Physiologie mit
gutem Gewissen zu treiben«: what is asserted is a condition of doing physiology, and the ground
given is that appearances »als solche« could be no causes. The sentence names no philosopher
and no passage; »im Sinne der idealistischen Philosophie« is left as it stands, and the map does
not say whose idealism is meant. Schopenhauer's statement that appearance means representation
and every object is appearance ([c-schopenhauer-appearance-is-representation](../../c/schopenhauer-appearance-is-representation/), § 21) and
that the body is given as representation among objects ([c-schopenhauer-body-given-in-two-ways](../../c/schopenhauer-body-given-in-two-ways/),
§ 18) are the nearest in the map; the map sets no attack link, since the attacker's statement
would have to deny what the target states and this one denies that the sense organs are
appearances in a sense it does not identify as Schopenhauer's, and the organs are not named in
Schopenhauer's statements. A reader who holds that the sense is his may dispute the absence of
the link. The comparison [x-thing-in-itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) sets the three texts side by side in its row on
whether the world of appearance is the subject's own work.
