What is asserted, in the indicative, is two things joined by the semicolon and »ja«: that it is impossible that something in nature ought to be otherwise than it in fact is in all these temporal relations, and that the ought, under the condition »wenn man bloß den Lauf der Natur vor Augen hat«, has no meaning at all; the second half's condition is kept in the statement, and the first half is not under it. The sentence is drawn from no other by a connective and »ja« joins its halves, intensifying, not drawing, named here; the third sentence, that the understanding can cognize of nature only what is there, or has been, or will be, in the passage and in no claim, precedes it with the three temporal relations this sentence's »diesen« points to, and the map sets no link, no connective joining the two; the fifth, that we cannot at all ask what ought to happen in nature, in the passage and in no claim, repeats for the question what this sentence says of the ought's meaning, and draws from it by no connective.
