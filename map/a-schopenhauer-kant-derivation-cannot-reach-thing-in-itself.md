The argument is the paragraph of the appendix that begins »Mit der in der ersten Auflage«
(ZA II 534–535), which Schopenhauer says »läßt sich mit sehr Wenigem deutlich machen«:
Kant's thing in itself rests on a causal inference from sensation to an external cause;
the law, the sensation and the space in which the cause is placed are each subjective
([c-schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective/));
»Mithin« the whole empirical intuition stays »auf subjektivem Grund und Boden, als ein
bloßer Vorgang in uns«, and nothing »von ihr gänzlich Verschiedenes, von ihr
Unabhängiges« can be brought in as a thing in itself or shown to be a necessary
presupposition.

What the form captures and what it leaves out. Premise 1 is the first sentence of the
exposition. Premise 2 is not stated in the appendix paragraph: the map reads the »Mithin«
as resting on the principle § 19 states in general, that the law of causality holds only
for representation »und über welches hinaus es nie führen kann« (ZA I 146–147), and lists
that passage beside the appendix for that reason; what the appendix itself gives in its
place is the subjectivity of the law, the sensation and the space, which the map records
as a claim and does not put in the form, since the step from the three subjective items
to »Mithin« is the step premise 2 states. Premise 3 is the map's bridge and is near to a
definition: a thing in itself is what is wholly different from and independent of
representation, so an inference whose law never leads beyond representation brings in
none. The form does not touch whether premise 1 is true of Kant: Schopenhauer gives no
passage for it and says the inference is »unter mancherlei Wendungen verdeckt«. The map
sets no attack link on a Kant node, because no node of Kant's in the map states the
inference Schopenhauer attributes to him, and because Schopenhauer accepts what Kant's
nearest claims say, that a thing in itself underlies appearance
([c-schopenhauer-only-the-derivation-is-faulty](../../c/schopenhauer-only-the-derivation-is-faulty/));
the Kant passages in the map that speak of the senses being affected are Prolegomena § 13
Anm. II (AA 4:289, »indem sie unsere Sinne afficiren«) and § 32 (AA 4:314.36–315.01). The
last sentence of the paragraph, the positive route through self-consciousness, is not
part of the argument
([c-schopenhauer-thing-in-itself-reached-only-through-self-consciousness](../../c/schopenhauer-thing-in-itself-reached-only-through-self-consciousness/)).
