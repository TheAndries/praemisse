The second half of the first sentence of the passage, after the semicolon; the first half,
against reifying cause and effect »wie es die Naturforscher thun«, is in the passage and not
in the statement. »Nur« is kept. The sentence is a »soll«, a prescription, and the statement
keeps it as one. Schopenhauer's Anhang has the law of causality known a priori and »eine
Funktion unsers Intellekts« ([c-schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective/),
ZA II 534), and § 19 has it hold inside representation only
([c-schopenhauer-causality-never-leads-beyond-representation](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-never-leads-beyond-representation/)); Kant's B xxvii has it
apply only to things as objects of experience
([c-kant-causality-applies-only-to-things-as-objects-of-experience](../../c/kant-causality-applies-only-to-things-as-objects-of-experience/)). The sentence is a »soll«, a
prescription on how the two words are to be used, and asserts nothing of the law's origin that
Schopenhauer's statement asserts, that it is known a priori; a prescription to use a concept as
a fiction does not deny a statement that the law is a function of the intellect, and none of
these statements says that cause and effect explain, so no attack link is set; the comparison
[x-thing-in-itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) sets the three side by side in its paragraph on causality.
