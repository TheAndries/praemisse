The argument is the run of sentences in § 19 (ZA I 148) from »Denn welche andere Art von
Daseyn oder Realität sollten wir der übrigen Körperwelt beilegen?« to »so müssen wir sagen,
daß sie außer der Vorstellung, also an sich und ihrem Innersten Wesen nach, Das sei, was
wir in uns selbst unmittelbar als Willen finden« (the section continues for five more
sentences, on what belongs to the will's essence and what to its appearance). The »Denn«
gives the reason for the assumption stated just before it, that what remains of other
objects when their existence as representation is set aside must be will
([c-schopenhauer-other-objects-judged-by-analogy-with-body](../../c/schopenhauer-other-objects-judged-by-analogy-with-body/)),
and the »Wenn also« sentence restates that assumption as a conditional, which is the
conclusion claim. Schopenhauer introduces the whole step as a judgment »nach Analogie
jenes Leibes« and an assumption (»annehmen«, ZA I 147–148), and the paragraph before says
that theoretical egoism, which denies that objects known only as representations are
appearances of a will, »ist zwar durch Beweise nimmermehr zu widerlegen« (ZA I 147). The
argument therefore gives a reason, not a proof; its conclusion is conditional and
epistemic, what we »müssen sagen«, and it does not claim to establish its antecedent.

What the form captures and what it leaves out. Premise 1 is the map's: the text asks two
rhetorical questions, what other reality we *should* attribute and whence we should *take
the elements*, and the premise states the principle they presuppose, that a reality we can
attribute must be one we know or can think. Premise 2 is the text's answer, »Außer dem
Willen und der Vorstellung ist uns gar nichts bekannt, noch denkbar«: a reality known or
thinkable to us is representation or will. Premise 3 is the map's, and only says that a
reality *beyond* representation is not representation; the form needs it to eliminate the
disjunct. The text's own chain between premise 2 and the conclusion is not formalized: that
the greatest reality we can attribute to the corporeal world is the one our own body has,
»denn der ist Jedem das Realste«; that the body's reality, apart from representation, is
nothing but will
([c-schopenhauer-reality-of-body-exhausted-by-will](../../c/schopenhauer-reality-of-body-exhausted-by-will/));
and »daher« that no other reality can be found to attribute. The form says nothing about
whether premise 1 is true.
