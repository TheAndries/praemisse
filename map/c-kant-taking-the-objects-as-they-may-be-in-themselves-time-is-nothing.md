The first sentence of [KrV A 34–35 / B 51](../../p/krv-a34-35-b51-time-nothing-when-the-objects-are-taken-as-they-may-be-in-themselves/), the paragraph of § 6 after its three
conclusions (AA 3:60.28–31), kept whole: a conditional whose antecedent is an act of abstraction
that the text puts in the first person plural, »Wenn wir ... abstrahiren und mithin die Gegenstände
nehmen, so wie sie an sich selbst sein mögen«, and whose consequent, »so ist die Zeit nichts«, is
indicative. The statement keeps the »wir«, the »mögen«, "as they may be in themselves", and the
»mithin«, "hence", by which taking the objects as they may be in themselves is drawn from the
abstraction. What the sentence says under its »wenn« the third sentence of the paragraph asserts
outright two sentences on, »Die Zeit ist also ... an sich, außer dem Subjecte, nichts« ([c-kant-time-merely-a-subjective-condition-of-our-intuition-and-in-itself-apart-from-the-subject-nothing](../../c/kant-time-merely-a-subjective-condition-of-our-intuition-and-in-itself-apart-from-the-subject-nothing/)),
drawn from this and the second sentence by »also«, set as a link on that claim; and what A 26 / B 42
says of space in a subordinate clause, that no determination of things in themselves would remain
if one abstracted from all subjective conditions of intuition ([c-kant-space-not-of-things-in-themselves](../../c/kant-space-not-of-things-in-themselves/)), this sentence
says of time by the same abstraction, in the text's own »an sich selbst«; the two are parallel and
no connective joins them, so no link between Kant's claims is set. The claim is a conditional and
shares and denies nothing: no `shares_premise_with` and no attack is set; the premise that time is
no determination of the thing in itself is entered on the [comparison on the thing in itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) under conclusion (a)'s main clause
([c-kant-time-not-subsisting-for-itself-nor-an-objective-determination-attaching-to-things](../../c/kant-time-not-subsisting-for-itself-nor-an-objective-determination-attaching-to-things/)), which asserts it outright, and this sentence is named beside it. Among the row-mates in
the row's Kant cell: B xxvi's appearance without anything that appears, Prolegomena § 57's absurdity, B xxvii's two consequences for causality and for the free will, A 536's »so ist Freiheit nicht zu retten«, A 536–537's appearances as mere representations with grounds that are not appearances, A 538's transcendental object, A 540's transcendental object laid under appearances, B 311's division not admissible in a positive sense, B 313–314's »wie sie erscheinen« in an empirical sense, A 257's object taken as noumenon and the B 312 footnote's sensible or intelligible by kind of intuition, each of which says what appearances are, what follows from taking them for things in themselves, or what the noumenon is, and none of which says what time is or is not; and the two that say of time or of space what this claim says of time, A 539's »denn die Zeit ist nur die Bedingung der Erscheinungen, nicht aber der Dinge an
sich selbst« ([c-kant-intelligible-character-under-no-conditions-of-time](../../c/kant-intelligible-character-under-no-conditions-of-time/)) and A 26 / B 42, both named here. The claim is set on the [comparison on the thing in itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) in the
row on the division of appearance from thing in itself, for what its consequent says of time when
the objects are taken as they may be in themselves, the row's first question, by the criterion
that set A 539's »denn« clause and A 26 / B 42 there on 2026-10-29; the reading the map did not
take, that a conditional on an act of abstraction says nothing of the thing in itself outright and
could be held on no row, is open to dispute.
