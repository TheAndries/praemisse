GD, »Die ›Vernunft‹ in der Philosophie« 6, »Erster Satz. Die Gründe, darauf hin "diese"
Welt als scheinbar bezeichnet worden ist, begründen vielmehr deren Realität, - eine andre
Art Realität ist absolut unnachweisbar.« The map reads the grounds meant as those section 5
names, the »Vernunft-Vorurtheil« that posits unity, identity, duration, substance,
cause, thinghood and being ([p-gd-vernunft-5-will-as-cause](../../p/gd-vernunft-5-will-as-cause/)). The second half, that another reality cannot be shown, does not by
itself deny Kant's [c-kant-things-in-themselves-unknown](../../c/kant-things-in-themselves-unknown/),
which says that things in themselves are unknown, not that they cannot be shown to be; what Nietzsche adds is the first half and the fourth proposition,
[c-nietzsche-dividing-world-true-and-apparent-is-decadence](../../c/nietzsche-dividing-world-true-and-apparent-is-decadence/).
