fern es Object der sinnlichen Anschauung ist, d. i. als Erscheinung«; nevertheless it is
always reserved »daß wir eben dieselben Gegenstände auch als Dinge an sich selbst, wenn
gleich nicht erkennen, doch wenigstens müssen denken können. Denn sonst würde der
ungereimte Satz daraus folgen, daß Erscheinung ohne etwas wäre, was da erscheint«
(AA 3:16.30–17.07). The thing in itself is kept as what must be thinkable, and the ground
is the absurdity of appearance without anything that appears
([a-kant-appearance-requires-thing-in-itself](../../a/kant-appearance-requires-thing-in-itself/)).

# Where a premise is shared

**There is a thing in itself to a given appearance.** Kant at B xxvi, »etwas ..., was da
erscheint« (3:17.06–07), and Prolegomena § 32, »so gestehen wir hiedurch doch zugleich,
daß ihnen ein Ding an sich selbst zum Grunde liege« (4:314.33–315.06); Schopenhauer in the
Anhang, keeping »die Anerkennung eines Dinges an sich zur gegebenen Erscheinung« (ZA II
535). The link `shares_premise_with` is set between
[c-kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing](../../c/kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing/),
which asserts it, and
[c-schopenhauer-only-the-derivation-is-faulty](../../c/schopenhauer-only-the-derivation-is-faulty/),
whose sentence excepts Kant's »Anerkennung« from the defect it finds and so expressly
keeps Kant's assertion; the sharing is named in the text, not constructed by the map, and
the convention for such a link is in
[`MODEL.md`](https://github.com/TheAndries/praemisse/blob/main/MODEL.md). The premise is
worded in the texts' terms, »zum Grunde liege« and »zur gegebenen Erscheinung«, because
what it says depends on the reading of Kant: on the
[two-aspect reading](../../r/kant-two-aspect/) it concerns the same object considered
apart from the conditions of our sensibility, on the
[two-object reading](../../r/kant-two-object/) a distinct ground of the appearance; the
sharing holds on both only so worded. Since 2026-10-28 the page holds, in the row on the division, A 537's »so müssen sie selbst noch Gründe haben, die nicht Erscheinungen sind« ([c-kant-appearances-mere-representations-must-have-grounds-that-are-not-appearances](../../c/kant-appearances-mere-representations-must-have-grounds-that-are-not-appearances/)) and A 538's »transscendentaler Gegenstand zum Grunde liegen muß« ([c-kant-transcendental-object-must-underlie-appearances](../../c/kant-transcendental-object-must-underlie-appearances/)), and since 2026-10-29 A 540's »so wie wir überhaupt einen transscendentalen Gegenstand den Erscheinungen in Gedanken zum Grunde legen müssen« ([c-kant-intelligible-character-never-known-immediately](../../c/kant-intelligible-character-never-known-immediately/), set in the row on immediacy for its »unmittelbar« and in the row on the division for this clause; the main clause of the A 538 sentence, [c-kant-nothing-hinders-attributing-causality-to-transcendental-object](../../c/kant-nothing-hinders-attributing-causality-to-transcendental-object/), is on no comparison), three Kant sentences in the map near this premise and not entered under it: each says, as Prolegomena § 32 does with »zum Grunde liege«, that something underlies appearances, and none says »Ding an sich« of what it names, »Gründe« in the one and »transscendentaler Gegenstand« in the others; whether any is the thing in itself of B xxvi is not said in any passage in the map, and the map, which does not identify them, enters none, a reading open to dispute.

**Space is given a priori.** Kant at A 24 / B 38, space »eine nothwendige Vorstellung a
priori, die allen äußeren Anschauungen zum Grunde liegt« (the first sentence of the
passage at 3:52.30–53.16, [c-kant-space-a-priori](../../c/kant-space-a-priori/));
Schopenhauer in the Anhang, »der Raum ... ist eine a priori gegebene, folglich subjektive
Form unsers Intellekts« (ZA II 534,
[c-schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective/)),
in a sentence that opens by calling the a priori law of causality Kant's »eigenen und
richtigen Entdeckung«. Schopenhauer uses the shared premise against Kant: because space,
the sensation and the law of causality are all subjective, the inference he attributes to
Kant stays inside representation. The Schopenhauer statement asserts three things; this
premise and the next are its third, that space is »eine a priori gegebene, folglich
subjektive Form«, in its two parts.
The appendix's opening says the same with Kant's name and gives it as the ground of his
distinction: the Kantian distinction declares Locke's primary qualities too to belong only to
the appearance, »und zwar gerade deshalb, weil Bedingungen desselben, Raum, Zeit und
Kausalität, von uns a priori erkannt werden« (ZA II 513–514,
[c-schopenhauer-kant-makes-primary-qualities-appearance-because-forms-cognized-a-priori](../../c/schopenhauer-kant-makes-primary-qualities-appearance-because-forms-cognized-a-priori/)); the link is set on that sentence too, and a second entry under this premise names
the pair. The sentence's subject is the Kantian distinction, and the map reads its »weil«

»Vorstellung a priori«, Schopenhauer's »a priori erkannt«, and the premise covers what both
assert, that space is given a priori, not the faculty each assigns it to.
§ 2, in the first Book and earlier in the text than either Anhang sentence, says it too, and with Kant's name: the essential
and therefore universal forms of every object, »welche Zeit, Raum und Kausalität sind«, can
»auch ohne die Erkenntniß des Objekts selbst, vom Subjekt ausgehend gefunden und vollständig
erkannt werden«, »d.h. in Kants Sprache, a priori in unserm Bewußtseyn liegen« (ZA I 31,
[c-schopenhauer-forms-of-all-object-found-from-the-subject-a-priori](../../c/schopenhauer-forms-of-all-object-found-from-the-subject-a-priori/)), and the next

# Where they diverge

**On the inference to the thing in itself.** The ground Kant's text gives at B xxvi for
keeping the thing in itself is the absurdity of »Erscheinung ohne etwas ..., was da
erscheint« (3:17.06–07): a conditional with an absurd consequent, and the thing in itself
as what must be thinkable; that ground is not a causal inference. Schopenhauer's Anhang
attributes to Kant a different ground, »wiewohl unter mancherlei Wendungen verdeckt«: an
inference by the law of causality from the sensation to an external cause (ZA II 534,
[c-schopenhauer-kant-grounds-thing-in-itself-on-causal-inference](../../c/schopenhauer-kant-grounds-thing-in-itself-on-causal-inference/)),
and his checked argument
([a-schopenhauer-kant-derivation-cannot-reach-thing-in-itself](../../a/schopenhauer-kant-derivation-cannot-reach-thing-in-itself/))
concludes that such an inference never leaves representation (§ 19, ZA I 146–147). The map
has no Kant node stating the inference Schopenhauer attacks, and so sets no attack link
from Schopenhauer on any Kant node (said on both Schopenhauer nodes). The Kant passages in
the map that speak of affection are Prolegomena § 32, »die Art, wie unsre Sinnen von
diesem unbekannten Etwas afficirt werden« (4:314.36–315.01), and § 13 Anm. II, where the
things we are given are known only through »die Vorstellungen, die sie in uns wirken,
indem sie unsere Sinne afficiren« (4:289.03–14): a causal verb, »wirken«, of things as
they are in themselves. Whether that is the »Schluß nach dem Kausalitätsgesetz« the
Anhang names is a question about Kant that a reader can put to the texts here, side by
side, and that the map leaves open. Schopenhauer's own way of raising the question, at § 24, is a
conditional of his own: »Sollten nun aber die in diesen Formen erscheinenden Objekte nicht
leere Phantome seyn; sondern eine Bedeutung haben: so müßten sie auf etwas deuten ..., das
... keine Vorstellung, sondern ein Ding an sich wäre«, after which the question »ließe sich
wenigstens fragen« ([c-schopenhauer-objects-not-empty-phantoms-must-point-to-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-objects-not-empty-phantoms-must-point-to-thing-in-itself/)); the answer, the will, is § 21's and comes by the route of self-consciousness, not from this conditional.
Set beside B xxvi, the two conditionals run from different antecedents, Kant's from our
being unable to think the objects also as things in themselves to an absurdity, Schopenhauer's from the objects' having a
meaning to a thing in itself, and neither is the causal inference the Anhang attributes to
Kant; the map sets no link between them, since neither states the other's conditional. § 24
adds what the sciences cannot do: mathematics, however complete, gives only spatial and temporal relation,
etiology only why each appearance shows itself here and now, and »so dringen wir mit deren Hülfe doch
nimmermehr in das innere Wesen der Dinge«
([c-schopenhauer-mathematics-and-etiology-never-penetrate-inner-nature](../../c/schopenhauer-mathematics-and-etiology-never-penetrate-inner-nature/)), a claim about
explanation that the map keeps apart from § 19's claim about the law of causality.

**On causality and the in-itself.** The three texts deny causality of the thing in itself
and part on what causality is. Kant at B xxvii: if the deduction is right, »der Grundsatz der
Causalität« applies »nur auf Dinge im ersten Sinne genommen, nämlich so fern sie Gegenstände
der Erfahrung sind« (3:17.21–24,
[c-kant-causality-applies-only-to-things-as-objects-of-experience](../../c/kant-causality-applies-only-to-things-as-objects-of-experience/)),
a principle whose application is restricted. Schopenhauer's Anhang has it by Kant's »eigenen
und richtigen Entdeckung« that the law of causality is known a priori, and calls the law »eine
Funktion unsers Intellekts« (ZA II 534,
[c-schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective/)),
and § 19 keeps it inside representation
([c-schopenhauer-causality-never-leads-beyond-representation](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-never-leads-beyond-representation/)).
Nietzsche's JGB 21 has »"Ursache"« and »"Wirkung"« as »reiner Begriffe ..., das heisst als
conventioneller Fiktionen zum Zweck der Bezeichnung, der Verständigung, nicht der Erklärung«
([c-nietzsche-cause-and-effect-conventional-fictions](../../c/nietzsche-cause-and-effect-conventional-fictions/)),
and makes the step the other two do not: »Wir sind es, die allein die Ursachen ... erdichtet
haben«, so that to read the sign-world »als "an sich" in die Dinge« is to proceed
»mythologisch« ([c-nietzsche-projecting-sign-world-as-in-itself-is-mythology](../../c/nietzsche-projecting-sign-world-as-in-itself-is-mythology/)).
Set beside row 2, the sentence bears, as the map reads it, on Schopenhauer's charge against
Kant: where the Anhang attacks an inference by the law of causality to a thing in itself
because the law holds only inside representation, JGB 21 prescribes that cause and effect be
used as fictions and not for explanation, and on the map's reading an inference by them to
anything »an sich« goes with that; the sentence is a »soll« about the use of two words and
says nothing of the law's origin, so it does not deny the Anhang's statement that the law is
known a priori, and no attack link is set. JGB 15 adds a reductio
([a-nietzsche-external-world-not-work-of-organs](../../a/nietzsche-external-world-not-work-of-organs/)):
if the external world were the work of our organs, the organs would be their own work, on
the map's bridge a causa sui, which JGB 21 calls »der beste Selbst-Widerspruch, der bisher ausgedacht worden ist«
([c-nietzsche-causa-sui-best-self-contradiction](../../c/nietzsche-causa-sui-best-self-contradiction/));
the row on the subject's work sets that chain beside Kant's subjective condition of
sensibility and Schopenhauer's »Vorstellung«, neither of which says what the chain denies. Since 2026-11-08 the map holds A 542–543 / B 570–571, which calls the law of nature, that everything that happens has a cause, a law of the understanding through which appearances first constitute a nature ([c-kant-the-law-of-nature-is-a-law-of-the-understanding-from-which-it-is-permitted-under-no-pretext-to-except-any-appearance](../../c/kant-the-law-of-nature-is-a-law-of-the-understanding-from-which-it-is-permitted-under-no-pretext-to-except-any-appearance/)); whether Kant's law of nature there and the law of causality the Anhang calls a function of our intellect are one law no passage in the map says, the map does not identify them, and no premise is entered, said on the node.

**On what the division is.** Kant's conditional at B xxvi supposes that, were there no
thing in itself, appearance would remain and would be appearance of nothing, and calls that
absurd ([c-kant-without-things-in-themselves-appearance-without-appearing](../../c/kant-without-things-in-themselves-appearance-without-appearing/)).
Stage 6 of the Fabel abolishes the true world and denies that the apparent one remains:
»welche Welt blieb übrig? die scheinbare vielleicht?... Aber nein!«
([c-nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent](../../c/nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent/)).
On the map's reading, set side by side, the two texts agree that the apparent, Kant's
»Erscheinung« and the stage's »die scheinbare«, does not stand alone, and part on what is
given up: Kant keeps the thing in itself, and stage 6 gives up the predicate »scheinbar«,
which on the map's reading of the first proposition of »Die ›Vernunft‹ in der
Philosophie« 6 ([c-nietzsche-grounds-for-apparent-establish-reality](../../c/nietzsche-grounds-for-apparent-establish-reality/))
leaves the world real and undivided. The stage gives no ground of its own, so both
readings are the map's; the map sets no link between the two claims, since neither states
the other's conditional. Schopenhauer keeps the thing in itself and both terms: appearance
»heißt Vorstellung, und weiter nichts« (§ 21, ZA I 153–154), and the world is »einerseits

# What depends on the reading of Kant

The map holds two readings of »eben dieselben Gegenstände« at B xxvi
([two-aspect](../../r/kant-two-aspect/), [two-object](../../r/kant-two-object/)), and
the comparison changes with them. On the two-object reading, things in themselves are
entities distinct from appearances that ground them (Prolegomena § 32, »Wesen, die den
Erscheinungen zum Grunde liegen«, 4:315.02–05), and Schopenhauer's § 1 remark that Kant's
thing in itself »ihm leider unter den Händen ausgeartet ist« into an object in itself
(ZA I 30), together with the Anhang's attribution of a causal inference, has on that
reading a target in Kant. On the two-aspect reading, the thing in itself is the same
object considered apart from the conditions of our sensibility (A 26 / B 42, 3:55.04–05),
there is no second entity to be inferred, and Schopenhauer's target is not Kant's doctrine
but a reading of it. Nietzsche's fourth proposition names »in der Art Kant's« without
saying which; the claim's node relates it to Kant's appearance and thing in itself
([c-kant-objects-known-only-as-appearances](../../c/kant-objects-known-only-as-appearances/))
