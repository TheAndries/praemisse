# What the three say, in their own words

Kant, B xxvi: we can have cognition of no object as a thing in itself, »sondern nur so
fern es Object der sinnlichen Anschauung ist, d. i. als Erscheinung«; nevertheless it is
always reserved »daß wir eben dieselben Gegenstände auch als Dinge an sich selbst, wenn
gleich nicht erkennen, doch wenigstens müssen denken können. Denn sonst würde der
ungereimte Satz daraus folgen, daß Erscheinung ohne etwas wäre, was da erscheint«
(AA 3:16.30–17.07). The thing in itself is kept as what must be thinkable, and the ground
is the absurdity of appearance without anything that appears
([a-kant-appearance-requires-thing-in-itself](../../a/kant-appearance-requires-thing-in-itself/)).
What it is remains »gänzlich unbekannt« (A 42 / B 59, 3:65.17–19), and the noumenon is to
be understood »nur in negativer Bedeutung« (B 308–309, 3:210.24–34).

Schopenhauer, Anhang: Kant's »Hauptgebrechen« is the introduction of the thing in itself
»auf die von ihm gewählte Weise« (ZA II 534); »jedoch auch nur die Art der Ableitung,
nicht die Anerkennung eines Dinges an sich zur gegebenen Erscheinung« (ZA II 535). The
thing in itself is kept and named: »Ding an sich aber ist allein der Wille: als solcher
ist er durchaus nicht Vorstellung, sondern toto genere von ihr verschieden« (§ 21, ZA I
154), and the word will designates »keineswegs eine unbekannte Größe, ein durch Schlüsse
erreichtes Etwas; sondern ein durchaus unmittelbar Erkanntes« (§ 22, ZA I 155).

Nietzsche, GD: »Die "scheinbare" Welt ist die einzige: die wahre Welt ist nur
hinzugelogen« (»Die ›Vernunft‹ in der Philosophie« 2); to divide the world into a true
and an apparent one, »sei es in der Art Kant's«, is »nur eine Suggestion der décadence«
(ibid. 6, fourth proposition); and at the last stage of the Fabel, »mit der wahren Welt
haben wir auch die scheinbare abgeschafft!« JGB 16: »"unmittelbare Gewissheit", ebenso wie
"absolute Erkenntniss" und "Ding an sich"« includes »eine contradictio in adjecto«, and
Schopenhauer's »ich will« is named as a superstition.

# Where a premise is shared

**There is a thing in itself to a given appearance.** Kant at B xxvi, »etwas ..., was da
erscheint« (3:17.06–07), and Prolegomena § 32, »so gestehen wir hiedurch doch zugleich,
daß ihnen ein Ding an sich selbst zum Grunde liege« (4:314.33–315.06); Schopenhauer in the
Anhang, keeping »die Anerkennung eines Dinges an sich zur gegebenen Erscheinung« (ZA II
535). The link `shares_premise_with` is set between
[c-kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing](../../c/kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing/),
which asserts it, and
[c-schopenhauer-only-the-derivation-is-faulty](../../c/schopenhauer-only-the-derivation-is-faulty/),
whose sentence excepts Kant's »Anerkennung« from the defect it finds and so expressly
keeps Kant's assertion; the sharing is named in the text, not constructed by the map, and
the convention for such a link is in
[`MODEL.md`](https://github.com/TheAndries/praemisse/blob/main/MODEL.md). The premise is
worded in the texts' terms, »zum Grunde liege« and »zur gegebenen Erscheinung«, because
what it says depends on the reading of Kant: on the
[two-aspect reading](../../r/kant-two-aspect/) it concerns the same object considered
apart from the conditions of our sensibility, on the
[two-object reading](../../r/kant-two-object/) a distinct ground of the appearance; the
sharing holds on both only so worded.

**Space is given a priori.** Kant at A 24 / B 38, space »eine nothwendige Vorstellung a
priori, die allen äußeren Anschauungen zum Grunde liegt« (the first sentence of the
passage at 3:52.30–53.16, [c-kant-space-a-priori](../../c/kant-space-a-priori/));
Schopenhauer in the Anhang, »der Raum ... ist eine a priori gegebene, folglich subjektive
Form unsers Intellekts« (ZA II 534,
[c-schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective/)),
in a sentence that opens by calling the a priori law of causality Kant's »eigenen und
richtigen Entdeckung«. Schopenhauer uses the shared premise against Kant: because space,
the sensation and the law of causality are all subjective, the inference he attributes to
Kant stays inside representation. The Schopenhauer statement asserts three things; this
premise and the next are its third, that space is »eine a priori gegebene, folglich
subjektive Form«, in its two parts.

**Space is a subjective form.** Kant at A 26 / B 42, conclusion (b), space is »die
Form aller Erscheinungen äußerer Sinne, d. i. die subjective Bedingung der Sinnlichkeit,
unter der allein uns äußere Anschauung möglich ist« (3:55.09–11,
[c-kant-space-subjective-condition-of-sensibility](../../c/kant-space-subjective-condition-of-sensibility/));
Schopenhauer in the same Anhang sentence, »subjektive Form unsers Intellekts« (ZA II
534). Kant's word is sensibility and »Bedingung«, Schopenhauer's intellect and »Form«;
the premise covers what both assert, that the form is the subject's, and not the faculty
each assigns it to. The two texts also run the inference in opposite directions:
Schopenhauer from »a priori gegebene« to »folglich subjektive«, Kant from the subject's
receptivity to how the form »a priori im Gemüthe gegeben sein könne«.

**Space is no determination of the thing in itself.** Kant at A 26 / B 42, »Der Raum
stellt gar keine Eigenschaft irgend einiger Dinge an sich ... vor« (3:55.02–08,
[c-kant-space-not-of-things-in-themselves](../../c/kant-space-not-of-things-in-themselves/));
Schopenhauer at § 23, the will as thing in itself »völlig frei von allen Formen« of its
appearance, among them »Zeit und Raum« (ZA I 156,
[c-schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance](../../c/schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance/)).
The sharing is named a page later, where § 23 says that we shall then understand »den
Sinn der Kantischen Lehre ..., daß Zeit, Raum und Kausalität nicht dem Dinge an sich
zukommen, sondern nur Formen des Erkennens sind« (ZA I 157). The Schopenhauer statement
asserts more, that the thing in itself is will and free of every form including that of
object for a subject; the shared premise is the part about space.

**None with Nietzsche.** No Nietzsche statement in the map asserts, in his own voice, a
proposition that a Kant or Schopenhauer node states. The nearest are these, and each
differs. JGB 16 calls »Ding an sich« a contradictio in adjecto, where Schopenhauer calls
only an *object* in itself, »die keines von diesen Beiden wäre« (neither representation
nor will), a dreamt-up absurdity (§ 1, ZA I 30): Schopenhauer keeps a thing in itself and
Nietzsche's words hit the term as such. The fourth proposition of »Die ›Vernunft‹ in der
Philosophie« 6 names Kant's division of the world, but to name a division as décadence is
to diagnose it, not to share or state it. Stage 4 of the Fabel has the true world »als
unerreicht auch unbekannt«, which is close to Kant's »bleibt uns gänzlich unbekannt« (A 42
/ B 59), but the map frames the stage-4 claims as the positivist stage of a history of an
error, not as Nietzsche's own ([the reading followed is said on each node](../../c/nietzsche-true-world-unattained/)),
so no shared premise is set on them.

# Where they diverge

**On the inference to the thing in itself.** The ground Kant's text gives at B xxvi for
keeping the thing in itself is the absurdity of »Erscheinung ohne etwas ..., was da
erscheint« (3:17.06–07): a conditional with an absurd consequent, and the thing in itself
as what must be thinkable; that ground is not a causal inference. Schopenhauer's Anhang
attributes to Kant a different ground, »wiewohl unter mancherlei Wendungen verdeckt«: an
inference by the law of causality from the sensation to an external cause (ZA II 534,
[c-schopenhauer-kant-grounds-thing-in-itself-on-causal-inference](../../c/schopenhauer-kant-grounds-thing-in-itself-on-causal-inference/)),
and his checked argument
([a-schopenhauer-kant-derivation-cannot-reach-thing-in-itself](../../a/schopenhauer-kant-derivation-cannot-reach-thing-in-itself/))
concludes that such an inference never leaves representation (§ 19, ZA I 146–147). The map
has no Kant node stating the inference Schopenhauer attacks, and so sets no attack link
from Schopenhauer on any Kant node (said on both Schopenhauer nodes). The Kant passages in
the map that speak of affection are Prolegomena § 32, »die Art, wie unsre Sinnen von
diesem unbekannten Etwas afficirt werden« (4:314.36–315.01), and § 13 Anm. II, where the
things we are given are known only through »die Vorstellungen, die sie in uns wirken,
indem sie unsere Sinne afficiren« (4:289.03–14): a causal verb, »wirken«, of things as
they are in themselves. Whether that is the »Schluß nach dem Kausalitätsgesetz« the
Anhang names is a question about Kant that a reader can put to the texts here, side by
side, and that the map leaves open.

**On what the division is.** Kant's conditional at B xxvi supposes that, were there no
thing in itself, appearance would remain and would be appearance of nothing, and calls that
absurd ([c-kant-without-things-in-themselves-appearance-without-appearing](../../c/kant-without-things-in-themselves-appearance-without-appearing/)).
Stage 6 of the Fabel abolishes the true world and denies that the apparent one remains:
»welche Welt blieb übrig? die scheinbare vielleicht?... Aber nein!«
([c-nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent](../../c/nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent/)).
On the map's reading, set side by side, the two texts agree that the apparent, Kant's
»Erscheinung« and the stage's »die scheinbare«, does not stand alone, and part on what is
given up: Kant keeps the thing in itself, and stage 6 gives up the predicate »scheinbar«,
which on the map's reading of the first proposition of »Die ›Vernunft‹ in der
Philosophie« 6 ([c-nietzsche-grounds-for-apparent-establish-reality](../../c/nietzsche-grounds-for-apparent-establish-reality/))
leaves the world real and undivided. The stage gives no ground of its own, so both
readings are the map's; the map sets no link between the two claims, since neither states
the other's conditional. Schopenhauer keeps the thing in itself and both terms: appearance
»heißt Vorstellung, und weiter nichts« (§ 21, ZA I 153–154), and the world is »einerseits
durch und durch Vorstellung, so andererseits durch und durch Wille« (§ 1, ZA I 30), two
sides and no third.

**On immediacy.** Schopenhauer's thing in itself is cognized »durchaus unmittelbar« (§ 22,
ZA I 155), and the identity of will and body »kann ... ihrer Natur nach niemals bewiesen ...
werden, eben weil sie selbst die unmittelbarste ist« (§ 18, ZA I 144–145). Nietzsche's
JGB 16 names that belief, »wie es der Aberglaube Schopenhauer's war, "ich will"«, and JGB
19 says Schopenhauer »ein Volks-Vorurtheil übernommen und übertrieben« in holding the
will known »ganz und gar«: the two attacks set on
[c-schopenhauer-will-not-inferred-but-immediately-known](../../c/schopenhauer-will-not-inferred-but-immediately-known/).
JGB 19's »dass Wille und Aktion irgendwie Eins seien«, as the product of an expected
»Wirkung des Befehls«, attacks the identity of act of will and action of the body
(§ 18, [c-schopenhauer-act-of-will-and-action-of-body-identical](../../c/schopenhauer-act-of-will-and-action-of-body-identical/)).
Kant says nothing of immediacy, but he is on a side as to the thing in itself: what
objects are in themselves »bleibt uns gänzlich unbekannt« (A 42 / B 59,
[c-kant-things-in-themselves-unknown](../../c/kant-things-in-themselves-unknown/)), where
Schopenhauer's § 21 names the thing in itself will and § 22 calls the will »ein durchaus
unmittelbar Erkanntes«. The map sets no attack link from Schopenhauer on the Kant claim,
because its convention requires the denying words inside one statement and here two
statements do it between them; a reader who wants the link may dispute its absence. The
positive noumenon would need »eine besondere Anschauungsart ..., die aber nicht die
unsrige ist« (B 307–308, 3:209.32–210.12); whether Schopenhauer's self-consciousness is
an intuition of that kind is not stated in any passage in the map, and the map does not
say.

**On the will.** Schopenhauer's will as thing in itself is »völlig frei von allen Formen«
of appearance, even »die des Objekts für ein Subjekt« (§ 23, ZA I 156), groundless and one
(§ 23, ZA I 157). Nietzsche's GD has the will among the »Trugbilder und Irrlichter« of the
»innere Welt«, »der Wille ist eins von ihnen. Der Wille bewegt nichts mehr« (»Die vier
grossen Irrthümer« 3, [c-nietzsche-will-moves-nothing](../../c/nietzsche-will-moves-nothing/)),
and JGB 19 has willing as »etwas Complicirtes, Etwas, das nur als Wort eine Einheit ist«
([c-nietzsche-willing-is-complicated](../../c/nietzsche-willing-is-complicated/)). JGB 36
then makes the matter conditional, »ob wir an die Causalität des Willens glauben: thun
wir das ... so müssen wir den Versuch machen, die Willens-Causalität hypothetisch als die
einzige zu setzen«, and closes: »Die Welt von innen gesehen, die Welt auf ihren
"intelligiblen Charakter" hin bestimmt und bezeichnet - sie wäre eben "Wille zur Macht"
und nichts ausserdem.« Whether that is asserted is the map's second contested reading
([asserted](../../r/nietzsche-will-to-power-asserted/),
[not asserted](../../r/nietzsche-will-to-power-not-asserted/)). On the first reading
Nietzsche puts a will in the place Schopenhauer's will held, »von innen gesehen«, and the
comparison has three answers to the question what the world is in itself; on the second
it has two, and Nietzsche's answer to the question is that the question divides the world
(»Die ›Vernunft‹ in der Philosophie« 6, fourth proposition). The map does not pick.

# What depends on the reading of Kant

The map holds two readings of »eben dieselben Gegenstände« at B xxvi
([two-aspect](../../r/kant-two-aspect/), [two-object](../../r/kant-two-object/)), and
the comparison changes with them. On the two-object reading, things in themselves are
entities distinct from appearances that ground them (Prolegomena § 32, »Wesen, die den
Erscheinungen zum Grunde liegen«, 4:315.02–05), and Schopenhauer's § 1 remark that Kant's
thing in itself »ihm leider unter den Händen ausgeartet ist« into an object in itself
(ZA I 30), together with the Anhang's attribution of a causal inference, has on that
reading a target in Kant. On the two-aspect reading, the thing in itself is the same
object considered apart from the conditions of our sensibility (A 26 / B 42, 3:55.04–05),
there is no second entity to be inferred, and Schopenhauer's target is not Kant's doctrine
but a reading of it. Nietzsche's fourth proposition names »in der Art Kant's« without
saying which; the claim's node relates it to Kant's appearance and thing in itself
([c-kant-objects-known-only-as-appearances](../../c/kant-objects-known-only-as-appearances/))
and to the Fabel's stage 3, »königsbergisch«, and the comparison adds nothing to that.
