statement: "On whether there is a thing in itself to a given appearance, Kant and Schopenhauer share the premise that there is, Kant asserting it and Schopenhauer expressly keeping Kant's assertion, and Nietzsche denies it; on how the thing in itself would be reached, Kant holds that it can only be thought, never cognized, Schopenhauer that it is cognized immediately, in self-consciousness, as will, and Nietzsche that immediate certainty and the thing in itself alike include a contradictio in adjecto."

    note: "Kant: what objects are in themselves remains entirely unknown; the noumenon is to be understood only in the negative sense, as a thing insofar as it is not an object of our sensible intuition, the positive sense needing an intuition that is not ours; and reason sees a space for their cognition. Schopenhauer: the will as thing in itself is free of every form of appearance, groundless and one, and, never being object, bears the name will only a potiori; § 25 draws, and the map's checked argument formalizes on two bridges it names, that if the thing in itself is the will it lies outside time and space and knows no plurality. # What the three say, in their own words

Kant, B xxvi: we can have cognition of no object as a thing in itself, »sondern nur so
fern es Object der sinnlichen Anschauung ist, d. i. als Erscheinung«; nevertheless it is
always reserved »daß wir eben dieselben Gegenstände auch als Dinge an sich selbst, wenn
gleich nicht erkennen, doch wenigstens müssen denken können. Denn sonst würde der
ungereimte Satz daraus folgen, daß Erscheinung ohne etwas wäre, was da erscheint«
(AA 3:16.30–17.07). The thing in itself is kept as what must be thinkable, and the ground
is the absurdity of appearance without anything that appears
([a-kant-appearance-requires-thing-in-itself](../../a/kant-appearance-requires-thing-in-itself/)).
What it is remains »gänzlich unbekannt« (A 42 / B 59, 3:65.17–19), and the noumenon is to
be understood »nur in negativer Bedeutung« (B 308–309, 3:210.24–34). The footnote whose
mark stands after »denken können«, in the map since 2026-10-23, says what the two verbs
require: »Einen Gegenstand erkennen, dazu wird erfordert, daß ich seine Möglichkeit ...
beweisen könne. Aber denken kann ich, was ich will, wenn ich mir nur nicht selbst
widerspreche, d. i. wenn mein Begriff nur ein möglicher Gedanke ist, ob ich zwar dafür
nicht stehen kann, ob im Inbegriffe aller Möglichkeiten diesem auch ein Object
correspondire oder nicht«; objective validity, »reale Möglichkeit«, needs »etwas mehr«,
which »kann auch in praktischen« sources of cognition lie
([c-kant-thinking-requires-only-that-i-not-contradict-myself](../../c/kant-thinking-requires-only-that-i-not-contradict-myself/)
and the three claims beside it in the row on what can be said). The note names no
concept; that it bears on the thing in itself is read from the place of its mark.

Schopenhauer, Anhang: Kant's »Hauptgebrechen« is the introduction of the thing in itself
»auf die von ihm gewählte Weise« (ZA II 534); »jedoch auch nur die Art der Ableitung,
nicht die Anerkennung eines Dinges an sich zur gegebenen Erscheinung« (ZA II 535). The
thing in itself is kept and named: »Ding an sich aber ist allein der Wille: als solcher
ist er durchaus nicht Vorstellung, sondern toto genere von ihr verschieden« (§ 21, ZA I
154), and the word will designates »keineswegs eine unbekannte Größe, ein durch Schlüsse
erreichtes Etwas; sondern ein durchaus unmittelbar Erkanntes« (§ 22, ZA I 155).

Nietzsche, GD: »Die "scheinbare" Welt ist die einzige: die wahre Welt ist nur
hinzugelogen« (»Die ›Vernunft‹ in der Philosophie« 2); to divide the world into a true
and an apparent one, »sei es in der Art Kant's«, is »nur eine Suggestion der décadence«
(ibid. 6, fourth proposition); and at the last stage of the Fabel, »mit der wahren Welt
haben wir auch die scheinbare abgeschafft!« JGB 16: »"unmittelbare Gewissheit", ebenso wie
"absolute Erkenntniss" und "Ding an sich"« includes »eine contradictio in adjecto«, and
Schopenhauer's »ich will« is named as a superstition.

# Where a premise is shared

**There is a thing in itself to a given appearance.** Kant at B xxvi, »etwas ..., was da
erscheint« (3:17.06–07), and Prolegomena § 32, »so gestehen wir hiedurch doch zugleich,
daß ihnen ein Ding an sich selbst zum Grunde liege« (4:314.33–315.06); Schopenhauer in the
Anhang, keeping »die Anerkennung eines Dinges an sich zur gegebenen Erscheinung« (ZA II
535). The link `shares_premise_with` is set between
[c-kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing](../../c/kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing/),
which asserts it, and
[c-schopenhauer-only-the-derivation-is-faulty](../../c/schopenhauer-only-the-derivation-is-faulty/),
whose sentence excepts Kant's »Anerkennung« from the defect it finds and so expressly
keeps Kant's assertion; the sharing is named in the text, not constructed by the map, and
the convention for such a link is in
[`MODEL.md`](https://github.com/TheAndries/praemisse/blob/main/MODEL.md). The premise is
worded in the texts' terms, »zum Grunde liege« and »zur gegebenen Erscheinung«, because
what it says depends on the reading of Kant: on the
[two-aspect reading](../../r/kant-two-aspect/) it concerns the same object considered
apart from the conditions of our sensibility, on the
[two-object reading](../../r/kant-two-object/) a distinct ground of the appearance; the
sharing holds on both only so worded. Since 2026-10-28 the page holds, in the row on the division, A 537's »so müssen sie selbst noch Gründe haben, die nicht Erscheinungen sind« ([c-kant-appearances-mere-representations-must-have-grounds-that-are-not-appearances](../../c/kant-appearances-mere-representations-must-have-grounds-that-are-not-appearances/)) and A 538's »transscendentaler Gegenstand zum Grunde liegen muß« ([c-kant-transcendental-object-must-underlie-appearances](../../c/kant-transcendental-object-must-underlie-appearances/)), and A 540's »so wie wir überhaupt einen transscendentalen Gegenstand den Erscheinungen in Gedanken zum Grunde legen müssen« ([c-kant-intelligible-character-never-known-immediately](../../c/kant-intelligible-character-never-known-immediately/), set in the row on immediacy for its »unmittelbar«; the main clause of the A 538 sentence, [c-kant-nothing-hinders-attributing-causality-to-transcendental-object](../../c/kant-nothing-hinders-attributing-causality-to-transcendental-object/), is on no comparison), three Kant sentences in the map near this premise and not entered under it: each says, as Prolegomena § 32 does with »zum Grunde liege«, that something underlies appearances, and none says »Ding an sich« of what it names, »Gründe« in the one and »transscendentaler Gegenstand« in the others; whether any is the thing in itself of B xxvi is not said in any passage in the map, and the map, which does not identify them, enters none, a reading open to dispute.

**Space is given a priori.** Kant at A 24 / B 38, space »eine nothwendige Vorstellung a
priori, die allen äußeren Anschauungen zum Grunde liegt« (the first sentence of the
passage at 3:52.30–53.16, [c-kant-space-a-priori](../../c/kant-space-a-priori/));
Schopenhauer in the Anhang, »der Raum ... ist eine a priori gegebene, folglich subjektive
Form unsers Intellekts« (ZA II 534,
[c-schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective/)),
in a sentence that opens by calling the a priori law of causality Kant's »eigenen und
richtigen Entdeckung«. Schopenhauer uses the shared premise against Kant: because space,
the sensation and the law of causality are all subjective, the inference he attributes to
Kant stays inside representation. The Schopenhauer statement asserts three things; this
premise and the next are its third, that space is »eine a priori gegebene, folglich
subjektive Form«, in its two parts.
The appendix's opening says the same with Kant's name and gives it as the ground of his
distinction: the Kantian distinction declares Locke's primary qualities too to belong only to
the appearance, »und zwar gerade deshalb, weil Bedingungen desselben, Raum, Zeit und
Kausalität, von uns a priori erkannt werden« (ZA II 513–514,
[c-schopenhauer-kant-makes-primary-qualities-appearance-because-forms-cognized-a-priori](../../c/schopenhauer-kant-makes-primary-qualities-appearance-because-forms-cognized-a-priori/)); the link is set on that sentence too, and a second entry under this premise names
the pair. The sentence's subject is the Kantian distinction, and the map reads its »weil«
clause, in the indicative, as expressly keeping Kant's assertion; Kant's word at A 24 / B 38 is
»Vorstellung a priori«, Schopenhauer's »a priori erkannt«, and the premise covers what both
assert, that space is given a priori, not the faculty each assigns it to.
§ 2, in the first Book and earlier in the text than either Anhang sentence, says it too, and with Kant's name: the essential
and therefore universal forms of every object, »welche Zeit, Raum und Kausalität sind«, can
»auch ohne die Erkenntniß des Objekts selbst, vom Subjekt ausgehend gefunden und vollständig
erkannt werden«, »d.h. in Kants Sprache, a priori in unserm Bewußtseyn liegen« (ZA I 31,
[c-schopenhauer-forms-of-all-object-found-from-the-subject-a-priori](../../c/schopenhauer-forms-of-all-object-found-from-the-subject-a-priori/)), and the next
sentence calls the discovery of this »ein Hauptverdienst Kants und ein sehr großes«
([c-schopenhauer-discovery-of-the-forms-a-priori-a-chief-merit-of-kant](../../c/schopenhauer-discovery-of-the-forms-a-priori-a-chief-merit-of-kant/)); the link is set on the
first of the two sentences, and a third entry under this premise names the pair. That sentence
asserts its »daß« clause as what the commonality of the boundary between subject and object
shows, and the premise covers what both assert of space, that it is given a priori, Kant's
»Vorstellung a priori« and Schopenhauer's »a priori in unserm Bewußtseyn liegen«; what § 2
adds, that the forms are found »vom Subjekt ausgehend« even without cognition of the object,
is Schopenhauer's and not in Kant's sentence, and the premise does not cover it.

**Space is a subjective form.** Kant at A 26 / B 42, conclusion (b), space is »die
Form aller Erscheinungen äußerer Sinne, d. i. die subjective Bedingung der Sinnlichkeit,
unter der allein uns äußere Anschauung möglich ist« (3:55.09–11,
[c-kant-space-subjective-condition-of-sensibility](../../c/kant-space-subjective-condition-of-sensibility/));
Schopenhauer in the same Anhang sentence, »subjektive Form unsers Intellekts« (ZA II
534). Kant's word is sensibility and »Bedingung«, Schopenhauer's intellect and »Form«;
the premise covers what both assert, that the form is the subject's, and not the faculty
each assigns it to. The two texts also run the inference in opposite directions:
Schopenhauer from »a priori gegebene« to »folglich subjektive«, Kant from the subject's
receptivity to how the form »a priori im Gemüthe gegeben sein könne«.

**Space is no determination of the thing in itself.** Kant at A 26 / B 42, »Der Raum
stellt gar keine Eigenschaft irgend einiger Dinge an sich ... vor« (3:55.02–08,
[c-kant-space-not-of-things-in-themselves](../../c/kant-space-not-of-things-in-themselves/));
Schopenhauer at § 23, the will as thing in itself »völlig frei von allen Formen« of its
appearance, among them »Zeit und Raum« (ZA I 156,
[c-schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance](../../c/schopenhauer-will-free-of-forms-of-appearance/)).
The sharing is named a page later, where § 23 says that we shall then understand »den
Sinn der Kantischen Lehre ..., daß Zeit, Raum und Kausalität nicht dem Dinge an sich
zukommen, sondern nur Formen des Erkennens sind« (ZA I 157). The Schopenhauer statement
asserts more, that the thing in itself is will and free of every form including that of
object for a subject; the shared premise is the part about space.
The same premise is named a second time, with Kant's name, at § 24: »Was auch immer das
Ding an sich sei, so hat Kant richtig geschlossen, daß Zeit, Raum und Kausalität ... nicht
Bestimmungen desselben seyn, sondern ihm erst zukommen konnten, nachdem und sofern es
Vorstellung geworden« ([c-schopenhauer-kant-rightly-concluded-forms-not-determinations-of-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-kant-rightly-concluded-forms-not-determinations-of-thing-in-itself/)),
Schopenhauer's »Bestimmungen« being Kant's word at A 26 / B 42, »keine Bestimmung
derselben«; the link is set on that sentence too, and a second entry under the same premise names the pair. § 25
restates it, »nur unsere Erkenntnißform, nicht Eigenschaft des Dinges an sich«
([c-schopenhauer-plurality-conditioned-by-time-and-space-not-of-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-plurality-conditioned-by-time-and-space-not-of-thing-in-itself/)), and no third
link is set on it: § 25 asserts it by reference back, »wie oben auseinandergesetzt«, as a
restatement of §§ 23–24, and the map sets the link where the doctrine is stated, not where
it is recalled; a reader may ask for it.

**Space is not dependent on the objects that appear in it.** Kant at A 24 / B 38, space
»nicht als eine von ihnen abhängende Bestimmung« ([c-kant-space-a-priori](../../c/kant-space-a-priori/));
Schopenhauer at § 24, the forms »ganz unabhängig von den Objekten, die in ihnen erscheinen«
([c-schopenhauer-forms-in-consciousness-independent-of-objects](../../c/schopenhauer-forms-in-consciousness-independent-of-objects/)), in a sentence
that names its source, »von dem großen Kant gelernt«. Kant's sentence is about a
determination's dependence on appearances, Schopenhauer's about the forms' presence in
consciousness independently of the objects; the premise covers what both assert, that space
does not depend on what appears in it, and the Schopenhauer statement asserts it of time and
causality too.

**The laws that govern experience are not to be applied beyond it.** Kant at A 236 / B 295, »alles,
was der Verstand aus sich selbst schöpft, ohne es von der Erfahrung zu borgen, das habe er
dennoch zu keinem andern Behuf, als lediglich zum Erfahrungsgebrauch« (3:203.04–09,
[c-kant-understanding-principles-only-for-experience](../../c/kant-understanding-principles-only-for-experience/)); Schopenhauer in the appendix, where Kant »zeigte, daß die Gesetze, welche im
Daseyn, d.h. in der Erfahrung überhaupt, mit unverbrüchlicher Nothwendigkeit herrschen, nicht
anzuwenden sind, um das Daseyn selbst abzuleiten und zu erklären, daß also die Gültigkeit
derselben doch nur eine relative ist« (ZA II 516, [c-schopenhauer-kant-showed-laws-of-experience-not-applicable-to-derive-existence](../../c/schopenhauer-kant-showed-laws-of-experience-not-applicable-to-derive-existence/)). Schopenhauer's sentence reports
what Kant showed, and »zeigte« keeps the assertion as true; his next sentence names the laws,
»Zeit und Raum sowohl als Kausalität und Schlußfolge«, the principle of sufficient reason, and
the paragraph's last sentence has Kant show them »als durch die Erkenntnißweise des Subjekts
bedingt« ([c-schopenhauer-kant-showed-laws-and-world-conditioned-by-subjects-mode-of-cognition](../../c/schopenhauer-kant-showed-laws-and-world-conditioned-by-subjects-mode-of-cognition/)). Kant's subject is what the understanding draws from itself, his
»Grundsätze des reinen Verstandes«; Schopenhauer's is the laws that rule »im Daseyn«, time,
space, causality and inference; the identification of the two is a step the map takes,
supported by the Kant claim's second passage, Prolegomena § 57, where Kant says of »Zeit,
Raum und alle Verstandesbegriffe« that they have »keinen andern Gebrauch ..., als blos
Erfahrung möglich zu machen« (4:350.27–33), Schopenhauer's list less inference, and by
Schopenhauer's last sentence, where Kant showed those laws conditioned by the subject. The
premise is worded to cover both predicates, Kant's »Erfahrungsgebrauch« and Schopenhauer's
»nicht anzuwenden ..., um das Daseyn selbst abzuleiten«, not what each takes the laws to be. A
reader who holds the two subjects distinct may dispute the link.

**Things cannot be cognized as they may be in themselves.** Kant at A 42 / B 59, what objects
may be in themselves »bleibt uns gänzlich unbekannt« (3:65.17–22,
[c-kant-things-in-themselves-unknown](../../c/kant-things-in-themselves-unknown/)); Schopenhauer in the appendix's first sentence, Kant's distinction resting
»auf Grund der Nachweisung, daß zwischen den Dingen und uns immer noch der Intellekt steht,
weshalb sie nicht nach dem, was sie an sich selbst seyn mögen, erkannt werden können« (ZA II
513, [c-schopenhauer-kants-greatest-merit-distinction-of-appearance-from-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-kants-greatest-merit-distinction-of-appearance-from-thing-in-itself/)). »Nachweisung« is read as »zeigte« is in the entry above, as saying
that the thing was shown, so both statements assert it; the Schopenhauer statement asserts
more, that the distinction is Kant's greatest merit and that the intellect stands between
the things and us, which is the ground and not the premise. A reader who holds that a
sentence praising a doctrine for its ground does not assert the ground may dispute the link.

**An appearance is a representation and nothing more.** Kant at A 536–537 / B 564–565, the passage in the map since 2026-10-27 and the claim since 2026-10-28, where appearances are to count for nothing more than they »in der That sind, nämlich nicht für Dinge an sich, sondern bloße Vorstellungen, die nach empirischen Gesetzen zusammenhängen« (3:365.21–25, [c-kant-appearances-mere-representations-must-have-grounds-that-are-not-appearances](../../c/kant-appearances-mere-representations-must-have-grounds-that-are-not-appearances/)); Schopenhauer at § 21, »Erscheinung heißt Vorstellung, und weiter nichts« (ZA I 153–154, [c-schopenhauer-appearance-is-representation](../../c/schopenhauer-appearance-is-representation/)). Kant's »bloße« and Schopenhauer's »und weiter nichts« each say that an appearance is a representation and no more than that, and both statements assert it: Kant's by »in der That sind«, inside the antecedent of a conditional whose consequent, the grounds, stands under the »wenn« while the antecedent's own content is asserted; Schopenhauer's as the sentence § 21 sets before »Ding an sich aber ist allein der Wille«. The premise is worded with the one word both texts use, »Vorstellung«; no passage in the map says how Schopenhauer's use of it stands to Kant's, and the sharing is set on the two statements, as the convention asks, not on a history of the term, which a reader may dispute. What each sets against representation differs in name: Kant's sentence continues to »Gründe ..., die nicht Erscheinungen sind«, Schopenhauer's »aber« to the will. The nearest Kant sentence not entered is A 538's »transscendentaler Gegenstand ..., der sie als bloße Vorstellungen bestimmt« ([c-kant-transcendental-object-must-underlie-appearances](../../c/kant-transcendental-object-must-underlie-appearances/)), where »bloße Vorstellungen« is what the transcendental object determines appearances as, a determination by the object and not a statement of what appearance is; the entry holds the sentence that says it of appearances by »sind«, a reading open to dispute. The nearest Schopenhauer sentence not entered is § 1's, that everything there for cognition is object in relation to the subject, »Vorstellung« ([c-schopenhauer-world-is-my-representation](../../c/schopenhauer-world-is-my-representation/)), whose subject is the world and not appearance, where Kant's names appearances.

**The Transcendental Aesthetic as a whole.** Schopenhauer counts its theorems among »den
unumstößlichen Wahrheiten« and calls it »eine wirkliche, große Entdeckung in der Metaphysik«
(ZA II 536, [c-schopenhauer-theorems-of-transcendental-aesthetic-irrefutable-truths](../../c/schopenhauer-theorems-of-transcendental-aesthetic-irrefutable-truths/)); the paragraph then argues, from what a priori means, that »Erkenntnisse
a priori« and »selbsteigene Formen des Intellekts« are two expressions for one thing
([a-schopenhauer-cognitions-a-priori-are-the-intellects-own-forms](../../a/schopenhauer-cognitions-a-priori-are-the-intellects-own-forms/), [c-schopenhauer-cognitions-a-priori-and-forms-of-the-intellect-two-expressions-for-one-thing](../../c/schopenhauer-cognitions-a-priori-and-forms-of-the-intellect-two-expressions-for-one-thing/)). No link is set on either sentence: the endorsement names no proposition
of the Aesthetic, and the convention asks that both statements word the one they share; the
conclusion is about a priori cognition as such, where Kant's statements in the map, A 24 /
B 38 and A 26 / B 42, are about space. The proposition the two come nearest to sharing is that
what is cognized a priori is a form of the subject's own, which Schopenhauer's conclusion
asserts of all a priori cognition and Kant's A 26 / B 42, conclusion (b), of space
([c-kant-space-subjective-condition-of-sensibility](../../c/kant-space-subjective-condition-of-sensibility/)); a reader may dispute the absence
of a link, and the sharing on space itself is set two entries above.

**None with Nietzsche.** No Nietzsche statement in the map asserts, in his own voice, a
proposition that a Kant or Schopenhauer node states. The nearest are these, and each
differs. JGB 16 calls »Ding an sich« a contradictio in adjecto, where Schopenhauer calls
only an *object* in itself, »die keines von diesen Beiden wäre« (neither representation
nor will), a dreamt-up absurdity (§ 1, ZA I 30): Schopenhauer keeps a thing in itself and
Nietzsche's words hit the term as such. The fourth proposition of »Die ›Vernunft‹ in der
Philosophie« 6 names Kant's division of the world, but to name a division as décadence is
to diagnose it, not to share or state it. Stage 4 of the Fabel has the true world »als
unerreicht auch unbekannt«, which is close to Kant's »bleibt uns gänzlich unbekannt« (A 42
/ B 59), but the map frames the stage-4 claims as the positivist stage of a history of an
error, not as Nietzsche's own ([the reading followed is said on each node](../../c/nietzsche-true-world-unattained/)),
so no shared premise is set on them. The nearest since 2026-10-12 is JGB 21: »Im "An-sich"
giebt es nichts von "Causal-Verbänden", von "Nothwendigkeit"«
([c-nietzsche-in-the-in-itself-nothing-of-causal-connections](../../c/nietzsche-in-the-in-itself-nothing-of-causal-connections/)),
beside Kant's B xxvii, where the principle of causality applies only to things »so fern sie
Gegenstände der Erfahrung sind« and the same things as things in themselves are »ihm nicht
unterworfen« (3:17.21–24,
[c-kant-causality-applies-only-to-things-as-objects-of-experience](../../c/kant-causality-applies-only-to-things-as-objects-of-experience/)),
and Schopenhauer's § 23, where the will as thing in itself is free of the forms that have
»ihren gemeinschaftlichen Ausdruck im Satz vom Grunde« (ZA I 156) and »Zeit, Raum und
Kausalität nicht dem Dinge an sich zukommen« (ZA I 157). Three sentences withhold causality
from the thing in itself, Kant's conditionally, and the map sets no link: Kant's clause stands under »wenn die
Deduction ihrer Verstandesbegriffe richtig ist«, Nietzsche's »An-sich« is a term in his own
quotation marks whose next sentence says we invented what we read into it
([c-nietzsche-projecting-sign-world-as-in-itself-is-mythology](../../c/nietzsche-projecting-sign-world-as-in-itself-is-mythology/)),
and the convention requires that both statements assert one proposition. Whether anything is
asserted of a term set off in quotation marks is the question, and a reader may dispute the
absence of the link. A second pair from the same sentence: »Wir sind es, die allein die
Ursachen ... erdichtet haben« beside the Anhang's law of causality as »eine Funktion unsers
Intellekts, also subjektiven Ursprungs« (ZA II 534,
[c-schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective/)).
Both put the origin of causality in us, and neither statement words that as a proposition: a
cause we »erdichtet haben« is not an a priori »Funktion unsers Intellekts«, and the two
statements assert the invention and the function, not a common sentence; no link is set, and
a reader may dispute that too.

# Where they diverge

**On the inference to the thing in itself.** The ground Kant's text gives at B xxvi for
keeping the thing in itself is the absurdity of »Erscheinung ohne etwas ..., was da
erscheint« (3:17.06–07): a conditional with an absurd consequent, and the thing in itself
as what must be thinkable; that ground is not a causal inference. Schopenhauer's Anhang
attributes to Kant a different ground, »wiewohl unter mancherlei Wendungen verdeckt«: an
inference by the law of causality from the sensation to an external cause (ZA II 534,
[c-schopenhauer-kant-grounds-thing-in-itself-on-causal-inference](../../c/schopenhauer-kant-grounds-thing-in-itself-on-causal-inference/)),
and his checked argument
([a-schopenhauer-kant-derivation-cannot-reach-thing-in-itself](../../a/schopenhauer-kant-derivation-cannot-reach-thing-in-itself/))
concludes that such an inference never leaves representation (§ 19, ZA I 146–147). The map
has no Kant node stating the inference Schopenhauer attacks, and so sets no attack link
from Schopenhauer on any Kant node (said on both Schopenhauer nodes). The Kant passages in
the map that speak of affection are Prolegomena § 32, »die Art, wie unsre Sinnen von
diesem unbekannten Etwas afficirt werden« (4:314.36–315.01), and § 13 Anm. II, where the
things we are given are known only through »die Vorstellungen, die sie in uns wirken,
indem sie unsere Sinne afficiren« (4:289.03–14): a causal verb, »wirken«, of things as
they are in themselves. Whether that is the »Schluß nach dem Kausalitätsgesetz« the
Anhang names is a question about Kant that a reader can put to the texts here, side by
side, and that the map leaves open. Schopenhauer's own way of raising the question, at § 24, is a
conditional of his own: »Sollten nun aber die in diesen Formen erscheinenden Objekte nicht
leere Phantome seyn; sondern eine Bedeutung haben: so müßten sie auf etwas deuten ..., das
... keine Vorstellung, sondern ein Ding an sich wäre«, after which the question »ließe sich
wenigstens fragen« ([c-schopenhauer-objects-not-empty-phantoms-must-point-to-thing-in-itself](../../c/schopenhauer-objects-not-empty-phantoms-must-point-to-thing-in-itself/)); the answer, the will, is § 21's and comes by the route of self-consciousness, not from this conditional.
Set beside B xxvi, the two conditionals run from different antecedents, Kant's from our
being unable to think the objects also as things in themselves to an absurdity, Schopenhauer's from the objects' having a
meaning to a thing in itself, and neither is the causal inference the Anhang attributes to
Kant; the map sets no link between them, since neither states the other's conditional. § 24

**On causality and the in-itself.** The three texts deny causality of the thing in itself
and part on what causality is. Kant at B xxvii: if the deduction is right, »der Grundsatz der
Causalität« applies »nur auf Dinge im ersten Sinne genommen, nämlich so fern sie Gegenstände
der Erfahrung sind« (3:17.21–24,
[c-kant-causality-applies-only-to-things-as-objects-of-experience](../../c/kant-causality-applies-only-to-things-as-objects-of-experience/)),
a principle whose application is restricted. Schopenhauer's Anhang has it by Kant's »eigenen
und richtigen Entdeckung« that the law of causality is known a priori, and calls the law »eine
Funktion unsers Intellekts« (ZA II 534,
[c-schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-sensation-and-space-are-subjective/)),
and § 19 keeps it inside representation
([c-schopenhauer-causality-never-leads-beyond-representation](../../c/schopenhauer-causality-never-leads-beyond-representation/)).
Nietzsche's JGB 21 has »"Ursache"« and »"Wirkung"« as »reiner Begriffe ..., das heisst als
conventioneller Fiktionen zum Zweck der Bezeichnung, der Verständigung, nicht der Erklärung«
([c-nietzsche-cause-and-effect-conventional-fictions](../../c/nietzsche-cause-and-effect-conventional-fictions/)),
and makes the step the other two do not: »Wir sind es, die allein die Ursachen ... erdichtet
haben«, so that to read the sign-world »als "an sich" in die Dinge« is to proceed
»mythologisch« ([c-nietzsche-projecting-sign-world-as-in-itself-is-mythology](../../c/nietzsche-projecting-sign-world-as-in-itself-is-mythology/)).
Set beside row 2, the sentence bears, as the map reads it, on Schopenhauer's charge against
Kant: where the Anhang attacks an inference by the law of causality to a thing in itself
because the law holds only inside representation, JGB 21 prescribes that cause and effect be
used as fictions and not for explanation, and on the map's reading an inference by them to
anything »an sich« goes with that; the sentence is a »soll« about the use of two words and
says nothing of the law's origin, so it does not deny the Anhang's statement that the law is
known a priori, and no attack link is set. JGB 15 adds a reductio
([a-nietzsche-external-world-not-work-of-organs](../../a/nietzsche-external-world-not-work-of-organs/)):
if the external world were the work of our organs, the organs would be their own work, on
the map's bridge a causa sui, which JGB 21 calls »der beste Selbst-Widerspruch, der bisher ausgedacht worden ist«
([c-nietzsche-causa-sui-best-self-contradiction](../../c/nietzsche-causa-sui-best-self-contradiction/));
the row on the subject's work sets that chain beside Kant's subjective condition of
sensibility and Schopenhauer's »Vorstellung«, neither of which says what the chain denies.

**On what the division is.** Kant's conditional at B xxvi supposes that, were there no
thing in itself, appearance would remain and would be appearance of nothing, and calls that
absurd ([c-kant-without-things-in-themselves-appearance-without-appearing](../../c/kant-without-things-in-themselves-appearance-without-appearing/)).
Stage 6 of the Fabel abolishes the true world and denies that the apparent one remains:
»welche Welt blieb übrig? die scheinbare vielleicht?... Aber nein!«
([c-nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent](../../c/nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent/)).
On the map's reading, set side by side, the two texts agree that the apparent, Kant's
»Erscheinung« and the stage's »die scheinbare«, does not stand alone, and part on what is

which on the map's reading of the first proposition of »Die ›Vernunft‹ in der
Philosophie« 6 ([c-nietzsche-grounds-for-apparent-establish-reality](../../c/nietzsche-grounds-for-apparent-establish-reality/))
leaves the world real and undivided. The stage gives no ground of its own, so both
readings are the map's; the map sets no link between the two claims, since neither states
the other's conditional. Schopenhauer keeps the thing in itself and both terms: appearance
»heißt Vorstellung, und weiter nichts« (§ 21, ZA I 153–154), and the world is »einerseits
durch und durch Vorstellung, so andererseits durch und durch Wille« (§ 1, ZA I 30), two

# What depends on the reading of Kant

The map holds two readings of »eben dieselben Gegenstände« at B xxvi
([two-aspect](../../r/kant-two-aspect/), [two-object](../../r/kant-two-object/)), and
the comparison changes with them. On the two-object reading, things in themselves are
entities distinct from appearances that ground them (Prolegomena § 32, »Wesen, die den
Erscheinungen zum Grunde liegen«, 4:315.02–05), and Schopenhauer's § 1 remark that Kant's
thing in itself »ihm leider unter den Händen ausgeartet ist« into an object in itself
(ZA I 30), together with the Anhang's attribution of a causal inference, has on that
reading a target in Kant. On the two-aspect reading, the thing in itself is the same
object considered apart from the conditions of our sensibility (A 26 / B 42, 3:55.04–05),
there is no second entity to be inferred, and Schopenhauer's target is not Kant's doctrine
but a reading of it. Nietzsche's fourth proposition names »in der Art Kant's« without
saying which; the claim's node relates it to Kant's appearance and thing in itself
([c-kant-objects-known-only-as-appearances](../../c/kant-objects-known-only-as-appearances/))
and to the Fabel's stage 3, »königsbergisch«, and the comparison adds nothing to that.
