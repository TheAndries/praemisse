The second »wenn« clause of the sentence at B xxvii–xxviii (AA 3:17.20–24). It is not a
free-standing assertion in the text but a condition inside Kant's conditional, and the
statement keeps it conditional on the deduction's being right; the restriction of
causality is drawn from that by »mithin« (3:17.21). »Im ersten Sinne« and »nach der
zweiten Bedeutung« are the two meanings the first clause names, »als Erscheinung oder als
Ding an sich selbst« (3:17.19–20), which the statement spells out. The same restriction is
stated elsewhere in the map of the understanding's principles in general
([c-kant-understanding-principles-only-for-experience](../../c/kant-understanding-principles-only-for-experience/),
Prolegomena § 57) and of the categories (B 308–309); this node is the sentence that applies
it to causality for the sake of the will. It is not a premise of
[a-kant-freedom-thinkable-under-the-critical-distinction](../../a/kant-freedom-thinkable-under-the-critical-distinction/),
whose antecedent takes the restriction as given; it is the sentence that supplies it, and
the argument's body says so.
