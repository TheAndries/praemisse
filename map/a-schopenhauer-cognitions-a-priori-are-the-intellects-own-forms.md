The argument is the paragraph at ZA II 536–537 from its fourth sentence, »Denn«, to its last,
»Demnach«: a priori means not come from outside; what is in the intellect without having come
from outside is its own; if what is thus in it is the manner all its objects must present
themselves, it is the forms of its cognizing; accordingly cognitions a priori and the
intellect's own forms are two expressions for one thing.

What the form captures and what it leaves out. Premise 1 is the fact the third sentence takes
as proved by Kant ([c-schopenhauer-part-of-our-cognitions-a-priori-a-fact-proved-by-kant](../../c/schopenhauer-part-of-our-cognitions-a-priori-a-fact-proved-by-kant/)), that a part of our cognitions is a priori; the atoms speak of
that part throughout. Premise 2 is the definition ([c-schopenhauer-a-priori-means-not-gained-through-experience](../../c/schopenhauer-a-priori-means-not-gained-through-experience/)), a → ¬e; the text's »also« joins
»nicht auf dem Wege der Erfahrung gewonnen« and »nicht von außen in uns gekommen«, and the
atom e carries both. Premise 3 is the fifth sentence ([c-schopenhauer-what-is-in-the-intellect-without-coming-from-outside-is-its-own-essence](../../c/schopenhauer-what-is-in-the-intellect-without-coming-from-outside-is-its-own-essence/)), ¬e → b. Premise 4 is the sixth
sentence's conditional ([c-schopenhauer-if-what-is-in-the-intellect-is-how-objects-must-present-themselves-it-is-its-forms](../../c/schopenhauer-if-what-is-in-the-intellect-is-how-objects-must-present-themselves-it-is-its-forms/)), (b ∧ m) → f, where b glosses »dies so in ihm selbst
Vorhandene«, what the sentences before have reached. The first bridge, m, is the map's: the
text's conditional is never discharged in so many words, and its »Demnach« draws the
conclusion as if it had been; that the a priori cognitions consist in the universal manner all
objects must present themselves to the intellect is what the Aesthetic's theorems say of space
and time, which the paragraph's second sentence counts among the irrefutable truths
([c-schopenhauer-theorems-of-transcendental-aesthetic-irrefutable-truths](../../c/schopenhauer-theorems-of-transcendental-aesthetic-irrefutable-truths/)), and the map names the step as its own rather than read it into that sentence. The
second bridge, f → s, is the map's for »Demnach«: if the a priori cognitions are the forms of
the intellect's cognizing, the two expressions name one thing. Both are named in the
statement. The form proves s, the conclusion claim's proposition, its two hedges, »im Grunde nur« and
»gewissermaaßen«, left to the statement ([c-schopenhauer-cognitions-a-priori-and-forms-of-the-intellect-two-expressions-for-one-thing](../../c/schopenhauer-cognitions-a-priori-and-forms-of-the-intellect-two-expressions-for-one-thing/)); it does not prove
the third sentence's »gar keine andere Erklärung« ([c-schopenhauer-a-priori-cognitions-explained-only-as-forms-of-the-intellect](../../c/schopenhauer-a-priori-cognitions-explained-only-as-forms-of-the-intellect/)), which the paragraph states as its
thesis and which the chain supports without establishing the uniqueness. The check says nothing
of whether any cognition is a priori, nor of whether Kant proved it; what the map holds of
Kant's own proof for space is A 24 / B 38 ([c-kant-space-a-priori](../../c/kant-space-a-priori/)), and the comparison
[x-thing-in-itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) says why no link is set between that and this general conclusion.
