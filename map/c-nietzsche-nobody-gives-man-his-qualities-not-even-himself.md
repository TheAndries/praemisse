The second sentence of section 8, counting its opening question as the first, whole:
the answer to »Was kann allein unsre Lehre sein?«, a subordinate clause in the text,
»Dass Niemand ...«, which the statement makes a main clause and names so; the
parenthesis's dash is dropped and its »ist ... gelehrt worden« rendered as a participial
clause, "having been taught", both named here. The sentence is the one in the two
sections that names Kant, and what it attributes to him is reported as Nietzsche's
attribution: that the notion rejected last, that man gives himself his qualities, is
nonsense and was taught by Kant as »"intelligible Freiheit"«, the term in Nietzsche's
quotation marks. The comparison [x-the-will](../../x/the-will/) sets the sentence in its
row on whether the will is free, since 2026-10-24, for the words »intelligible Freiheit« and
»noch er selbst«; the sentence names a »"intelligible Freiheit"« and Kant, not the will,
and the row's note says so. No attack link is set on any Kant statement in the map. The phrase
»intelligible Freiheit« is in no Kant passage the map holds; what the map holds is the
»intelligibelen Charakter« of A 539 / B 567, by which »dieses thätige Wesen« would be
»von aller Naturnothwendigkeit ... unabhängig und frei«
([c-kant-active-being-free-of-natural-necessity-by-intelligible-character](../../c/kant-active-being-free-of-natural-necessity-by-intelligible-character/)), and
the subject that »seine Wirkungen in der Sinnenwelt von selbst anfange«
([c-kant-subject-begins-its-effects-of-itself](../../c/kant-subject-begins-its-effects-of-itself/)), each under »würde«, and, since 2026-10-27, A 537's effect that »kann ... in Ansehung ihrer intelligibelen Ursache als frei ... angesehen werden« ([c-kant-effect-regardable-as-free-with-respect-to-its-intelligible-cause-and-as-necessary-as-appearance](../../c/kant-effect-regardable-as-free-with-respect-to-its-intelligible-cause-and-as-necessary-as-appearance/)), the one Kant sentence in the map joining the adjective and »frei« in a clause not under »würde«, which says what can be regarded; none states
that man gives himself his qualities, »Eigenschaften«, and what this sentence denies is
that, so that it denies no Kant statement in the map, and whether Kant's intelligible
character is Nietzsche's »"intelligible Freiheit"« no passage in the map says. The map
does not identify them; a reader who holds that Kant's section states what this sentence
denies may dispute the absence. Nor is a link set to JGB 21, where the desire for
»"Freiheit des Willens"« is the desire to be »causa sui«
([c-nietzsche-desire-for-freedom-of-the-will-is-to-be-causa-sui](../../c/nietzsche-desire-for-freedom-of-the-will-is-to-be-causa-sui/)): that giving
oneself one's qualities is being causa sui is the map's reading and no passage's, and the
texts are joined by no connective. The sentence's »Lehre« is the doctrine of »wir«, the
first person plural of the section, kept.
