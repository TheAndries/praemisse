title: Without the distinction, a free will subject to natural necessity would be an obvious contradiction
thinker: t-kant
statement: "Had the distinction of things as objects of experience from the very same things as things in themselves not been made, I could not say of the very same being, for instance the human soul, that its will is free and yet at the same time subject to natural necessity, that is, not free, without falling into an obvious contradiction, because I have taken the soul in both propositions in the very same meaning, as a thing in general (as a thing in itself), and without a preceding critique could not take it otherwise."

The second sentence of the case at B xxvii (AA 3:17.12–18), still under the supposition of
the first, which the statement restates in its opening clause so that the sentence can be
read alone; the taking of the soul in one meaning is in the indicative, »genommen habe«,
and only the saying is counterfactual, which the translation keeps. The text draws it from the first by
»also« (3:17.12): »Von eben demselben Wesen also ... würde ich nicht sagen können«, so
`depends_on` names the first sentence's claim
([c-kant-without-distinction-causality-holds-of-all-things](../../c/kant-without-distinction-causality-holds-of-all-things/))
and the step is not formalized as an argument; a reader who wants it checked may ask for
it. The reason the sentence gives for the contradiction is the middle term of the whole
case: the soul taken »in beiden Sätzen in eben derselben Bedeutung« (3:17.15–16). Kant
writes »Ding überhaupt (als Sache an sich selbst)« (3:17.16–17), the parenthesis glossing
the noun, and the statement renders both nouns »thing«. The contradiction is of saying free and not free of
one thing in one meaning; the next sentence
([c-kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself](../../c/kant-same-will-not-free-as-appearance-free-as-thing-in-itself/))
removes it by the two meanings. Since 2026-10-28 the claim is set also on the comparison on the thing in itself, in the row on the division of appearance from thing in itself and what follows from refusing it, beside A 536's »sind Erscheinungen Dinge an sich selbst, so ist Freiheit nicht zu retten« ([c-kant-if-appearances-are-things-in-themselves-freedom-not-to-be-saved](../../c/kant-if-appearances-are-things-in-themselves-freedom-not-to-be-saved/)), the same consequence of the same case in the resolution of the third antinomy, as the map reads the two. The two places draw the consequence by no connective from each other, and no link is set between them.
