The fifth and sixth sentences of the passage (AA 3:212.21–27; A 256 / B 312), the close of the
paragraph, both asserted outright and joined into one statement by the semicolon, the sixth's
»Aber ... auch« kept as "but ... also". »nun auf diese Weise«, "now in this way", which refers
the extension to the paragraph's sentences before it, is dropped from the statement and named
here; the parenthesis »(nicht als Erscheinungen betrachtet)« is kept in commas; »d. i.« is
rendered by the colon; »mithin« "hence", »sofort« "at once", »Grenzen« "limits", »eines unbekannten Etwas« "an unknown something". The name is the one Prolegomena § 32 gives the thing in itself, »diesem unbekannten Etwas« (AA 4:314.36–315.01, [c-kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing](../../c/kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing/)); the identification of the two places' »Etwas« is the map's, and no link is set, no connective joining them. The sentences say what the understanding does with
things in themselves, names them and thinks them under a name, and what it denies itself,
cognizing them through categories; they are set for »erkennen« and »denken«, the two verbs of
the preface's sentence that the same objects must be thinkable as things in themselves »wenn
gleich nicht erkennen«
([c-kant-things-in-themselves-thinkable-not-knowable](../../c/kant-things-in-themselves-thinkable-not-knowable/)),
and of its footnote; the fit with the preface is the map's reading, drawn by no connective
across the two texts, and no link is set. »Noumena« here names things in themselves »nicht
als Erscheinungen betrachtet«, which is the noumenon in the negative sense of B 307,
»ein Ding ..., so fern es nicht Object unserer sinnlichen Anschauung ist«
([c-kant-noumenon-negative-only](../../c/kant-noumenon-negative-only/)); the identification is
the map's, by the words of the two definitions, and open to dispute.

No premise is shared and no attack is set. Schopenhauer's § 22, that the word will designates
»keineswegs eine unbekannte Größe, ein durch Schlüsse erreichtes Etwas; sondern ein durchaus
unmittelbar Erkanntes« ([c-schopenhauer-will-not-inferred-but-immediately-known](../../c/schopenhauer-will-not-inferred-but-immediately-known/)),
is the one Schopenhauer statement in the map that says of the thing in itself that it is not
an unknown something, where this sentence says the understanding thinks things in themselves
only under that name; the two meet in the word »Etwas«, and they would deny each other if
Schopenhauer's will is the thing in itself this sentence speaks of and his »erkannt« is Kant's
»erkennen«, which no passage in the map says, and which the comparison leaves open under *On
immediacy* for the sentences already set there; no attack is set, the identification being the
map's to refuse, and a reader may dispute it. Nietzsche's JGB 21, that in the »"An-sich"«
there is nothing of causal connections ([c-nietzsche-in-the-in-itself-nothing-of-causal-connections](../../c/nietzsche-in-the-in-itself-nothing-of-causal-connections/)),
says something of what the in-itself lacks and nothing of whether it is to be thought under a
name; Fabel's stage-4 claims, that the true world, as unattained, is also unknown and that something unknown cannot obligate us ([c-nietzsche-unknown-cannot-obligate](../../c/nietzsche-unknown-cannot-obligate/) and the two beside it), share the word "unknown"; they are the positivist stage's, narrated, and no premise is set on them. JGB 16's contradictio in adjecto is set as an attack on the two Kant claims that say the
concept is thinkable and not contradictory, not on this one, which says under what name the
understanding thinks it. Each absence is open to dispute.

Set on the comparison on the thing in itself, in the row on what can be said of the thing in
itself, since 2026-10-30, for what the sentences say the understanding can do with things in
themselves, name and think them, and cannot, cognize them. Not set in the row on the
division, where the passage's first sentence stands: »nicht als Erscheinungen betrachtet«
names the respect in which the understanding calls them noumena and says nothing of what
divides appearances from things in themselves, which is that row's first question.
