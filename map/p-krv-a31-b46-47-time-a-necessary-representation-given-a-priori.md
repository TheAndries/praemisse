original: "Die Zeit ist eine nothwendige Vorstellung, die allen Anschauungen zum Grunde liegt. Man kann in Ansehung der Erscheinungen überhaupt die Zeit selbst nicht aufheben, ob man zwar ganz wohl die Erscheinungen aus der Zeit wegnehmen kann. Die Zeit ist also a priori gegeben. In ihr allein ist alle Wirklichkeit der Erscheinungen möglich. Diese können insgesammt wegfallen, aber sie selbst (als die allgemeine Bedingung ihrer Möglichkeit) kann nicht aufgehoben werden. Auf diese Nothwendigkeit a priori gründet sich auch die Möglichkeit apodiktischer Grundsätze von den Verhältnissen der Zeit oder Axiomen von der Zeit überhaupt. Sie hat nur Eine Dimension: verschiedene Zeiten sind nicht zugleich, sondern nach einander (so wie verschiedene Räume nicht nach einander, sondern zugleich sind). Diese Grundsätze können aus der Erfahrung nicht gezogen werden, denn diese würde weder Strenge Allgemeinheit, noch apodiktische Gewißheit geben. Wir würden nur sagen können: so lehrt es die gemeine Wahrnehmung; nicht aber: so muß es sich verhalten. Diese Grundsätze gelten als Regeln, unter denen überhaupt Erfahrungen möglich sind, und belehren uns vor derselben und nicht durch dieselbe."
translation: "Time is a necessary representation that underlies all intuitions. One cannot, in regard to appearances in general, annul time itself, although one can quite well take the appearances out of time. Time is therefore given a priori. In it alone is all actuality of appearances possible. These can all drop away, but time itself (as the general condition of their possibility) cannot be annulled. On this necessity a priori rests also the possibility of apodictic principles of the relations of time, or axioms of time in general. It has only one dimension: different times are not simultaneous but successive (just as different spaces are not successive but simultaneous). These principles cannot be drawn from experience, for experience would give neither strict universality nor apodictic certainty. We would only be able to say: so common perception teaches; but not: so it must be. These principles hold as rules under which experiences are possible at all, and instruct us before experience and not through it."

The second and third of the five points of the metaphysical exposition of the concept of
time, § 4 of the Transcendental Aesthetic's second section, *Von der Zeit* (AA 3:57.27–58.14):
ten sentences counted by full stop, three colons and a semicolon joining halves (the seventh's colon, the ninth's two colons and its semicolon), the second point
3:57.27–58.03 (its first sentence 3:57.27–28, its third, »Die Zeit ist also a priori gegeben«,
3:57.30, its fifth 3:58.01–03) and the third point 3:58.04–14 (its last sentence 3:58.12–14). The
numerals 2) and 3) that open the points in the edition are omitted, as the numerals of the
points on space are omitted in [KrV A 24–25 / B 38–40](../../p/krv-a24-b38/), the passage this one
parallels point for point. The 1781 page A 31 begins with the second point and carries the third
whole, its fourth point beginning on the same page; the B pages, 46–47, are given from the standard
concordance and are not verified. The AA line is the locator.

**The 1781 text, collated on wikisource's page-by-page transcription of the scan.** Two wording
variants: the 1781 edition reads »die Zeit selbsten nicht aufheben« where the Akademie text (the
second edition's) has »die Zeit selbst nicht aufheben«, and, in the parenthesis of the seventh
sentence, »sondern zugleich seyn)« for »sondern zugleich sind)«; and the Akademie text's capital in
»nur Eine Dimension«, which marks the word, is lower case in 1781, »nur eine Dimension«, and in
the eighth sentence 1781 reads »weder strenge Allgemeinheit« where the Akademie text prints
»weder Strenge Allgemeinheit«, the capital kept as the transcription has it. Punctuation: the
parenthesis of the fifth sentence is set off by commas in 1781, »aber sie selbst, als die
allgemeine Bedingung ihrer Möglichkeit, kan nicht aufgehoben werden«; a comma after »Zeit« in the
sixth, »Verhältnissen der Zeit, oder Axiomen«; in the ninth a comma for the semicolon and a comma for the colon
after »nicht aber«, »so lehrt es die gemeine Wahrnehmung, nicht aber, so muß es sich verhalten«;
in the tenth no comma before »und belehren« and a comma before »und nicht«. Orthography (»kan«,
»wol«, »insgesamt«, »apodictischer«) is not listed. The transcription sets »a priori« in
antiqua, as the Akademie text sets it, and marks nothing as emphasized; the korpora HTML sets
nothing in these lines in another face.

**Translation.** »aufheben« is "annul", the map's word since B xxx (»das Wissen aufheben«);
»Wirklichkeit« "actuality"; »zugleich« and »nach einander« "simultaneous" and "successive";
»Grundsätze« "principles", as for the understanding's principles at A 236 / B 295; »Eine
Dimension« "only one dimension", »nur« "only", the capital of the edition, which marks the word, not
reproduced; »insgesammt«
"all"; »belehren« "instruct"; »diese« in the eighth sentence and »derselben« and »dieselbe« in the
tenth are resolved by name as experience, the noun each sentence names before it; »Strenge Allgemeinheit« "strict universality".
The indicative is kept throughout; »würde« in the eighth and ninth sentences is "would".

**What is held and what is not.** The first, third, fifth and tenth sentences are in one claim,
[c-kant-time-a-necessary-representation-underlying-all-intuitions-given-a-priori](../../c/kant-time-a-necessary-representation-underlying-all-intuitions-given-a-priori/), the premise of the map's argument on time
([a-kant-time-not-subsisting-for-itself-nor-an-objective-determination-of-things](../../a/kant-time-not-subsisting-for-itself-nor-an-objective-determination-of-things/)) and entered on the [comparison on the thing in itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) under the premise that time is given a priori, beside the
premise that space is given a priori. The other six sentences are in the passage and in no
claim: the second, the ground of the first, that one cannot annul time itself in regard to
appearances though one can take the appearances out of time, the fourth, that in time alone is
all actuality of appearances possible, and the sixth to ninth, on the apodictic principles of
time, their one dimension and their not being drawn from experience; none names the thing in
itself or what divides it from appearance; the argument's bridge draws on the third, fifth and tenth
sentences, all in the claim, and on none of these. The first point of § 4 (3:57.21–26), that
time is no empirical concept, the fourth and fifth (3:58.15–30), that time is no discursive
concept but a pure form of sensible intuition, and that its infinitude means that every
determinate magnitude of time is possible only through limitations of one underlying time, and
§ 5 (3:59.01–16), the transcendental exposition, are in no passage; the section's conclusions
follow in [KrV A 32–33 / B 49](../../p/krv-a32-33-b49-time-not-subsisting-for-itself-nor-an-objective-determination-of-things/).
