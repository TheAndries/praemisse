The second sentence of the passage, after »Man gestehe sich doch so viel ein«. The statement
keeps »wollte man« as "wanted", the supposition »gesetzt, ihr könntet das« as "supposing they
could", and the »ihr« and »eurer« as "they" and "their", the addressees being the »manche
Philosophen« of »tugendhafter Begeisterung und Tölpelei«, who are not named; »mindestens« and
»Tölpelei« are dropped from the statement and kept in the quotation, and the quotation marks
on »"Wahrheit"« are Nietzsche's. The sentence is the nearest the map holds to a converse of
stage 6 of the Fabel, where
abolishing the true world abolishes the apparent one
([c-nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent](../../c/nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent/), read on that node as Nietzsche's
own voice): here doing away with the apparent world leaves nothing of those philosophers'
»"Wahrheit"«. That the »"Wahrheit"« of this sentence is the »wahre Welt« of the Fabel is the
map's identification, not the texts'; the two sentences are in two works, no connective joins
them, the map sets no link and formalizes no argument, and the comparison [x-thing-in-itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) sets them beside Kant's B xxvi, where without
things in themselves there would be appearance without anything that appears
([c-kant-without-things-in-themselves-appearance-without-appearing](../../c/kant-without-things-in-themselves-appearance-without-appearing/)). Kant's sentence
concerns appearance and the thing in itself, this one the apparent world and »Wahrheit«, so no
link is set between them either.
