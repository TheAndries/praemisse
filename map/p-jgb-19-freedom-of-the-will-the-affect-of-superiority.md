translation: "That which is called \"freedom of the will\" is essentially the affect of superiority with regard to him who must obey: \"I am free, \"he\" must obey\" – this consciousness lies in every will, and likewise that tension of attention, that straight gaze which fixes exclusively on one thing, that unconditional valuation \"now this and nothing else is needful\", that inner certainty that there will be obedience, and whatever else belongs to the state of the one who commands. A man who wills –, commands a something in himself that obeys or that he believes obeys."

The seventh and eighth sentences of aphorism 19, counting its fourteen sentences by full stop,
question mark and exclamation mark (a colon joins halves), between the opening the map holds to
»Affekt des Commando's« ([p-jgb-19-willing-complicated](../../p/jgb-19-willing-complicated/)) and the two sentences on the
»Wunderlichste am Willen« ([p-jgb-19-will-and-action-one](../../p/jgb-19-will-and-action-one/)); with the close
([p-jgb-19-close-freedom-of-the-will-the-word-for-a-state-of-pleasure](../../p/jgb-19-close-freedom-of-the-will-the-word-for-a-state-of-pleasure/)) the four spans hold the aphorism whole. The transcription prints one kind of quotation
mark for both levels, so that »"ich bin frei, "er" muss gehorchen"« has the inner marks on »er« in the
same straight quotes as the outer; kept as transcribed, the printed edition's marks unchecked. The
dash the transcription prints after »Ein Mensch, der will« is kept. Terms: »Überlegenheits-Affekt«
"affect of superiority"; »in Hinsicht auf Den, der gehorchen muss« "with regard to him who must
obey"; »steckt in« "lies in", as the opening's »steckt das Volks-Vorurtheil« is rendered;
»Bewusstsein« "consciousness"; »Gewissheit« "certainty", as in every Nietzsche passage in the map;
»jetzt thut dies und nichts Anderes Noth« "now this and nothing else is needful", »Noth« rendered by
the adjective and not by "necessary", so that »Nothwendigkeit« (JGB 21) keeps "necessity"; »dass
gehorcht werden wird«, an impersonal passive, "that there will be obedience", the noun supplied where
English has no impersonal passive; »ein Etwas« "a something"; »des Befehlenden« "of the one who
commands", as »der Wollende« is "the one who wills" in the sentences that follow.
