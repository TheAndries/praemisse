The argument is the fourth and fifth sentences of the section (AA 3:366.19–27): the fourth
states the conclusion, the fifth gives its ground, »Denn«, in the form »da ..., so hindert
nichts«. Premise 1 is the »da« clause: appearances »an sich keine Dinge sind«, and because
they are not, »ein transscendentaler Gegenstand zum Grunde liegen muß, der sie als bloße
Vorstellungen bestimmt«; the form gives it two atoms and reads the »weil« as the conditional
`n -> g` together with the assertion of `n`. Premise 2 is the main clause, read as the step
the »da ..., so« draws: given the transcendental object, nothing hinders attributing to it a
causality that is not appearance, `g -> h`; the claim's own statement keeps the »since« and
asserts `h` on that ground. Premise 3 is the map's: the text passes from the unhindered
attribution of a non-appearing causality to the transcendental object back to the sentence
before, that the double side of the faculty of an object of the senses contradicts no concept
of experience, by »Denn« alone, and does not say that the two are one thought. They are not
word for word: the fourth sentence speaks of »das Vermögen eines Gegenstandes der Sinne« and
of a »doppelte Seite«, the fifth of »diesem transscendentalen Gegenstande« and of »eine
Causalität ..., die nicht Erscheinung ist« beside »der Eigenschaft, dadurch er erscheint«.
The bridge `h -> d` identifies the attribution of a non-appearing causality besides the
appearing property with the thought of the double side, and the transcendental object with
what the object of the senses is in itself, and the text's »hindert nichts«, that nothing stands in the way of the attribution, with its »widerspricht keinem von den Begriffen«, consistency with the concepts of experience; the three identifications are the map's, named here, and are what a reader may dispute. Premise 2's claim asserts, by its »since«, both the transcendental object and the unhindered attribution; the form reads the causal »da ..., so« as a sufficient condition, `g -> h`, which is the map's reading, and a reader who holds that the honest form is the bare `h`, with premise 1 idle and so removed, may dispute it. The conclusion is the fourth sentence whole.

The truth table says only that `d` holds in every assignment that satisfies the three
premises. It does not say that appearances are in themselves no things, that a transcendental
object underlies them, or that the bridge is faithful; those are the premises and matters of
fidelity. Nor does it say that any object of the senses has the double side: the conclusion
is a non-contradiction, and the second sentence of the section, which gives the double side,
is conditional
([c-kant-causality-of-a-being-considered-from-two-sides](../../c/kant-causality-of-a-being-considered-from-two-sides/)).
Whether the transcendental object of premise 1 is the thing in itself of B xxvi
([c-kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing](../../c/kant-appearance-presupposes-something-appearing/))
is not stated in any passage in the map, and the form does not need it; how »an einem
Gegenstande der Sinne« is to be read is what the two readings of Kant differ on
([two-aspect](../../r/kant-two-aspect/), [two-object](../../r/kant-two-object/)), and the map
does not pick.
