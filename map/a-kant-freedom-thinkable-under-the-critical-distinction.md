The argument is the two sentences at B xxvii–xxviii (AA 3:17.18–18.11), and its conclusion
is as conditional as they are. Premise 1 is the first sentence whole: if the critique has
not erred in taking the object in two meanings, and if its deduction is right, so that
causality is restricted to things as objects of experience, then the same will is thought
as not free and as free »ohne daß hiebei ein Widerspruch vorgeht« (3:17.18–28). The
conclusion is the main clause of the second sentence: »so kann ich mir doch die Freiheit
denken, d. i. die Vorstellung davon enthält wenigstens keinen Widerspruch in sich, wenn
unsere kritische Unterscheidung beider (der sinnlichen und intellectuellen)
Vorstellungsarten und die davon herrührende Einschränkung der reinen Verstandesbegriffe
... statt hat« (3:18.06–11). The form reaches it by hypothetical syllogism under the one
antecedent, the two meanings and the restriction together.

Three things are the map's and are said here. First, the step from premise 1 to the
conclusion: Kant's first sentence concludes that the *will* is thought free without
contradiction, his second that *freedom* can be thought, its representation containing no
contradiction, and the text joins them only by the concessive »Ob ich nun gleich ... so
kann ich mir doch«. The two words »Widerspruch« are not the same: the first (3:17.28) is a
contradiction between the two predicates, free and not free, of one will; the second
(3:18.07–08) is a contradiction inside the representation of freedom. The bridge that
passes from the one to the other is the second premise, an assumption, and that passage is
what a reader may dispute. Second, the antecedent: the first sentence's conditions are
that the critique rightly »das Object in zweierlei Bedeutung nehmen lehrt« and that »die
Deduction ihrer Verstandesbegriffe richtig ist, mithin« causality is restricted
(3:17.18–24); the second sentence's are »unsere kritische Unterscheidung beider ...
Vorstellungsarten und die davon herrührende Einschränkung« (3:18.08–11). The form takes
the two meanings of the object and the two kinds of representation as one distinction,
`k`, and the restriction as one, `r`, which the first sentence draws from the deduction
and the second from the distinction itself; both identifications are the map's. Third,
the deduction: the first sentence makes the restriction depend on it (»wenn die Deduction
... richtig ist, mithin«,
[c-kant-causality-applies-only-to-things-as-objects-of-experience](../../c/kant-causality-applies-only-to-things-as-objects-of-experience/)),
and the second sentence does not name it; since the conclusion claim's antecedent has the
restriction and not the deduction, the form takes `r` as given in the antecedent and the
sentence on the deduction is not a premise. It is the sentence that supplies `r`, and a
reader who wants the deduction in the chain may ask for the step.

The truth table says only that the conclusion holds in every assignment that satisfies
the premises. It does not say that the critique is right, that the deduction is right, or
that the two meanings remove the contradiction, which is premise 1 and a matter of
fidelity, nor does it settle how »zweierlei Bedeutung« is to be read
([two-aspect](../../r/kant-two-aspect/), [two-object](../../r/kant-two-object/)). The
negative half of the case, that without the distinction the contradiction stands
([c-kant-without-distinction-free-will-is-a-contradiction](../../c/kant-without-distinction-free-will-is-a-contradiction/)),
is not a premise here; it is the contrast the argument answers. Why freedom cannot be
cognized, though thought, is the concessive clause of the same second sentence
([c-kant-freedom-cannot-be-cognized-by-speculative-reason](../../c/kant-freedom-cannot-be-cognized-by-speculative-reason/)).
