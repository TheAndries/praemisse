original: "Wenn wir von unsrer Art, uns selbst innerlich anzuschauen und vermittelst dieser Anschauung auch alle äußere Anschauungen in der Vorstellungskraft zu befassen, abstrahiren und mithin die Gegenstände nehmen, so wie sie an sich selbst sein mögen, so ist die Zeit nichts. Sie ist nur von objectiver Gültigkeit in Ansehung der Erscheinungen, weil dieses schon Dinge sind, die wir als Gegenstände unsrer Sinne annehmen; aber sie ist nicht mehr objectiv, wenn man von der Sinnlichkeit unsrer Anschauung, mithin derjenigen Vorstellungsart, welche uns eigenthümlich ist, abstrahirt und von Dingen überhaupt redet. Die Zeit ist also lediglich eine subjective Bedingung unserer (menschlichen) Anschauung (welche jederzeit sinnlich ist, d. i. sofern wir von Gegenständen afficirt werden) und an sich, außer dem Subjecte, nichts. Nichts desto weniger ist sie in Ansehung aller Erscheinungen, mithin auch aller Dinge, die uns in der Erfahrung vorkommen können, nothwendiger Weise objectiv. Wir können nicht sagen: alle Dinge sind in der Zeit, weil bei dem Begriff der Dinge überhaupt von aller Art der Anschauung derselben abstrahirt wird, dieser aber die eigentliche Bedingung ist, unter der die Zeit in die Vorstellung der Gegenstände gehört. Wird nun die Bedingung zum Begriffe hinzugefügt, und es heißt: alle Dinge als Erscheinungen (Gegenstände der sinnlichen Anschauung) sind in der Zeit, so hat der Grundsatz seine gute objective Richtigkeit und Allgemeinheit a priori."
translation: "If we abstract from our way of intuiting ourselves inwardly and, by means of this intuition, also encompassing all outer intuitions in the power of representation, and hence take the objects as they may be in themselves, then time is nothing. It is of objective validity only in regard to appearances, because these are already things that we take as objects of our senses; but it is no longer objective if one abstracts from the sensibility of our intuition, hence from that kind of representation which is peculiar to us, and speaks of things in general. Time is therefore merely a subjective condition of our (human) intuition (which is always sensible, that is, insofar as we are affected by objects), and in itself, apart from the subject, nothing. Nonetheless it is, in regard to all appearances, hence also all things that can occur to us in experience, necessarily objective. We cannot say: all things are in time, because in the concept of things in general abstraction is made from every kind of intuition of them, whereas this is the proper condition under which time belongs to the representation of objects. If now the condition is added to the concept, and it is said: all things as appearances (objects of sensible intuition) are in time, then the principle has its good objective correctness and universality a priori."

The paragraph of § 6 of the Transcendental Aesthetic's section on time that follows its three
lettered conclusions (AA 3:60.28–61.11): six sentences counted by full stop, the second's
semicolon and the fifth's and sixth's colons joining halves (the first 3:60.28–31; the second 3:60.31–36; the third
3:60.36–61.02; the fourth 3:61.02–04; the fifth 3:61.04–08; the sixth 3:61.08–11). The 1781
page A 34 carries the paragraph from its beginning to »aber sie ist nicht mehr« and ends inside
the word »objectiv«, ob-|jectiv, with the catchword »ob-«, read on the scan of the page; the rest
is on A 35, which runs on into the paragraph after this one, on the empirical reality and the
transcendental ideality of time (3:61.12–32, in no passage). The B page, 51, is given from the
standard concordance and is not verified. The AA line is the locator.

**The 1781 text, collated on wikisource's page-by-page transcription of the scan.** One wording
variant: in the fifth sentence the 1781 edition reads »diese aber die eigentliche Bedingung ist«
where the Akademie text (the second edition's) has »dieser aber die eigentliche Bedingung ist«.
Otherwise the first sentence's »Nichts« is capitalized in 1781, »so ist die Zeit Nichts«, and
»Vorstellungskraft« is hyphenated, »Vorstellungs-Kraft«. Punctuation: in the first sentence a comma
after »anzuschauen« and after »abstrahiren«, »innerlich anzuschauen, und vermittelst« and
»abstrahiren, und mithin«; in the second a comma for the semicolon and a comma after »abstrahirt«,
»abstrahirt, und von Dingen«; in the third a comma before the parenthesis, »Anschauung, (welche«; in
the sixth a comma after »Dinge«, »alle Dinge, als Erscheinungen«. Orthography (»obiectiver«,
»subiective«, »iederzeit«, »so fern«, »ausser«, »Subiecte«, »bey«, »seyn«) is not listed. The transcription
sets »a priori« in antiqua, as the Akademie text sets it, and marks »Gegenstände unsrer Sinne« in
the second sentence and »von Dingen überhaupt« in the same sentence as emphasized, which the scan
of A 34 shows as letterspacing for the first, the second standing on A 35, read on the transcription; the korpora HTML sets nothing in these lines in another face, and no
emphasis is reproduced.

**Translation.** »abstrahiren« "abstract", »befassen« "encompassing", »Vorstellungskraft« "the
power of representation"; »so wie sie an sich selbst sein mögen« "as they may be in themselves",
the »mögen« kept; »nichts« "nothing", in the first and the third sentence; »von objectiver
Gültigkeit« "of objective validity"; »dieses schon Dinge sind« "these are already things", the
neuter demonstrative read as the appearances; »Vorstellungsart« "kind of representation";
»lediglich« "merely"; »(menschlichen)« and the parenthesis after »Anschauung« kept in their
parentheses; »sofern« "insofar as"; »afficirt« "affected"; »an sich, außer dem Subjecte, nichts«
"in itself, apart from the subject, nothing"; »Nichts desto weniger« "nonetheless"; »vorkommen«
"occur"; »dieser aber« in the fifth sentence is "whereas this", the demonstrative read as the kind
of intuition abstracted from, which the 1781 text's »diese«, agreeing with »Art der Anschauung«,
says in so many words; the Akademie text's »dieser«, as the nominative subject of »ist«, is
masculine, and the sentence's one masculine noun, »Begriff«, would make the concept of things in
general the condition under which time belongs to the representation of objects, which the
sentence's own »weil« clause excludes, so the map passes over it, the reading being the map's; »Richtigkeit«
"correctness".

**What is held and what is not.** The first sentence is [c-kant-taking-the-objects-as-they-may-be-in-themselves-time-is-nothing](../../c/kant-taking-the-objects-as-they-may-be-in-themselves-time-is-nothing/), the second [c-kant-time-objectively-valid-only-in-regard-to-appearances-not-objective-of-things-in-general](../../c/kant-time-objectively-valid-only-in-regard-to-appearances-not-objective-of-things-in-general/), the third
[c-kant-time-merely-a-subjective-condition-of-our-intuition-and-in-itself-apart-from-the-subject-nothing](../../c/kant-time-merely-a-subjective-condition-of-our-intuition-and-in-itself-apart-from-the-subject-nothing/), which the text draws from the two before it by »also« (3:60.36), set as links on the
third. The first and the third are set on the [comparison on the thing in itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/) in the row on the division of appearance from
thing in itself, the third also in the row on what is the subject's own, beside conclusion (b) on
space; the second is in no row and named there under *What the three say*. The fourth to sixth
sentences are in the passage and in no claim: the fourth, that time is nonetheless necessarily
objective in regard to all appearances and so all things that can occur in experience, names
neither the thing in itself nor what divides it from appearance; the fifth and sixth, that one
cannot say that all things are in time, since the concept of things in general abstracts from
every kind of intuition of them, but that all things as appearances are in time, draw the
contrast the second sentence draws, appearances against things in general, in the same words,
and fall outside the criterion of the row on the division for the reason the second sentence's
claim gives, that no passage in the map says whether Kant's things in general are things in
themselves; the one contrast gets the one outcome, and they are left unclaimed.
Conclusion (c), the paragraph before this one (3:60.13–27), and the paragraph after it, on
empirical reality and transcendental ideality (3:61.12–32), with § 7, the elucidation, are in no
passage.
