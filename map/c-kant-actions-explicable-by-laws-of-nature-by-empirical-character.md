The first two sentences of the section's third paragraph (AA 3:367.21–30), subjunctive
throughout, »würde ... unterworfen sein«, »wäre«, »abflössen«, »müßten«, kept as »would«; the
»also« of the first sentence resumes the empirical character of the first paragraph
([c-kant-empirical-character-connects-actions-by-laws-of-nature](../../c/kant-empirical-character-connects-actions-by-laws-of-nature/)).
»dieses Subject« is the acting subject of the paragraphs before, and the statement restates the supposition that says which subject, since the sentence opens with an anaphor. The second sentence's two »wie« clauses, »So wie äußere Erscheinungen in dasselbe einflössen, wie sein empirischer Charakter ... durch Erfahrung erkannt wäre«, are comparative, »as ... as«, not a condition, and the statement keeps both as the passage renders them. »erkannt« is »cognized«, kept
apart from »gekannt«. The quotation is the sentence's conclusion about the actions, the
load-bearing clause for the row on the will as cause, where this is the side of the subject
under which its actions flow from nature; the other side is
[c-kant-active-being-free-of-natural-necessity-by-intelligible-character](../../c/kant-active-being-free-of-natural-necessity-by-intelligible-character/),
and the sentence that joins them is
[c-kant-freedom-and-nature-together-in-the-same-actions](../../c/kant-freedom-and-nature-together-in-the-same-actions/),
drawn by »So würde denn«. The word »Wille« does not occur in the section.
