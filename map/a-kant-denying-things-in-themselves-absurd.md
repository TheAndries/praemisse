The second paragraph of Prolegomena §57 (AA 4:350.34–351.12). Kant states the verdict
first, "eine noch größere Ungereimtheit", and the reason after: denying things in
themselves, or taking our experience for the only possible mode of cognition, "mithin"
takes principles of the possibility of experience for universal conditions of things in
themselves (4:350.34–351.05), premise 1; "demnach" our restricting principles "könnten ...
selbst transscendent werden" and pass off the bounds of our reason for bounds of the
possibility of things (4:351.06–09), premise 2. Premise 3 is the map's: that a principle
which restricts reason to possible experience should itself be transcendent is absurd.
Kant does not say so in words; he says it "could" happen, that Hume's *Dialogues* are an
example of it happening, and that only a careful critique guards against it (4:351.09–12).
The map reads his verdict "Ungereimtheit" at 4:350.34 as resting on this: what makes
denying things in themselves absurd is that it turns the critique's own restricting
principles into transcendent claims about things. The conclusion is the sentence's own
verdict.

The reconstruction is the map's: the text gives the consequences as the explanation of the
verdict, and the form arranges them as a modus tollens. The interpretive choice is wholly
in premise 3. A reader who thinks the "Ungereimtheit" is asserted rather than argued here,
or that Kant's "könnten" leaves the outcome merely possible rather than unacceptable,
disputes premise 3, not the inference.

The conclusion is the counterpart of B xxvi's "Erscheinung ohne etwas ..., was da
erscheint" (3:17.06–07): both argue that something must be granted beyond appearances,
there from the concept of appearance, here from the restricting principles themselves.
