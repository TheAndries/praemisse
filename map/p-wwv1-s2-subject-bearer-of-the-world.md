original: "Dasjenige, was Alles erkennt und von Keinem erkannt wird, ist das Subjekt. Es ist sonach der Träger der Welt, die durchgängige, stets vorausgesetzte Bedingung alles Erscheinenden, alles Objekts: denn nur für das Subjekt ist, was nur immer daist. Als dieses Subjekt findet Jeder sich selbst, jedoch nur sofern er erkennt, nicht sofern er Objekt der Erkenntniß ist. Objekt ist aber schon sein Leib, welchen selbst wir daher, von diesem Standpunkt aus, Vorstellung nennen. Denn der Leib ist Objekt unter Objekten und den Gesetzen der Objekte unterworfen, obwohl er unmittelbares Objekt ist. Er liegt, wie alle Objekte der Anschauung, in den Formen alles Erkennens, in Zeit und Raum, durch welche die Vielheit ist. Das Subjekt aber, das Erkennende, nie Erkannte, liegt auch nicht in diesen Formen, von denen selbst es vielmehr immer schon vorausgesetzt wird: ihm kommt also weder Vielheit, noch deren Gegensatz, Einheit, zu. Wir erkennen es nimmer, sondern es eben ist es, das erkennt, wo nur erkannt wird."
translation: "That which cognizes everything and is cognized by none is the subject. It is accordingly the bearer of the world, the pervasive, always presupposed condition of everything that appears, of every object: for whatever there is at all is only for the subject. As this subject everyone finds himself, yet only in so far as he cognizes, not in so far as he is object of cognition. But his body is already object, even which we therefore, from this standpoint, call representation. For the body is object among objects and subject to the laws of objects, although it is immediate object. It lies, like all objects of intuition, in the forms of all cognizing, in time and space, through which there is plurality. The subject, however, the cognizing, never cognized, does not lie in these forms either, by which themselves it is rather always already presupposed: to it, therefore, belongs neither plurality nor its opposite, unity. We never cognize it; rather it is just that which cognizes, wherever there is any cognizing at all."

The whole first paragraph of § 2, eight sentences by full stop, the colon-joined ones counted as one each. zeno.org's transcription has »daist« at the end of the second sentence, as twice in § 1 (ZA I 28, »nur als Vorstellung daist«, »für die Erkenntniß daist«), where Grisebach's text (Projekt Gutenberg-DE, chapter 4) has »da ist«; the letters are kept as the transcription's, which prints the word so consistently, and Grisebach's text has otherwise the same words, so no other variant is to be named. Schopenhauer's footnote mark after »unmittelbares Objekt ist« in the fifth sentence, whose note reads »Ueber den Satz vom Grunde, 2. Aufl. §. 22.« in Grisebach's text, is omitted with the mark, as the work's page says of all footnote marks. The first two sentences are [c-schopenhauer-subject-bearer-of-the-world](../../c/schopenhauer-subject-bearer-of-the-world/); the six that follow, on everyone finding himself as this subject only in so far as he cognizes, on the body as object and so called representation, on the body as object among objects though immediate object, on its lying in time and space through which there is plurality, on the subject lying in none of these forms and having neither plurality nor unity, and on its never being cognized, are in no claim. The paragraph after this one opens with [p-wwv1-s2-two-halves-and-the-forms-a-priori](../../p/wwv1-s2-two-halves-and-the-forms-a-priori/). »Träger« is "bearer", »durchgängige« "pervasive", »was nur immer da ist« "whatever there is at all", »nimmer« "never", »wo nur erkannt wird« "wherever there is any cognizing at all"; »Erkenntniß« is "cognition" and »erkennen« "cognize", as on every Schopenhauer passage in the map.
