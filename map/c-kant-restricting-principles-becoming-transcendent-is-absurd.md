This claim is the map's reading of Prolegomena §57, not a sentence of Kant's, and it is
held as a node so that it can be disputed as one. What Kant says is that the restricting
principles "könnten demnach selbst transscendent werden und die Schranken unsrer Vernunft
für Schranken der Möglichkeit der Dinge selbst ausgeben" (AA 4:351.06–09), that Hume's
*Dialogues* "zum Beispiel dienen können" (4:351.09), and that this happens "wenn nicht eine
sorgfältige Kritik" guards the limits of reason (4:351.10–12). He calls the position that
leads there "eine noch größere Ungereimtheit" (4:350.34). The map takes the absurdity to lie
here: a principle whose whole content is that reason is restricted to possible experience
cannot, without undoing itself, be a claim about the possibility of things. A reader who
holds that Kant merely asserts the verdict, or that "könnten" leaves the outcome possible
rather than unacceptable, disputes this claim.
