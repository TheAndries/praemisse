The argument is stage 4 of »Wie die ›wahre Welt‹ endlich zur Fabel wurde«: »Die wahre
Welt - unerreichbar? jedenfalls unerreicht. Und als unerreicht auch unbekannt. Folglich
auch nicht tröstend, erlösend, verpflichtend: wozu könnte uns etwas Unbekanntes
verpflichten?...«

What the form captures and what it leaves out. Premise 1 is »jedenfalls unerreicht«.
Premise 2 is the map's reading of »als unerreicht auch unbekannt«: the stage draws
"unknown" from "unattained" without a stated ground, and the map records the step as an
assumption rather than attributing a general principle to Nietzsche. Premise 3 renders
the rhetorical question that closes the stage, and the claim's body says that this is
the map's reading of a question as a denial. The »Folglich« is the inference. The form
reaches only »verpflichtend«: the stage's full sentence has »tröstend, erlösend« too, but
the ground it gives speaks of obligation alone, so the conclusion claim is restricted to
obligation and the full sentence is a separate claim
([c-nietzsche-true-world-not-consoling-redeeming-obligating](../../c/nietzsche-true-world-not-consoling-redeeming-obligating/))
that no argument reaches.

Whose argument it is. The chapter narrates six stages and labels this one by its
parenthesis, »Hahnenschrei des Positivismus«, as it labels stage 1 Plato's and stage 3
»königsbergisch«; the map attributes stages 1 to 3 to no one but the positions named,
and cannot without a reading attribute stage 4 to Nietzsche in his own voice. The chapter
ends by calling stage 6 »Höhepunkt der Menschheit; INCIPIT ZARATHUSTRA«, which shows
that Nietzsche endorses the terminus, not that he endorses each stage's reasoning; and
stage 6 discards what stage 4 still keeps, »die scheinbare«. The map therefore frames
the three stage-4 claims and this argument as the positivist stage's position, which is
what the text states, and leaves open whether it is also Nietzsche's: stage 4's
parenthesis names a position, »Positivismus«, as stages 1 and 3 name Plato and
Königsberg, and the map does not pick between the reading on which the stages are
positions Nietzsche passed through and left (Clark 1990, ch. 3, on the chapter as
Nietzsche's own development) and one on which stages 4 to 6 are a single progression he
endorses. Stages 5 and 6 are different: their parentheses name no position but the free
spirits and »INCIPIT ZARATHUSTRA«, and since the owner's review of 2026-10-10 the map
reads them as Nietzsche's own voice, said on
[c-nietzsche-true-world-idea-refuted](../../c/nietzsche-true-world-idea-refuted/) and
[c-nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent](../../c/nietzsche-abolishing-true-world-abolishes-apparent/).
A reader may dispute the framing on either side. Stage 5 continues from the conclusion
by »folglich«, a step the map has not formalized.
