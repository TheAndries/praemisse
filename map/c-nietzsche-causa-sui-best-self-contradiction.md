The opening of JGB 21, before the colon; what follows the colon, on the pride of man, is in
the passage and not in the statement. The sentence asserts in Nietzsche's own voice what JGB 15
had supposed, »gesetzt, dass der Begriff causa sui etwas gründlich Absurdes ist«
([c-nietzsche-external-world-not-work-of-our-organs](../../c/nietzsche-external-world-not-work-of-our-organs/)); the map sets no link between the
two, since a self-contradiction and an absurdity are two words and the JGB 15 argument runs
under its supposition. The aphorism applies the causa sui to the »Verlangen nach "Freiheit des Willens"«, in the map since 2026-10-20 ([c-nietzsche-desire-for-freedom-of-the-will-is-to-be-causa-sui](../../c/nietzsche-desire-for-freedom-of-the-will-is-to-be-causa-sui/)) and set on its comparison on the will; that sentence's »nämlich« explains the clause after the colon, which is in no statement, and draws nothing from this one. Since 2026-10-27 the map holds A 533's idea, which reason creates for itself, of »einer Spontaneität, die von selbst anheben könne zu handeln, ohne daß eine andere Ursache vorangeschickt werden dürfe« ([c-kant-reason-creates-the-idea-of-a-spontaneity-that-could-begin-to-act-of-itself](../../c/kant-reason-creates-the-idea-of-a-spontaneity-that-could-begin-to-act-of-itself/)); whether such a spontaneity is a causa sui no passage in the map says, Kant asserts the idea as reason's and its content under »könne«, and this sentence names no thinker, so no attack is set and the identification is left to dispute.
