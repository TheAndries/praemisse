The third sentence of aphorism 21, the second of the passage. It stands under
»Gesetzt«, kept as "Supposing", and is a request in the first person, »so bitte ich ihn
nunmehr«, kept as "I now ask him", Nietzsche's, with »dergestalt« as "in this way"; the statement drops only the »ich meine« that introduces the apposition and keeps the rest. »Freier Wille« is
called »dieses berühmten Begriffs« and then »jenes Unbegriffs«, a concept and a non-concept
in one sentence, and the »unfreie Wille« is its »Umkehrung«; the sentence does not say that
either is false, it asks that both be struck, and the statement keeps that. What the "misuse of cause and effect" is, the next sentence says, as the map reads the two: one should not reify cause and
effect but use the two »eben nur als reiner Begriffe ..., das heisst als conventioneller
Fiktionen« ([c-nietzsche-cause-and-effect-conventional-fictions](../../c/nietzsche-cause-and-effect-conventional-fictions/)), and the close
of the aphorism says what the unfree will then is
([c-nietzsche-unfree-will-is-mythology](../../c/nietzsche-unfree-will-is-mythology/)). No link is set to Kant's B xxvii, where without the critical distinction
one could not say of one being that its will is free and yet subject to natural necessity
»ohne in einen offenbaren Widerspruch zu gerathen«
([c-kant-without-distinction-free-will-is-a-contradiction](../../c/kant-without-distinction-free-will-is-a-contradiction/)): Kant's sentence is
about what could not be said had the distinction not been made, and this one asks for two
terms to be struck; neither statement denies what the other states.
