The third sentence of the passage (AA 3:369.07–12), asserted in its main clause, »Hier ist nur die Frage«, with »nur« kept; what follows the colon is the question itself, two indirect clauses joined by »oder ob« in the Konjunktiv of indirect questions, »möglich sei« and »angetroffen werde«, which the English marks by the indicative of an indirect question. What the sentence asserts is that this is the only question here; it asserts neither that the same event can be regarded as an effect from freedom nor that a direct contradiction is found, and the statement keeps that, so that the claim is a claim about what the question is, not an answer. »Hier« points, as the map reads it, to the subsection's matter, the law of the paragraph before ([c-kant-the-law-of-nature-is-a-law-of-the-understanding-from-which-it-is-permitted-under-no-pretext-to-except-any-appearance](../../c/kant-the-law-of-nature-is-a-law-of-the-understanding-from-which-it-is-permitted-under-no-pretext-to-except-any-appearance/)) and freedom; the map's reading, said here. »eben dieselbe« is read as the event, the singular of the »Begebenheiten« just before it, and "event" is supplied in the translation, with the reason on the passage; »diesen zwei Arten von Causalität« are the two the sentence names, an effect of nature and an effect from freedom; »lauter Naturnothwendigkeit anerkennt« is "acknowledges nothing but natural necessity", and whether what is acknowledged is the law of nature of the paragraph before no connective says, the identification being the map's. The two kinds of causality are those A 532 says alone can be thought, »entweder nach der Natur, oder aus Freiheit« ([c-kant-only-two-kinds-of-causality-thinkable-nature-or-freedom](../../c/kant-only-two-kinds-of-causality-thinkable-nature-or-freedom/)), named in the same words, »aus Freiheit«, and that the pair is the same is the map's reading; A 532 asserts that only two can be thought, and this sentence asks whether a contradiction is found between them, so no link is set.
