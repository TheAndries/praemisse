The ninth sentence of section 8, counting its opening question as the first, whole;
the dash before »damit erst« is rendered "and" in the statement and kept in the
quotation, the closing dots dropped, and »erst« rendered "alone" both times, all named
here. »Befreiung« is "liberation": the word is not »Freiheit«, and the sentence does not
speak of the freedom of the will. »causa prima« is the text's Latin, and is not the
»causa sui« of JGB 21 ([c-nietzsche-causa-sui-best-self-contradiction](../../c/nietzsche-causa-sui-best-self-contradiction/)): the one
is a first cause to which the manner of being is not to be traced back, the other a cause
of itself; the map does not identify them and sets no link, and no connective joins the
texts. The sentence is set on no row of the comparison [x-the-will](../../x/the-will/),
since it names neither the will nor its freedom; that page's paragraph on freedom names
it as the close of the sequence that section 7 opens, as the map reads the two sections,
and the six sentences between, among them those on the fatality of one's essence and on man
as not the consequence of a will or a purpose, are in the passage and in no claim. No attack link is
set: no Kant or Schopenhauer statement in the map says that anyone is responsible or that
being is to be traced back to a causa prima; Kant's B xxviii supposes that morality
presupposes freedom and asserts only that it needs freedom not to contradict itself
([c-kant-if-freedom-unthinkable-morality-yields-to-mechanism](../../c/kant-if-freedom-unthinkable-morality-yields-to-mechanism/),
[c-kant-same-action-in-another-relation](../../c/kant-same-action-in-another-relation/)), a supposition this
sentence does not name.
