**The texts it rests on.** B xxvi–xxvii: without things in themselves there would be
"Erscheinung ohne etwas ..., was da erscheint" (AA 3:17.06–07): an appearance and a
something that appears. Prolegomena §32: to regard the objects of the senses as mere
appearances is to grant "daß ihnen ein Ding an sich selbst zum Grunde liege"; the
understanding "gesteht auch das Dasein von Dingen an sich selbst zu"; they are "Wesen, die
den Erscheinungen zum Grunde liegen", "bloßer Verstandeswesen" (4:314.33–315.06): existence,
grounding, beings of the understanding. A 42 / B 59: how it stands with "den Gegenständen an
sich und abgesondert von aller dieser Receptivität unserer Sinnlichkeit" remains unknown;
"wir kennen nichts als unsere Art, sie wahrzunehmen" (3:65.17–20). Prolegomena §57: it is
absurd "gar keine Dinge an sich selbst einräumen" (4:350.35): things in themselves are
something one admits or denies.

**What it holds.** On this reading the thing in itself is a distinct entity, the ground of
the appearance (Strawson 1966, Part Four; Guyer 1987, Part V; Van Cleve 1999, ch. 10). The
argument at B xxvi ([a-kant-appearance-requires-thing-in-itself](../../a/kant-appearance-requires-thing-in-itself/))
is then an inference to the existence of such a ground, and the Prolegomena §57 argument
([a-kant-denying-things-in-themselves-absurd](../../a/kant-denying-things-in-themselves-absurd/))
a second one; the unknowability claim
([c-kant-things-in-themselves-unknown](../../c/kant-things-in-themselves-unknown/)) is a
claim about those entities.

**What depends on it.** Read this way, the Prolegomena §57 argument is an existence
proof, and the restriction of the categories to objects of experience (B 308–309,
3:210.24–34) becomes a problem for it, since "ground" is a category; the unknowability
claim then says of distinct things that nothing of them is known. Schopenhauer's and
Nietzsche's criticisms, when the map reaches them, will be recorded against this reading
where their texts name it.

**What tells against it.** B xxvi's "eben dieselben Gegenstände" (3:17.03–04) and B
xxviii–xxix's "eben derselben Handlung (in anderer Beziehung genommen)" (3:18.24–25) speak
of one object or action under two descriptions, the text the
[two-aspect reading](../../r/kant-two-aspect/) rests on. And the inference to a distinct
ground sits uneasily with the restriction of the categories, cause among them, to objects
of possible experience (B 308–309, 3:210.24–34).
