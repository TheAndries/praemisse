The last two sentences of the passage, both questions. The statement reports them as
questions, »Nietzsche asks ...«, keeping »gleichsam« and the painters' »valeurs«, and does not turn them into the assertions a reader will hear in
them, that nothing compels the opposition and that degrees suffice: the text asks, and a
dispute may contest either the restraint or its opposite. »Stufen der Scheinbarkeit« is
"degrees of apparentness" and »Gesammttöne des Scheins« "overall tones of semblance", the two
words kept apart. The map sets the questions beside the division of the world into true and apparent that the
fourth proposition of »Die ›Vernunft‹ in der Philosophie« 6 calls décadence
([c-nietzsche-dividing-world-true-and-apparent-is-decadence](../../c/nietzsche-dividing-world-true-and-apparent-is-decadence/)), the questions offer what
would stand in the division's place; the two texts are joined by no connective and the map sets
no link. No Kant or Schopenhauer statement in the map asserts an essential opposition of true
and false in these words, so no attack link is set; the comparison [x-thing-in-itself](../../x/thing-in-itself/)
sets the questions in its row on the division.
