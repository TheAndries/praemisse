6 · Apparatus note, in the two-column node layout, with 7 · Passage pair

Claim

It would be absurd that there be appearance without something that appears; and to regard the objects of the senses as mere appearances is to grant that a thing in itself underlies them.

B xxvi–xxvii has only the first half, and negatively: without the reservation that we can think the same objects as things in themselves, "the absurd proposition would follow that there is appearance without anything that appears" (AA 3:17.05–07); it says "something that appears", not "thing in itself". The second half is Prolegomena §32, which puts it positively: by admitting appearances the understanding also admits the existence of things in themselves, and the representation of such beings is not only admissible but unavoidable (AA 4:314.33–315.06).

A link in running text, such as the argument this claim supports, takes the accent and an underline; a link in a list takes neither until hover.

Gleichwohl wird, welches wohl gemerkt werden muß, doch dabei immer vorbehalten, daß wir eben dieselben Gegenstände auch als Dinge an sich selbst, wenn gleich nicht erkennen, doch wenigstens müssen denken können. Denn sonst würde der ungereimte Satz daraus folgen, daß Erscheinung ohne etwas wäre, was da erscheint.

Nevertheless, and this must be well noted, it is always reserved that we must at least be able to think, even if we cannot cognize, these same objects also as things in themselves. For otherwise the absurd proposition would follow that there is appearance without anything that appears.
